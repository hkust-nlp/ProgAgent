You are the underwriting analyst responsible for processing all commercial property insurance applications that are currently pending review. Your job is to evaluate each one by assembling a composite risk profile, determining whether the applicant qualifies for coverage, and producing a fully priced quote for those who do.

Begin by familiarizing yourself with the configuration and policy files available to you. One file lays out the underwriting policy, including the eligibility screening criteria and the overall evaluation workflow. Another provides the detailed risk scoring rules, specifying exactly how each of five risk components is computed, their maximum point contributions, and how the total score maps to tier codes. A third contains the premium schedule with base rates, construction multipliers, a risk-score loading factor, and a stability discount. Finally, a coverage matrix lists the maximum insurable amount for every combination of risk tier and construction type.

Once you understand the rules, retrieve all applications marked as pending review. For every such application, gather the following information. First, pull the full application details to learn the applicant identity, the linked property, its region, and the requested coverage amount. Then obtain the building permit record for that property, which provides the construction type, year built, fire safety rating, and structural grade needed for the construction risk component. Separately, retrieve the historical claims data for the property's region, covering the years 2016 through 2025 inclusive, to compute the claims risk component based on average severities and peril mix. Next, fetch all occupancy sensor readings for the property to determine mean occupancy and whether any sensor was in a degraded state, feeding the occupancy risk component. Also pull the applicant's most recent financial assessment, which supplies the stability score and debt ratio used for both the financial risk component and the eligibility gate. Lastly, consult the property assessment batch files to find the environmental data for that property, specifically the flood zone distance, the nearest fire station distance, and the building condition rating, which together form the environmental risk component.

Before calculating any scores, check the eligibility rule described in the underwriting policy. If the applicant fails the gate, mark that application as declined and move on to the next. For all remaining applications, compute each of the five risk components according to the formulas and brackets specified in the risk scoring rules file, sum them, and clamp the total between zero and one hundred. Map the total risk score to a tier code using the tier mapping table in that same file. Determine the recommended coverage as the lesser of the applicant's requested coverage and the maximum allowed for the assigned tier and construction type from the coverage matrix, expressed in whole US dollars. Then calculate the annual premium following the step-by-step procedure in the premium schedule, rounding the final figure to exactly two decimal places.

Compile your results into a file called `quote_list.csv` with columns in this exact order: `application_id`, `company_name`, `property_address`, `region_code`, `risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `recommended_coverage`, `annual_premium`, `status`. Quoted applications carry the status `QUOTED`. Declined applications carry the status `DECLINED`, and their `recommended_coverage` and `annual_premium` fields must be left empty. Sort all rows by `risk_score` in descending order, breaking ties by `application_id` in ascending lexicographic order. Declined rows, which have no numeric risk score, appear after all quoted rows.

On top of this, produce a file called `risk_summary.json` containing these keys: `total_reviewed` as the integer count of all pending applications you processed, `declined_count` and `quoted_count` as integers, `tier_distribution` as an object mapping each tier code from T1 through T4 to its count among quoted applications, `average_premium` as the arithmetic mean of annual premiums across quoted applications rounded to two decimal places, and `total_recommended_coverage` as the integer sum of recommended coverage across all quoted applications.
