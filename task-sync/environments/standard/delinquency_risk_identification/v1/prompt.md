You have been brought in as a credit-risk analyst to evaluate an entire portfolio of customer accounts and determine which ones are likely to become seriously delinquent within the coming quarter. The organization maintains a database of customer records, invoices, orders, and payment history, all accessible through query tools. Alongside those tools, a collection of configuration and reference files has been prepared for you, describing the scoring methodology, economic backdrop for each industry, and the rules for classifying accounts into risk tiers.

Begin by familiarizing yourself with the configuration files. A YAML policy document lays out the composite scoring model: five weighted component scores that together produce a single risk score per account. The weights, the minimum score that qualifies an account as high risk, and the lookback window are all specified there. Only accounts whose status is listed among the eligible statuses in that policy should be evaluated. A separate JSON file details the formulas for computing each of the five component scores — payment delay, aging concentration, order trend, industry risk, and credit utilization — including floors, caps, and edge-case handling. Another JSON file maps composite-score ranges to named risk levels and spells out the recommended action and contact urgency for each tier. Finally, a CSV of industry-level economic indicators provides sector default rates, GDP growth, revenue trends, average days-sales-outstanding, and a bankruptcy index that feed into the industry risk component.

For every eligible customer, you will need to retrieve their account details to learn their credit limit, contracted payment terms, industry affiliation, and contact information. Then pull their current invoice aging breakdown, which gives outstanding amounts bucketed by days past due. Retrieve their average payment delay over the lookback window defined in the policy and their recent order activity covering the last six months. Also obtain a credit summary showing total invoiced, total paid, and total outstanding amounts. Using the formulas from the scoring-coefficients file, compute each of the five component scores and combine them with the weights from the policy to arrive at a composite risk score for the account.

The payment-delay component should be derived from the average number of days invoices were paid after their due date, scaled relative to the customer's payment terms. The aging-concentration component captures what fraction of the customer's total outstanding balance sits in buckets that are more than sixty days past due. The order-trend component measures whether the customer's ordering activity has declined by comparing the count of orders in the most recent three months against the count in the three months prior. The industry-risk component is calculated from the economic indicators file using the formula in the coefficients document. The credit-utilization component reflects how much of the customer's credit limit is currently consumed by outstanding balances.

Once every eligible account has been scored, filter down to those whose composite score meets or exceeds the high-risk threshold from the policy. Classify each flagged account into its risk tier using the risk-classification file, and record the corresponding recommended action. Sort the resulting list in descending order of risk score; where two accounts share the same score, the one with the larger total outstanding amount takes priority. Assign a sequential priority rank starting at one.

Save the prioritized contact list as `at_risk_accounts.csv` with the following columns in this exact order: `customer_id`, `company_name`, `risk_score`, `risk_level`, `total_outstanding`, `recommended_action`, `priority_rank`, `contact_name`, `contact_email`, `contact_phone`. Risk scores should appear as numbers rounded to two decimal places. Total outstanding amounts should also be rounded to two decimal places.

After writing the file, respond with a brief executive summary that states how many accounts were flagged, how many fall into each of the highest risk tiers, and lists the top accounts by priority with their scores, risk levels, outstanding amounts, and recommended actions. Make sure to reference specific customer IDs in your summary so stakeholders can take immediate action on the most critical cases.
