Your organization maintains a standard cost system for every finished good it manufactures. Each product's standard cost is built from two components: a material component, which sums the standard quantity of every raw material in the product's bill of materials multiplied by that material's standard unit price, and a labor component, which sums the standard hours for each production operation multiplied by the standard labor rate for that operation. These standard figures are stored in the system and can be retrieved through the available query interfaces.

Over recent periods several factors have caused actual costs to diverge from these standards. Raw material spot prices have shifted, and the price your organization actually pays depends on which vendor from the approved vendor list supplies each material as well as any material-specific handling surcharges documented in the material specification files. Labor performance on the shop floor has also varied from the routing standards, and production yield — the ratio of good finished units to planned input — further affects the effective per-unit cost.

You are to perform a full cost variance analysis for every finished good. For each product, determine its standard material cost and standard labor cost from the established standards accessible through the query tools. Then compute the corresponding actual costs using the most current information drawn from the supporting files.

To determine the actual material unit price for a given raw material, first identify the approved vendors for that material using the vendor query, filtering to those whose status is active. Among the active vendors, locate the one flagged as preferred. Then find that preferred vendor's most recently quoted price in the vendor quote files and add the handling surcharge listed for that material in the material specification files. The sum of the quoted price and the handling surcharge is the actual unit price for the material. If the preferred vendor is inactive or no preferred vendor can be found among the active vendors, fall back to the lowest quoted price among all active approved vendors for that material, again adding the handling surcharge. Track for each product whether any of its materials required this fallback to a non-preferred vendor.

The actual labor cost for each product uses the actual hours recorded in the production summary reports rather than the standard hours. Multiply each operation's actual hours by the standard labor rate for that operation to arrive at the actual labor cost.

Once both actual material and actual labor costs have been determined, account for production yield. Compute each product's yield rate as total actual good output divided by total planned output across all recorded production batches found in the yield report files. The yield-adjusted total actual cost is the sum of actual material cost and actual labor cost, divided by the yield rate.

The total standard cost for a product is the sum of its standard material cost and standard labor cost. The total variance is the yield-adjusted total actual cost minus the total standard cost. The total variance percentage is this variance divided by the total standard cost, multiplied by one hundred.

After computing all variances, consult the threshold parameters provided in the cost thresholds configuration file to determine which products should be flagged. A product is flagged when the absolute value of its total variance percentage exceeds the total variance threshold. Separately, determine which products warrant a formal cost revision by consulting the revision policy file. A revision is recommended when any of the following hold: the absolute total variance percentage exceeds the critical total threshold, or the absolute material variance percentage exceeds the material critical threshold and at least one material in the product's bill of materials was sourced from a non-preferred vendor, or the product's yield rate falls below the minimum acceptable yield threshold.

Save your completed analysis as `variance_report.csv` with one row per product, sorted in descending order by the absolute value of total variance percentage. The file must contain exactly these columns in this order: `product_id`, `product_name`, `standard_material_cost`, `actual_material_cost`, `material_variance`, `material_variance_pct`, `standard_labor_cost`, `actual_labor_cost`, `labor_variance`, `labor_variance_pct`, `yield_rate`, `yield_adjusted_total`, `total_standard_cost`, `total_variance`, `total_variance_pct`, `flag`, `revision_recommended`. All monetary values must be rounded to exactly two decimal places. Percentage values must be rounded to two decimal places and represent the variance as a fraction of the corresponding standard cost multiplied by one hundred. The yield rate must be rounded to four decimal places. The `flag` and `revision_recommended` columns must contain either `YES` or `NO`.

In your final response, provide a concise summary listing every product that has been recommended for cost revision, stating for each one its product identifier, product name, total variance percentage, and the specific reason or reasons the revision was triggered.
