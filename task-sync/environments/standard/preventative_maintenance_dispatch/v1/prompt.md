You are the fleet maintenance coordinator for a large vehicle operation that spans multiple depot locations. Your objective is to produce an optimized weekly preventative maintenance dispatch schedule that minimizes the risk of unscheduled breakdowns while respecting technician availability, parts inventory, and vehicle reservation commitments.

Begin by collecting the current telemetry readings for every vehicle in the fleet. These readings, stored across several CSV files, provide the latest odometer mileage and engine hours for each vehicle. You will also need to examine the reservation schedule files in JSON format to understand which vehicles are committed to bookings during the upcoming scheduling window, since a vehicle cannot be pulled into the shop on any day it is reserved. A YAML policy configuration file defines the thresholds and scoring formulas that govern how urgency is calculated, including the overdue ratio formula, the multiplier rules, and the tier classification boundaries. A separate JSON configuration file specifies the exact dates of the scheduling week, the maximum number of dispatches allowed per day, the maximum hours any single technician may work in a day, and the deterministic rules for selecting technicians and ordering the schedule. Finally, a certification guide document explains the technician certification hierarchy and what each level authorizes.

Once you have absorbed the policy and scheduling parameters, query the fleet database to retrieve every vehicle along with its category and depot assignment. For each vehicle category, retrieve the full set of applicable maintenance rules, which include mileage intervals, engine-hour intervals, priority base values, required certification levels, and estimated service durations. Then, for every individual vehicle, pull its complete service history so you can identify the most recent service record for each maintenance type. If a vehicle has no history for a particular maintenance type, the policy file specifies a default overdue ratio to apply.

With telemetry, rules, and history in hand, compute the overdue ratio for each vehicle-and-maintenance-type pair using the formula documented in the policy file. The ratio is the larger of the mileage-based ratio and the engine-hours-based ratio. Any pair whose overdue ratio meets or exceeds the due threshold is a candidate for the dispatch list. For each such candidate, calculate the urgency score by multiplying the overdue ratio by the rule's priority base and by the appropriate multiplier, rounding to two decimal places. The multiplier depends on whether the overdue ratio has reached the critical threshold.

After computing all candidate urgency scores, sort them in descending order of urgency score. When two items share the same score, break the tie by ascending vehicle identifier, and if still tied, by ascending maintenance type. Then, working through this ordered list from highest to lowest urgency, greedily assign each item to the earliest available weekday where three conditions hold simultaneously: the vehicle has no reservation conflict on that day, the day has not yet reached its maximum dispatch count, and there exists at least one technician at the vehicle's depot whose certification level meets or exceeds the rule's requirement, whose daily hours on that day have not been exhausted, and whose total weekly hours can still accommodate the job. Among all technicians satisfying these constraints, choose the one with the lowest hourly rate, breaking ties by ascending technician identifier. If no valid day-and-technician combination exists for an item, omit it from the dispatch list entirely.

For each dispatched item, also determine the parts inventory status for that maintenance type. Query the inventory to see whether all required parts are in stock. If every relevant part has a positive quantity on hand that exceeds the reorder point, the status is "ready". If any part has zero quantity, the status is "backordered". Otherwise, if any part's on-hand quantity is positive but at or below its reorder threshold, the status is "low_stock".

Save the final dispatch schedule to a file named `dispatch_list.csv` with the following columns in this exact order: `priority_rank`, `vehicle_id`, `maintenance_type`, `urgency_score`, `assigned_tech_id`, `scheduled_day`, `estimated_duration_hours`, `parts_status`. The priority rank is a sequential integer starting at one, matching the position in the sorted urgency order. Urgency scores must appear with exactly two decimal places. Estimated duration hours must appear with exactly one decimal place. Scheduled days must be formatted as ISO dates.

Once the file is written, provide a concise summary in your final response. State the total number of dispatches on a line formatted exactly as "Total dispatches: N" where N is the integer count. On the next line state "Total estimated hours: H" where H is the sum of all estimated duration hours rounded to one decimal place. On a third line state the tier breakdown as "Critical: C, High: H, Routine: R" where C, H, and R are the counts of dispatched items falling into each urgency tier as defined by the tier thresholds in the policy file.
