You are conducting a royalty audit for a music distribution company that manages payments to recording artists across multiple international territories. Recent internal reviews have raised concerns that certain payments were processed using revenue share rates and withholding tax deductions from the wrong territory. A sale that took place in one country may have been paid out as though it occurred in another, resulting in incorrect royalty amounts. Your objective is to identify every overpayment arising from such territory misapplication and compile a formal recovery claim list.

The distributor sales reports are stored in the reports directory as CSV files. Each report contains sale references, artist identifiers, the territory where the sale actually occurred, gross revenue figures, and additional metadata. These reports constitute the authoritative record of what was sold and where. Separately, you can query the system for payment records, which indicate not only the territory of the sale but also the territory whose rates were actually used when the payment was computed. Any discrepancy between these two territory fields signals a potential miscalculation.

To determine whether an overpayment occurred, you must compute the correct net royalty for each flagged payment. The net royalty is calculated by multiplying the gross revenue of the sale by the artist's contractual revenue share percentage for the territory where the sale took place, then reducing the result by the withholding tax rate applicable to that same territory. The contractual revenue share percentages are available through the artist contracts query interface, and the withholding rates are documented in the tax treaty schedule file located alongside the reports. Once you have the correct net royalty, compare it against the amount that was actually paid. If the paid amount exceeds the correct amount by more than the minimum recovery threshold specified in the policy configuration file, the difference constitutes a recoverable overpayment.

Each overpayment must be assigned a recovery priority. The priority tiers and their corresponding minimum overpayment thresholds are defined in the policy configuration. Apply the highest qualifying tier: if an overpayment meets the threshold for the high tier, classify it as high regardless of whether it also meets the medium or low thresholds.

Once all overpayments have been identified, computed, and classified, save the results as `recovery_claims.csv`. This file must contain the following columns in exactly this order: `claim_id`, `payment_id`, `artist_name`, `territory_code`, `applied_territory_code`, `gross_revenue`, `correct_amount`, `paid_amount`, `overpayment_amount`, `recovery_priority`. The rows must be sorted by `overpayment_amount` in descending order, with ties broken by `payment_id` in ascending order. The claim identifiers follow the pattern specified in the policy configuration, assigned sequentially starting from one after sorting. All monetary values must be formatted to exactly two decimal places.

In your final response, state the total number of overpayment claims identified and the total recovery amount formatted to two decimal places. Also provide a brief breakdown showing, for each territory that has at least one overpayment, the number of claims and the aggregate overpayment for that territory.
