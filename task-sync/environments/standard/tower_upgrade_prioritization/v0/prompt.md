You are a network planning engineer responsible for identifying cell towers in the network that require capacity upgrades. Your goal is to analyze current traffic patterns, historical peaks, inter-tower handoff reliability, and the impact of planned nearby construction, then produce a prioritized list of towers that should receive new radio units.

Begin by retrieving the full inventory of towers across all regions. For every tower, gather its per-sector utilization statistics covering the most recent thirty-day window. The tower-wide average utilization is the mean of each sector's average utilization percentage, and the tower-wide peak utilization is the highest peak utilization percentage observed across all of its sectors. Separately, obtain the overall handoff failure rate for each tower, which reflects how reliably connections are transferred to and from adjacent sites. Then, for each tower, search for construction permits in the surrounding area using the proximity radius specified in the policy configuration; sum the estimated new residents across all permits found within that radius to gauge future demand pressure.

Four factors drive the upgrade decision. The first is whether the tower-wide average utilization exceeds the threshold defined in the upgrade policy configuration file. The second is whether the tower-wide peak utilization exceeds its own threshold from the same policy. The third is whether the overall handoff failure rate exceeds the failure-rate threshold in the policy. The fourth is whether the total estimated new residents from nearby permits exceeds the minimum resident count stated in the policy's permit-impact section. A tower is considered for upgrade only when at least the minimum number of these factors (as specified in the policy) are simultaneously triggered.

Physical feasibility is equally important. Site assessment reports spread across several data files in your working directory describe each tower's structural grade, available power headroom, and remaining radio-unit mounting slots. These files appear in varying formats and each batch covers a different subset of towers. Only towers whose structural grade appears in the list of acceptable grades defined in the upgrade policy may proceed to the final upgrade list.

Once you have identified all eligible towers, compute a priority score for each one. A scoring methodology file in your working directory explains how to compute individual factor scores on a zero-to-one-hundred scale and the weight each factor carries. The average utilization score equals the tower-wide average utilization percentage capped at one hundred. The peak utilization score equals the tower-wide peak utilization percentage capped at one hundred. The handoff failure score is the failure rate percentage divided by fifteen and multiplied by one hundred, then capped at one hundred. The permit impact score is the total nearby new residents divided by five thousand and multiplied by one hundred, then capped at one hundred. Multiply each factor score by its designated weight, sum the results, and then multiply by the tower-class multiplier found in the policy. Round the final priority score to two decimal places.

For each eligible tower, recommend a specific radio-unit model from the catalog file available in your working directory. The recommended unit must support every distinct frequency band present across the tower's sectors. Additionally, a band-compatibility configuration file specifies which radio-unit form factors are permitted for each tower class; the recommended unit's form factor must appear in the allowed list for that tower's class. Among all qualifying units, choose the one with the lowest cost. If two units share the same cost, choose the one whose model name comes first alphabetically.

Save the final results as `upgrade_priorities.csv` with the following columns in this exact order: `tower_id`, `tower_name`, `priority_rank`, `priority_score`, `avg_utilization_pct`, `peak_utilization_pct`, `handoff_failure_rate_pct`, `permit_impact_score`, `recommended_ru_model`, `estimated_cost_usd`. Rows must be sorted by priority score in descending order, with ties broken by ascending tower ID. The priority rank column should reflect this ordering starting from one. All percentage and score values should be rounded to two decimal places.

In your final response, provide a brief summary that explicitly mentions the tower ID and tower name of the top three highest-priority towers along with their priority scores and recommended radio-unit models. State the total number of towers that qualified for the upgrade list.
