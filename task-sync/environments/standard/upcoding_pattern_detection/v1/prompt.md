You have been brought in as a healthcare fraud analyst to investigate potential upcoding across a network of medical providers. Upcoding occurs when a provider systematically bills for more expensive or complex procedures than what was actually performed, and your job is to analyze all available evidence and produce a prioritized audit list ranking providers by their overall risk.

Your investigation must draw on several complementary sources. A set of configuration files describes the analytical framework you should follow. One file lays out the scoring weights assigned to each risk factor, the thresholds that determine whether a provider is flagged and at what priority level, and the expected age ranges considered typical for each procedure complexity tier. Another file maps each medical specialty to the procedure categories that specialty is authorized to bill. Regional benchmark files specify, for every combination of region, specialty, and procedure category, the expected percentage of claims that should fall into that category. Provider supplement files list each provider alongside their assigned peer group, years in practice, and certification level. Historical adjustment files record past instances where a provider's claim was downcoded or modified, including the original and adjusted procedure codes and the cost difference. You will need to read and consolidate data from all of these sources as they may be spread across multiple files of the same type.

In parallel, you should retrieve provider information using the available lookup capabilities. You can page through the full provider roster to obtain each provider's identifier, name, specialty, and region. For each provider you can retrieve their claims, which include the patient's age, the procedure code billed, the billed amount, claim date, approval status, and any denial reason. You can also look up detailed information about any procedure code, specifically its category, base cost, and complexity level, and you can pull denial statistics summarizing each provider's total and denied claim counts along with the denial rate.

Once you have gathered all data, compute five risk components for every provider. The first is the frequency deviation, which measures how much a provider's distribution of claims across authorized procedure categories diverges from the regional benchmark. To calculate it, consider only claims falling in categories the provider's specialty is authorized to bill, compute the percentage each authorized category represents out of those authorized claims, compare each percentage against the benchmark for that provider's region and specialty, sum up all positive differences where actual exceeds expected, and divide by one hundred. The second component is the out-of-scope rate, the fraction of the provider's total claims that fall in procedure categories their specialty is not authorized to bill. The third is the denial rate deviation, which equals the provider's denial rate minus the average denial rate of all providers sharing the same peer group, floored at zero. The peer group for each provider is found in the supplement files, and you must compute the peer group average across all providers assigned to that same group. The fourth is the adjustment rate, the count of historical adjustments recorded for the provider divided by their total claim count. The fifth is the demographic anomaly rate, the share of claims where the patient's age falls outside the typical range for the billed procedure's complexity level, using the age boundaries specified in the configuration.

Multiply each component by its respective weight from the configuration and sum the products to obtain the composite risk score. Round the composite and each component to the number of decimal places specified in the configuration. A provider is flagged for audit if their composite risk score meets or exceeds the flag threshold. Among flagged providers, those at or above the high-priority threshold are assigned priority "High", those at or above the medium-priority threshold receive "Medium", and all others receive "Low".

Save the results as `audit_list.csv` containing exactly these columns in order: provider_id, provider_name, specialty, composite_risk_score, frequency_deviation, out_of_scope_rate, denial_rate_deviation, adjustment_rate, demographic_anomaly_rate, priority. Include only flagged providers and sort rows in descending order by composite risk score, breaking ties by provider_id in ascending order.

In your final response, state how many providers were flagged for audit and list the top five by composite risk score, giving for each the provider identifier, name, specialty, composite risk score, and priority level.
