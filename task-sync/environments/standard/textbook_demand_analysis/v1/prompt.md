You are assisting a university library procurement team that needs to identify which textbooks are at risk of shortage for upcoming semesters and determine how many additional copies should be purchased. The analysis requires combining information from several sources: enrollment data files that list how many students are enrolled and waitlisted in each course section, a set of tools that provide access to course-textbook requirement mappings, current library inventory counts across branches, historical circulation statistics, and pending student reservation queues. A policy configuration file and a budget allocation file in your working directory govern the risk scoring methodology and departmental spending limits, while a supplier discount schedule determines the effective unit cost based on order volume.

Begin by reading all enrollment batch files to build a complete picture of student demand per course. Each file contains the course identifier, enrollment count, waitlisted student count, department, and other details. Only textbooks designated as "required" for a course contribute to shortage analysis — those marked as merely "recommended" should be excluded entirely. For every required textbook, compute its total demand as the sum of enrolled students plus waitlisted students across all course sections that list it as required.

Once you have mapped each required textbook to its total demand, retrieve the current inventory status for each one. The inventory data will show how many total copies exist across library branches and how many are currently available. Separately, obtain the circulation statistics for each textbook, which reveal how many copies are currently checked out and other usage patterns. Also retrieve the pending reservation queue for each textbook to learn how many students are actively waiting.

The procurement policy file specifies a weighted risk scoring formula with four components. The demand-supply ratio component equals the total demand divided by the number of total copies (use one if there are zero copies), normalized by dividing by ten and capping at one. The reservation pressure component equals the number of pending reservations divided by total copies, normalized by dividing by five and capped at one. The circulation intensity component equals the count of currently checked-out copies divided by total copies, capped at one. The waitlist factor component equals the total waitlisted students (across courses requiring that textbook) divided by the total enrolled students for those same courses, capped at one. Each component is multiplied by its respective weight from the policy file, and the four weighted values are summed to produce the final risk score, rounded to four decimal places.

The policy file also defines threshold boundaries for classifying risk levels. A textbook whose risk score meets or exceeds the critical threshold is labeled "critical," one meeting or exceeding the high threshold but below critical is "high," one meeting or exceeding the medium threshold but below high is "medium," and anything below the medium threshold is "low." Each risk level maps to a priority tier score as defined in the policy file.

To determine the recommended purchase quantity for each textbook, apply the formula described in the policy file: take the ceiling of total demand multiplied by the target ratio, then subtract the total inventory copies. If this result is positive but below the minimum order quantity, round it up to the minimum order. If it is zero or negative, no purchase is needed and the quantity should be zero.

For cost estimation, look up each textbook's retail price using the available tools and then apply the supplier discount schedule from the corresponding file. The discount percentage depends on which quantity bracket the recommended purchase quantity falls into. The unit cost is the retail price reduced by the applicable discount percentage. The total cost for each textbook is the unit cost multiplied by the recommended quantity, rounded to two decimal places.

When a textbook is required by courses spanning multiple departments, assign it to the department that comes first alphabetically among those departments.

After completing all calculations, save the results as `procurement_priority_list.csv` with the following columns in this exact order: `textbook_id`, `title`, `isbn`, `department`, `total_demand`, `total_copies`, `available_copies`, `pending_reservations`, `risk_score`, `risk_level`, `priority_tier`, `recommended_quantity`, `unit_cost`, `total_cost`. Sort the rows by priority tier in descending order, breaking ties by risk score in descending order, and further ties by textbook ID in ascending order.
