You are responsible for developing a pricing schedule for an upcoming flash sale campaign. The campaign targets a defined set of product categories, and your objective is to determine the optimal discount percentage for every eligible product using a multi-dimensional scoring approach that synthesizes clickstream engagement, competitive pricing intelligence, warehouse inventory pressure, and customer segment lifetime value.

Start by examining the configuration and policy documents available in your current directory. Among them you will find a campaign configuration that specifies the eligible product categories, the minimum margin percentage that must be preserved on every sale, a maximum discount cap, and the weights assigned to each scoring dimension. A separate file defines the discount tier table, listing tier names, minimum composite score thresholds, and the base discount percentage associated with each tier. You will also find a competitor reputation file that assigns a numeric weight to each competitor name, a warehouse configuration that provides a priority weight for each warehouse, and a policy document that describes normalization edge-case rules and the margin-enforcement formula. Read all of these thoroughly before proceeding, as every computation depends on values specified in those files.

Using the tools at your disposal, retrieve the full product catalog page by page. A product is eligible for the flash sale only if its category appears in the campaign's eligible category list. For each eligible product, obtain its base price and cost price through the product detail lookup, as these values are required for margin and pricing calculations.

Gather clickstream engagement data from the daily session log CSV files present in your directory. Each file records browsing sessions with columns for the product identifier, the customer segment identifier, and the event type, which may be page_view, click, add_to_cart, or purchase. Aggregate events across all available day files for each product, weighting each event type as follows: a page_view counts as one, a click as two, an add_to_cart as three, and a purchase as five. The sum of weighted events is the raw engagement value for each product.

For competitive positioning, use the tools to retrieve competitor prices for each eligible product. Consider only entries where the competitor has the product in stock. Weight each such price by the competitor's reputation weight from the reputation file, then compute the weighted average competitor price as the sum of each price times its weight divided by the sum of the weights. The raw price competitiveness value equals the product's own base price minus this weighted average, divided by the base price, multiplied by one hundred. A positive value indicates the product's own price exceeds the weighted competitor average, suggesting more competitive pressure to discount. If no competitor carries the product in stock, assign a raw competitiveness value of zero.

Assess inventory pressure by querying each eligible product's stock across warehouses through the tools. For every warehouse entry, compute the days of supply by dividing current stock by daily sales, where daily sales equals monthly units sold divided by thirty. Then compute the weighted average days of supply across all warehouses holding the product, using each warehouse's priority weight from the warehouse configuration. This weighted average is the raw inventory urgency value; products burdened with proportionally more stock relative to their sales velocity will naturally produce higher numbers, indicating stronger pressure to discount.

Determine segment value by cross-referencing the clickstream event data with customer segment information retrieved through the tools. For every clickstream event tied to a product, identify the customer segment and look up its average lifetime value score. The raw segment value for a product is the arithmetic mean of the lifetime value scores across all of that product's clickstream events. If a product has no clickstream events at all, its raw engagement and raw segment value are both zero.

After computing raw values in all four dimensions for every eligible product, normalize each dimension independently to a zero-through-one-hundred scale using min-max scaling across the eligible product set. Within each dimension the product with the lowest raw value receives zero and the one with the highest receives one hundred. Consult the policy document in your directory for the rule governing the edge case where all products share an identical raw value in a given dimension. Compute the composite score as the weighted sum of the four normalized sub-scores, applying the dimension weights from the campaign configuration.

Map each product's composite score to a discount tier by evaluating the tier definitions from the tier table top to bottom, assigning the first tier whose minimum score threshold the product's composite score meets or exceeds. The product's initial discount percentage is that tier's base discount percentage.

Before finalizing any discount, enforce the margin floor using the formula described in the policy document. Compute the sale price as the base price multiplied by one minus the discount percentage divided by one hundred, rounded to two decimal places. If this sale price falls below the margin floor, reduce the discount to the largest whole-number percentage that keeps the sale price at or above that floor. If even a one-percent discount would violate the margin floor, exclude the product from the schedule entirely. The maximum discount percentage from the campaign configuration also acts as an absolute cap that no product may exceed.

Save the completed pricing schedule as `pricing_schedule.csv`. The file must contain the following columns in exactly this order: `product_id`, `product_name`, `category`, `base_price`, `cost_price`, `composite_score`, `discount_tier`, `discount_pct`, `sale_price`. Round composite_score to two decimal places. Express base_price, cost_price, and sale_price to two decimal places. Express discount_pct as a whole number with no decimal point. Sort the rows by composite_score in descending order, breaking ties by product_id in ascending lexicographic order.

In your final response, state the total number of products included in the pricing schedule, the count of products assigned to each discount tier, and the overall average discount percentage across all included products rounded to two decimal places.
