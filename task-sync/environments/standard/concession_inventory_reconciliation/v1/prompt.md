You have been brought in to audit inventory records across a network of concession stands that operated during a two-week event period. Your objective is to reconcile the full stock flow for every product at every stand, identify where physical counts diverge from what records predict should be on hand, and flag stands where discrepancies suggest shrinkage, theft, or systematic recording failures.

Begin by reviewing the policy configuration file in your working directory, which specifies the analysis period dates, the thresholds for flagging a stand, and the priority tier definitions used when classifying flagged stands. A stand directory file provides the identifiers, names, and sections of all stands, while a product catalog file lists every product with its category, unit cost, and unit price. Two inventory snapshot files record the physical counts of each product at each stand at the beginning and end of the analysis period.

Delivery records from vendors are organized as batch files inside a delivery invoices folder. Each batch is a tabular file listing invoice numbers, stand and product identifiers, quantities delivered, delivery dates, vendor names, and cost information. All batches must be read and aggregated. Separately, waste and spoilage information is maintained in a waste logs folder containing individual sheets that track discarded stock by stand, product, quantity, date, and reason. These sheets come in several formats, including comma-separated, pipe-delimited, and markdown table layouts, and every one of them must be parsed and consolidated.

Point-of-sale transaction data is available through a system query interface that returns paginated records for each stand, including the product sold, quantity, sale price, transaction timestamp, shift type, and payment method. Customer return records can likewise be retrieved per stand, providing the product, quantity returned, return date, reason, and refund amount. Inter-stand product transfer logs record the originating and destination stands, the product, quantity, transfer date, and who authorized the move; these can be queried by stand and direction, either inbound or outbound. Staff scheduling records are also queryable per stand and return employee identifiers, names, shift dates, shift types, and clock-in and clock-out times.

For every combination of stand and product, compute the total supply as the sum of the beginning inventory count, all delivered quantities, all inbound transfer quantities, and all returned quantities. Compute total accounted consumption as the sum of all sold quantities, all wasted quantities, and all outbound transfer quantities. The expected ending inventory equals total supply minus total accounted consumption. The variance is then the expected ending inventory minus the actual physical ending count. A positive variance means units are unaccounted for. Calculate the shrinkage value for each row by multiplying the variance, when positive, by the product unit cost from the catalog. When the variance is zero or negative, the shrinkage value is zero.

Save the full reconciliation as `variance_report.csv` with columns in exactly this order: `stand_id`, `stand_name`, `product_id`, `product_name`, `beginning_qty`, `delivered_qty`, `transferred_in_qty`, `returned_qty`, `sold_qty`, `wasted_qty`, `transferred_out_qty`, `expected_ending_qty`, `actual_ending_qty`, `variance_qty`, `variance_pct`, `shrinkage_value_usd`. The variance percentage equals the variance divided by total supply, multiplied by one hundred. When total supply is zero, set the percentage to zero. Round all percentages and monetary values to exactly two decimal places. Sort rows first by stand identifier ascending, then by product identifier ascending.

A stand should be flagged for investigation when two conditions are both met: its aggregate shrinkage value across all products exceeds the minimum value threshold stated in the policy, and its aggregate shrinkage percentage exceeds the percentage threshold in the policy. The aggregate shrinkage percentage for a stand equals the sum of all positive product-level variances across that stand, divided by the sum of total supply across all its products, multiplied by one hundred. For each flagged stand, assign an investigation priority tier using the priority bands defined in the policy, based on the aggregate shrinkage percentage. Also compile all distinct names of staff members who were assigned to that stand at any point during the analysis period, sort them alphabetically, and join them with a semicolon followed by a space as the delimiter.

Save the flagged stands as `flagged_stands.csv` with columns: `stand_id`, `stand_name`, `total_shrinkage_value`, `shrinkage_pct`, `investigation_priority`, `staff_on_duty`. Sort by total shrinkage value descending. Round monetary values and percentages to two decimal places.

Once both files are written, state in your final response the total number of stands analyzed, the number of stands flagged for investigation, the total shrinkage value in USD across all flagged stands rounded to two decimal places, and the name of the stand with the highest shrinkage value together with that value.
