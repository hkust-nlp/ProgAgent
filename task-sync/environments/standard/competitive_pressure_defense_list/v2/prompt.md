Your organization is facing intensifying competition across its customer base, and the sales leadership needs a comprehensive, data-driven view of which accounts are most at risk. You have access to several configuration and intelligence files that define how competitive pressure should be measured, as well as a set of tools that expose live data on search rankings, product pricing, social media activity, and the internal deal pipeline. Your job is to bring all of these together into a single prioritized defense list.

Start by reading the configuration files that govern the analysis. A YAML file describes the weight each signal dimension carries in the composite score and specifies two key windows: the total analysis period in days and the number of most-recent days that constitute the "recent" window. A JSON file defines the threshold boundaries for classifying an account's pressure level as HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW, where any score at or above the HIGH threshold is HIGH, any score at or above the MEDIUM threshold but below HIGH is MEDIUM, and everything else is LOW. A CSV file maps each deal-pipeline stage to a numeric risk factor between zero and one. A Markdown playbook file lists the recommended defense action for each pressure level.

Separately, a collection of field intelligence report files provides qualitative observations from analysts about competitive activity at specific accounts. Each report contains entries keyed by account, and every entry includes a numeric intel modifier that should be applied multiplicatively to the account's composite score before classification. If an account does not appear in any intelligence report, treat its modifier as one.

Use the available tools to retrieve all customer accounts (they are returned in pages), then for every account retrieve the deals currently in the pipeline. Each deal links the account to a specific competitor and records the deal stage. The pipeline risk score for an account is the maximum risk factor among all of its deals, looked up from the stage-to-risk mapping in the CSV. If an account has no deals at all, its pipeline risk is zero and it has no competitors to evaluate, so all its signal scores are zero.

For every unique competitor encountered through the deals, gather three signal dimensions. First, request the competitor's search-ranking trend using the recent-window size from the configuration. For each keyword the competitor is tracked on, compute the rank improvement ratio as the difference between the early-period average rank and the recent-period average rank, divided by the early-period average rank; a positive value means the competitor is climbing. Take the maximum improvement ratio across all keywords for that competitor, clamping negatives to zero and capping at one. Second, request the competitor's pricing data. For each product, compute the undercut ratio as the difference between your average price and the competitor's average price, divided by your average price; a positive value means the competitor is cheaper. Take the maximum undercut ratio across products, clamping negatives to zero and capping at one. Third, request the competitor's social-media metrics using the same recent-window size. For each platform, compute the surge ratio as the difference between recent average daily posts and early average daily posts, divided by the greater of the early average and one; a positive value means posting volume is growing. Take the maximum surge ratio across platforms, clamping negatives to zero and capping at one.

Once you have all competitor-level scores, roll them up to the account level. For each account, find the maximum search score, maximum pricing score, and maximum social score across all competitors linked through its deals. Identify the primary competitor as the one whose sum of search, pricing, and social scores is highest. Compute the composite score as the weighted sum of the four dimensions using the weights from the configuration file, where the search-ranking weight multiplies the max search score, the pricing-undercut weight multiplies the max pricing score, the social-media-surge weight multiplies the max social score, and the pipeline-risk weight multiplies the pipeline risk score. Multiply the composite by the intel modifier for that account to obtain the final adjusted pressure score, rounded to four decimal places.

Classify every account using the thresholds, then sort all accounts in descending order of pressure score, breaking ties by ascending account ID. Assign a sequential rank starting at one. Save the result as `defense_list.csv` with exactly these columns in this order: `rank`, `account_id`, `account_name`, `tier`, `pressure_score`, `pressure_level`, `primary_competitor`, `recommended_action`. The recommended action for each row must match the text from the playbook corresponding to its pressure level.

In your final response, state the total number of accounts classified as HIGH, the total classified as MEDIUM, and the total classified as LOW. Then list the top five accounts by rank, including each account's name, ID, pressure score, pressure level, and primary competitor.
