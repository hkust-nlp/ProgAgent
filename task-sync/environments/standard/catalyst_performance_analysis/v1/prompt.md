You are responsible for evaluating the operational health of every fluid catalytic cracking unit across a multi-refinery petrochemical operation. The organization maintains a comprehensive set of records spanning catalyst performance, feedstock characteristics, unit throughput, and energy consumption. Your objective is to synthesize these disparate data sources into a single, actionable maintenance priority list that ranks every unit from most urgent to least urgent for catalyst intervention, whether that means regeneration, replacement, or continued monitoring.

Begin by identifying all active cracking units in the system. For each unit, retrieve its current catalyst batch information, including the catalyst type, the date the batch was loaded, and the expected catalyst lifetime. You will also need the full history of activity index measurements recorded for the active catalyst batch, the throughput records showing conversion percentages over time, and the energy consumption records covering fuel gas, steam, and electricity usage. All of this operational data is available through the provided query tools.

Separately, feedstock quality data is stored in a collection of CSV report files that reside alongside your other reference materials. Each report file contains dated entries with the unit identifier, API gravity, sulfur percentage, nitrogen in parts per million, Conradson carbon residue percentage, nickel concentration in ppm, and vanadium concentration in ppm. You must read all of these report files and aggregate the feedstock quality information per unit.

There are three reference documents you must also consult. One is a YAML configuration file that defines the scoring weights assigned to each performance factor, the minimum and maximum values used for normalization, energy unit conversion factors, feedstock severity calculation weights, and the score thresholds that delineate priority tiers. Another is a JSON file containing baseline specifications for each catalyst type, including baseline activity, expected lifetime in days, and baseline conversion percentage. The third is a text file that documents the maintenance decision guidelines, explaining exactly how the priority tier and catalyst age factor together determine the recommended maintenance action and the associated estimated downtime in days.

Once you have collected all necessary data, compute five performance factors for every unit. The activity decline rate is the monthly percentage decline in the activity index, calculated as the difference between the earliest and latest activity measurements for the active batch divided by the earliest measurement, multiplied by one hundred, then divided by the number of months (days between measurements divided by thirty) between those two readings. The energy efficiency ratio compares the unit's recent energy intensity to its earlier energy intensity: for each energy record, compute the total energy in MMBTU by summing fuel gas MMBTU, steam (in thousands of pounds) multiplied by the steam-to-MMBTU conversion factor from the configuration, and electricity (in MWh) multiplied by the electricity-to-MMBTU factor, then divide by the feed rate in barrels per day from the throughput record whose date is closest to that energy record's date. Split these per-barrel energy values chronologically into a first half and a second half, and the ratio is the mean of the second half divided by the mean of the first half. The conversion loss is the baseline conversion percentage for the unit's catalyst type minus the average conversion percentage from the unit's two most recent throughput records. The feedstock severity score is the mean severity across all feedstock records for the unit, where each record's severity equals sulfur percentage times the sulfur weight, plus CCR percentage times the CCR weight, plus nitrogen divided by one thousand times the nitrogen weight, plus nickel divided by ten times the nickel weight, plus vanadium divided by ten times the vanadium weight, with all weights taken from the configuration. The catalyst age factor is the number of days since the catalyst was loaded divided by the expected lifetime in days from the catalyst reference, where "today" is treated as the fixed reference date of December 31, 2025 to ensure deterministic results.

Normalize each factor to a zero-to-one-hundred scale by applying the formula: clip the quantity (value minus the configured minimum) divided by (configured maximum minus configured minimum) to the range zero through one, then multiply by one hundred. Multiply each normalized factor by its configured weight and sum the products to obtain the priority score for that unit.

Assign every unit to a priority tier based on the score thresholds in the configuration, then determine the recommended action and estimated downtime by following the decision rules in the maintenance guidelines document, which take into account both the tier and the catalyst age factor.

Rank all units in descending order of priority score. If two units share the same score, the one whose unit identifier comes first alphabetically should rank higher. Assign integer ranks starting from one.

Save the complete ranked list as `maintenance_priority.csv` with the following columns in this exact order: `unit_id`, `unit_name`, `priority_rank`, `priority_score`, `activity_decline_rate`, `energy_efficiency_ratio`, `feedstock_severity_score`, `conversion_loss_pct`, `catalyst_age_days`, `recommended_action`, `estimated_downtime_days`. All numeric values should be rounded to two decimal places.

In your final response, state how many units were analyzed, then list every unit that falls into the Critical or High priority tier, giving for each its unit identifier, unit name, tier designation, priority score, recommended action, and estimated downtime in days. Conclude by noting that the full priority list has been saved to the output file.
