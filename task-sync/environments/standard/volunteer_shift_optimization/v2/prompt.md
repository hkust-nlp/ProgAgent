You are coordinating volunteer coverage for a community service organization that runs multiple events throughout the upcoming week. Each event requires volunteers assigned to specific roles during designated time slots, and your goal is to produce a finalized weekly schedule that assigns exactly one primary volunteer to every shift while also designating backup volunteers who can step in if the primary is unavailable.

The working directory contains several reference documents you will need. A YAML file describes the scheduling policy, including the maximum acceptable historical no-show rate for a volunteer to qualify as a primary assignee, the rain probability threshold above which outdoor shifts require additional backup coverage, and the number of backup volunteers expected under normal conditions versus weather-affected outdoor conditions. A CSV file provides a matrix mapping each volunteer role to its baseline certification requirements and the minimum number of years of experience the role demands. A JSON file supplies a seven-day weather forecast, keyed by day of the week, with each entry containing the predicted rain probability among other conditions. A Markdown document lays out the detailed priority rules for selecting backup volunteers, which you should read carefully and follow precisely. Separately, a collection of event briefing documents in JSON format describes each event; some of these briefings specify an additional certification that every volunteer assigned to that event must hold, regardless of their particular role.

To build the schedule, you will need to query for the full roster of volunteers, including their certifications, maximum shifts per week, and years of experience. You will also need to retrieve shifts organized by day, each record showing the event it belongs to, the required role, the start and end times, the location, and whether the shift takes place outdoors. For each volunteer you are considering, you can look up their weekly availability windows and their attendance summary, which reports the historical number of attended and missed shifts along with the computed no-show rate.

The assignment procedure works as follows. Process shifts in ascending order of their shift identifier. For each shift, determine eligibility by checking that the volunteer holds every certification the role demands according to the certification matrix, plus any additional certification noted in the corresponding event briefing. The volunteer must also meet the role's minimum experience threshold and be available for the full duration of the shift on the relevant day. A volunteer who is already assigned as primary to another shift that overlaps in time on the same day is ineligible. Furthermore, a volunteer whose total primary assignments for the week would exceed their personal maximum is ineligible for further primary selection. Among the remaining eligible candidates, only those whose historical no-show rate does not exceed the threshold set in the scheduling policy may serve as primary. Rank these candidates by reliability score, computed as one minus the no-show rate, in descending order, and break ties by selecting the volunteer with the lower identifier. Assign the top candidate as primary.

Once the primary is chosen, select backup volunteers from the full set of eligible candidates for that shift, excluding the primary and excluding anyone with a time conflict from a primary assignment on the same day. Backup candidates need not meet the no-show threshold; they are ranked by the same reliability-then-identifier ordering used for primaries. Under normal conditions, assign the number of backups specified by the normal backup count in the scheduling policy. For outdoor shifts falling on days when the forecasted rain probability exceeds the rain threshold, assign the elevated outdoor backup count instead. Backup designations do not count toward a volunteer's weekly shift cap and do not create conflicts with other backup designations.

After completing the assignment process, save the primary schedule to a file called `weekly_schedule.csv` containing one row per shift with the following columns in this exact order: `shift_id`, `day`, `start_time`, `end_time`, `event_name`, `role`, `assigned_volunteer_id`, `assigned_volunteer_name`. Save the backup roster to a file called `backup_assignments.csv` with one row per backup designation and these columns: `shift_id`, `backup_rank`, `volunteer_id`, `volunteer_name`, `reliability_score`. The reliability score in the backup file must be formatted to exactly two decimal places.

In your final response, report three summary statistics: the total number of shifts that received a primary assignment, the coverage percentage expressed as the number of assigned shifts divided by total shifts multiplied by one hundred and rounded to one decimal place, and the average reliability score of all primary assignees rounded to two decimal places.
