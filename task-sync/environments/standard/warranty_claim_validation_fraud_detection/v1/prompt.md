You are a fraud analyst responsible for reviewing all warranty claims that are currently in pending review status. Your goal is to systematically evaluate every such claim against a set of fraud detection rules and produce a comprehensive report identifying suspicious claims for further investigation.

Start by locating and reading the fraud detection rules configuration file and the warranty policy file in your working directory. These define the exact thresholds, risk point values, and output formatting requirements you must follow. The rules configuration specifies six distinct fraud indicators: a photo timestamp anomaly check that flags claims where the customer-submitted failure photo was taken too far before or after the claim submission date, a warranty expiration check that compares the elapsed time since purchase against the model-specific warranty duration, a quality control contradiction check that identifies cases where every final test station record shows a passing result yet the customer alleges a manufacturing defect, a production date anomaly that catches serial numbers whose factory production date falls after the recorded purchase date, a repeat service flag for serial numbers with an excessive number of prior repair center visits, and an excessive repair cost flag triggered when the cumulative repair spending on a serial number exceeds a configured percentage of the product's manufacturer suggested retail price. Each rule carries a defined number of risk points, and the sum for a given claim is capped at the maximum specified in the configuration.

To evaluate each pending claim you will need to retrieve its full details including the customer identifier, serial number, submission date, type of defect alleged, and purchase date. You will then need the product record for that serial number to obtain the model name, production date, and retail price. Quality control test results for the serial number are required to determine whether every test passed. The service history endpoint provides the count and identifiers of prior repair visits, but detailed cost information is maintained separately by each repair center. You will find repair center log files organized by center in your working directory, each containing service identifiers, serial numbers, and the cost of each repair. Cross-reference those logs to compute the total historical repair cost for any serial number you are evaluating. Photo analysis batch files in your working directory contain the photo taken date for each claim, which you must use for the timestamp anomaly check.

Once you have applied every rule to every pending claim, compile the results. Only claims that trigger at least one fraud indicator should appear in the output. Save the output as `flagged_claims_report.csv` with columns `claim_id`, `serial_number`, `customer_id`, `flags`, `flag_count`, and `risk_score`. The flags column must contain the applicable rule codes separated by pipe characters and sorted in alphabetical order within each cell. Sort the entire report by risk score in descending order, breaking ties by claim identifier in ascending order. The risk score for each row is the sum of the risk points of its triggered rules, subject to the cap defined in the configuration.

In your final response, state the total number of claims you flagged for investigation.
