An emergency evacuation has been ordered for every school in the district. Your job is to produce a complete bus dispatch plan that gets every enrolled student from their school to a safe shelter, respecting vehicle weight limits on bridges, shelter occupancy caps, and bus seating capacities.

Begin by gathering the full list of schools and, for each school, retrieving its student roster. You will also need the complete inventory of buses along with their seating capacities, empty weights, and whether each bus is wheelchair-accessible. Shelter information is likewise available through queries, giving you each shelter's name, location, base capacity, and accessibility features. For any school-shelter pair you are considering, you can look up the pre-computed route, which tells you the ordered list of road-segment identifiers that compose the path, the total distance in kilometres, and an estimated travel time in minutes. For any individual road segment you can retrieve its distance, whether it carries a bridge, and the bridge's posted weight limit in tonnes.

Not all of that data lives in the query system, however. Several files in the working directory fill in the gaps that the tools cannot provide. A YAML policy document sets the ground rules for the evacuation: the minimum fuel-level percentage a bus must have to be dispatched, the weight in tonnes that each additional passenger adds to the bus, the maximum acceptable one-way travel time, whether students with special needs must ride wheelchair-accessible buses, the strategy for choosing shelters (nearest feasible first), the order in which students should be loaded onto each bus (special-needs students first, then by grade ascending), and the maximum load factor applied to a bus's rated capacity. A JSON file tracks current shelter status; some shelters are closed or operating at reduced capacity, and the entries in that file override whatever base capacity the tools report. Another JSON file contains updated bridge weight limits for certain road segments where recent inspections have lowered the original posted limit; if a segment appears there, the updated tonnage limit takes precedence. A collection of CSV files records each bus's current fuel level, driver name, driver certification type (either CDL_standard or CDL_special_needs), and maintenance status (ready or maintenance). Only buses whose status is ready and whose fuel level meets or exceeds the policy threshold may be dispatched. Additional CSV files list medical and transport details for certain students, including whether they require a wheelchair-accessible vehicle; cross-referencing these with the special-needs flag from the student roster is essential. Finally, a set of JSON files describes road conditions and speed multipliers for road segments.

To build the plan, work through every school. Determine how many students are enrolled and how many have special needs. Identify which shelters are reachable from that school by looking up available routes. For each candidate route, check every road segment on the path: compute the bus's loaded weight (empty weight plus the number of passengers times the per-passenger weight from the policy) and confirm it does not exceed the effective bridge limit on any segment of the route, taking into account any updated limits from the bridge-inspection file. A shelter is feasible only if it is not closed, its remaining effective capacity can absorb the students being sent there, and the travel time falls within the policy maximum. Prefer the nearest feasible shelter. If one shelter cannot take all of a school's students, split them across multiple shelters along different routes. Assign deployable buses to fill each load, choosing the lightest buses first when bridge constraints are tight, and ensuring wheelchair-accessible buses are used for loads that include students requiring them.

Once the plan is complete, save three files. The first, `loading_schedule.csv`, must contain exactly the columns `school_name`, `bus_id`, `student_ids`, `shelter_name`, and `departure_order`. The `student_ids` field is a semicolon-separated list of integer student identifiers, sorted in ascending order. The `departure_order` is an integer starting at one for each school, incrementing with each successive bus dispatched from that school. The second file, `dispatch_routes.csv`, must contain exactly the columns `bus_id`, `school_name`, `shelter_name`, `route_segment_ids`, `total_distance_km`, and `bus_loaded_weight_tons`. The `route_segment_ids` field is a semicolon-separated list of segment identifiers in route order. The `total_distance_km` value must be formatted to two decimal places, and `bus_loaded_weight_tons` likewise to two decimal places. The third file, `evacuation_summary.txt`, must contain three lines formatted exactly as follows: `TOTAL_STUDENTS_EVACUATED: ` followed by the integer count, then `TOTAL_BUSES_DISPATCHED: ` followed by the count of distinct buses used, then `SHELTERS_USED: ` followed by the number of distinct shelters that received at least one student.

After writing those files, state your final answer in a single line of the form `EVACUATION COMPLETE: N students evacuated on M buses to K shelters`, substituting the actual totals for N, M, and K.
