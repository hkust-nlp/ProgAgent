You are tasked with performing a full mechanical royalty reconciliation across multiple streaming platforms. The objective is to identify every track where expected royalty payments do not match what was actually paid, producing a formal discrepancy report that collection societies can act upon.

Several streaming platform report files are available in CSV format, each containing the ISRC code of every track streamed, the corresponding stream count, and the territory where those streams occurred. The specific platform and quarterly reporting period can be determined from each file's name, which follows the pattern of the platform name followed by the period label. Alongside these reports you will find a YAML configuration file listing the per-stream mechanical royalty rate for each platform, a CSV file mapping territory codes to collection society names and their commission rates, a JSON policy file specifying reconciliation parameters, and a markdown document with supplementary processing notes explaining the calculation methodology. Read these files carefully before proceeding.

Through the tools at your disposal you can look up track metadata by ISRC, retrieve the full set of publisher and songwriter registrations showing each party's split percentage and registration territory, and pull historical payment records for any given ISRC. These tools are the sole means of accessing the registration database and payment ledger; the information they provide complements but does not duplicate what is available in the files.

For every distinct ISRC appearing in any streaming report, retrieve its registration data and payment history. The expected net payment owed to each registered rights holder for a given reporting period is derived by first totaling the gross royalty for that ISRC in that period, where each streaming entry contributes its stream count multiplied by the corresponding platform's per-stream rate. That period gross is then multiplied by the holder's registered split percentage divided by one hundred and further reduced by the commission rate of the collection society serving the registration territory. Each such per-payee per-period amount should be rounded to two decimal places. The total expected net for the ISRC is the sum of these individually rounded amounts across all payees and all periods in which the ISRC appeared.

Four categories of discrepancy must be identified. An ISRC found in streaming data but absent from any registration record is classified as UNREGISTERED. An ISRC that has valid registrations and streaming activity yet carries zero payment entries is UNPAID. When payments exist but their total across all payees and periods falls short of the total expected net by more than the shortfall threshold specified in the policy file, the ISRC is UNDERPAID. When the registered split percentages for an ISRC do not sum to exactly one hundred, deviating beyond the tolerance stated in the policy file, the ISRC carries a SPLIT_ANOMALY. A single ISRC may qualify under multiple categories and should be listed once for each applicable type.

Save the final discrepancy report as `discrepancy_report.csv`. It must contain the columns `isrc`, `track_title`, `artist`, `discrepancy_type`, `expected_amount`, `paid_amount`, `shortfall`, and `details`, in that exact order. For UNREGISTERED rows, `expected_amount` should be the total gross royalty for the ISRC across all streaming entries, `paid_amount` should be `0.00`, and `shortfall` should equal `expected_amount`. For UNPAID rows, `expected_amount` is the sum of expected net amounts across all registered holders and periods, `paid_amount` is `0.00`, and `shortfall` equals `expected_amount`. For UNDERPAID rows, both `expected_amount` and `paid_amount` reflect totals aggregated over all payees and periods, with `shortfall` being the difference. For SPLIT_ANOMALY rows, `expected_amount` is `100.00`, `paid_amount` is the actual sum of split percentages, and `shortfall` is `0.00`. The `details` column should hold a concise explanation. Where a track title or artist cannot be resolved through the available tools, use the string "Unknown". All monetary values must be rounded to exactly two decimal places. Rows should be sorted first alphabetically by `discrepancy_type`, then by `isrc` in ascending order within each type.

Separately, produce a file called `summary.json` containing the keys `total_tracks_analyzed` giving the number of unique ISRCs across all streaming reports, `total_discrepancies` giving the count of rows in the discrepancy report, `unregistered_count`, `unpaid_count`, `underpaid_count`, `split_anomaly_count`, and `total_shortfall` giving the sum of all shortfall values in the report rounded to two decimal places.

When you have completed the analysis, state in your final response the total count of discrepancies identified and the aggregate shortfall in US dollars.
