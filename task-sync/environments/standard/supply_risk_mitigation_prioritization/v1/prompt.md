You are a supply chain risk analyst tasked with producing a comprehensive, prioritized mitigation action list for your organization's entire supplier base. The goal is to identify which suppliers pose the greatest risk of disruption and prescribe concrete actions drawn from an established playbook.

Begin by locating and reading the configuration files that govern how risk is scored. A YAML configuration file defines the six risk component weights (delivery_performance, geopolitical_risk, material_criticality, supply_alternative, quality_compliance, and financial_health, which together sum to 1.0), the tier thresholds (critical, high, and medium, each with a minimum composite score and a corresponding urgency in days), a late-delivery threshold in days, and a quantity-shortfall threshold as a percentage. A JSON playbook maps each risk component to a prescribed mitigation action string for each tier (critical, high, medium). A CSV criticality matrix maps each integer criticality level to a base risk score and label. These three files must be understood before any scoring can begin.

For every supplier in the system, you must compute six risk sub-scores on a 0-to-100 scale, then combine them into a single composite. Delivery performance risk is the fraction of that supplier's delivery records where the actual delivery date exceeded the promised date by more than the configured late-delivery threshold in days, multiplied by 60, plus the fraction of records where the quantity delivered fell below the quantity ordered times one minus the shortfall-threshold percentage divided by 100, multiplied by 40. Cap the result at 100. If a supplier has no delivery records, treat the delivery risk as zero.

Geopolitical risk starts from the base risk score obtained for the supplier's source region, then adds 20 if the region is under active sanctions, adds the trade-restriction level times 5, and adds a trend modifier of negative 5 for improving, 0 for stable, or positive 10 for declining political stability. Cap at 100. Sanctions status, trade-restriction levels, and stability trends are found in JSON intelligence files rather than through the region query.

Material criticality risk takes the base risk score from the criticality matrix corresponding to the supplier's primary material's criticality level, then adds the product of the material's demand growth rate (if positive; clamp negative growth to zero), the demand adjustment factor, and 50. Demand growth rate is computed as the last-period forecasted quantity minus the first-period forecasted quantity, divided by the first-period quantity, across the quarterly consumption forecast obtained for that material. The adjustment factor for each material is found in YAML adjustment files. If no adjustment entry exists for a material, use a factor of 1.0. Cap at 100.

Supply alternative risk depends on the count of alternate suppliers with a qualification status of "qualified" for the primary supplier. Zero qualified alternates yields a score of 100, one yields 60, two yields 30, and three or more yields 10.

Quality compliance risk uses audit data found in CSV audit report files. Average the quality scores recorded for the supplier, then compute 100 minus that average, adding 15 if any audit entry for that supplier has a compliance flag of zero. Cap at 100. If no audit data exists for a supplier, default the quality risk to 50.

Financial health risk uses structured text files containing each supplier's credit rating and debt ratio. Map the credit rating to a base value of 10 for A, 30 for B, 60 for C, or 90 for D, then add the debt ratio multiplied by 20. Cap at 100. If no financial data exists for a supplier, default the financial risk to 50.

The composite risk score is the weighted sum of all six components using the weights from the configuration file, rounded to exactly two decimal places. Assign each supplier a tier: Critical if the composite meets or exceeds the critical threshold, otherwise High if it meets or exceeds the high threshold, otherwise Medium if it meets or exceeds the medium threshold, otherwise Low. Determine the primary risk factor as the component whose weighted contribution (component score times its weight) is largest; break ties alphabetically by component name. Then look up the mitigation action string from the playbook using the primary risk factor and the supplier's tier.

Once all suppliers have been scored, produce a file called `mitigation_actions.csv` containing only suppliers whose tier is Critical, High, or Medium. Sort rows by composite_risk_score in descending order, breaking ties by supplier_id in ascending order. The file must have exactly these columns in this order: `rank`, `supplier_id`, `supplier_name`, `region_name`, `primary_material`, `composite_risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `primary_risk_factor`, `mitigation_action`, `urgency_days`. The rank column must be a sequential integer starting at 1. The composite_risk_score column must show exactly two decimal places. The urgency_days value comes from the tier configuration.

Separately, produce a file called `risk_summary.json` with four keys: `total_suppliers_assessed` set to the total number of suppliers evaluated, `risk_distribution` as an object mapping each tier name (Critical, High, Medium, Low) to its count, `top_risk_factors` as a list of the distinct primary risk factor names among the actionable suppliers ordered by descending frequency with ties broken alphabetically, and `assessment_date` set to today's date in YYYY-MM-DD format.

In your final response, state the total number of suppliers assessed, the count in each risk tier, and then list the top five highest-risk suppliers with their IDs, composite scores, tiers, primary risk factors, and prescribed mitigation actions.
