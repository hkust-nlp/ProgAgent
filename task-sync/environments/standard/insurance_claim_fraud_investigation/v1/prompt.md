You are tasked with conducting a comprehensive fraud investigation across all insurance claims currently flagged as under investigation. Your objective is to evaluate each such claim against six distinct fraud risk factors, compute a composite fraud score, rank the claims by severity, and produce a detailed case file for downstream review.

Begin by locating the scoring configuration files available to you, which define the exact weight assigned to each of the six fraud factors and the numerical thresholds that determine whether a factor is triggered. A separate file provides regional cost adjustment factors keyed by geographic region, and you will also find a collection of field investigation reports authored by independent adjusters as well as provider billing audit records in structured format. All of these must be consulted during your analysis.

The six fraud risk factors are evaluated as follows. The first factor, designated `amount_anomaly`, checks whether the billed claim amount exceeds the procedure's expected maximum cost after adjusting for the treating provider's geographic region. Retrieve the expected cost range for the claim's procedure code, determine the provider's region, look up the corresponding regional adjustment factor, multiply the expected maximum by that factor, and then check whether the claim amount exceeds this adjusted maximum by more than the percentage threshold specified in the scoring configuration. The second factor, `rapid_claims`, examines whether the claimant has filed an unusually high volume of claims within a concentrated time window. For each claim under investigation, retrieve the claimant's full claim history and count all claims whose dates fall within the configured number of days of the current claim's date, inclusive of the claim itself. If the count meets or exceeds the minimum specified in the configuration, this factor is triggered. The third factor, `provider_risk`, assesses the treating provider's billing deviation as documented in the provider audit records. Locate the provider's average claim deviation percentage from the audit files and compare it against the risk tier thresholds defined in the scoring configuration. If the deviation meets or exceeds the threshold for the high tier, this factor is triggered. The fourth factor, `new_policy`, checks whether the insurance policy associated with the claim was initiated recently relative to the claim filing date. Compute the number of days between the policy's start date and the claim date, and if this value is non-negative and does not exceed the configured maximum, the factor is triggered. The fifth factor, `adjuster_flags`, requires reviewing the field investigation reports for any mention of recognized red flag phrases associated with the claim. The red flag phrases to search for are: "inconsistent statements", "pre-existing condition suspected", "unverified injury", "suspicious timing", "conflicting documentation", and "exaggerated symptoms". If any of these phrases appear in the section of a report pertaining to the claim, this factor is triggered. The sixth and final factor, `coverage_ratio`, compares the claim amount to the total coverage amount of the associated policy. If the ratio of claim amount to coverage amount meets or exceeds the configured minimum threshold, this factor is triggered.

For each claim under investigation, compute the total fraud score by summing the configured weights of all triggered factors. Once all claims have been scored, rank them in descending order of fraud score, using the claim identifier in ascending alphabetical order as the tiebreaker for equal scores. Assign each claim a priority rank starting from one for the highest-scoring claim.

Save the complete results to a file named `fraud_cases.csv` containing exactly these columns in this order: `claim_id`, `claimant_name`, `provider_name`, `fraud_score`, `priority_rank`, `evidence_summary`. The claimant name must be formatted as the first name followed by a space and the last name. The fraud score must be an integer. The priority rank must be an integer reflecting the position in the sorted order described above. The evidence summary for each claim must list the identifiers of all triggered factors separated by the pipe character, using exactly the names `amount_anomaly`, `rapid_claims`, `provider_risk`, `new_policy`, `adjuster_flags`, and `coverage_ratio`, including only those that were triggered, listed in this fixed order. If no factors were triggered, the evidence summary should be an empty string.

In your final response, state the total number of cases analyzed in the format "Total cases analyzed: N" where N is the count. Provide a breakdown of cases by priority tier, where cases with a fraud score at or above the critical threshold are labeled Critical, those at or above the high threshold but below critical are High, those at or above the medium threshold but below high are Medium, and those below the medium threshold are Low. Present this as "Critical: N", "High: N", "Medium: N", "Low: N" using the tier boundaries defined in the scoring configuration. Finally, list the top three highest-scoring cases by their claim identifiers and fraud scores.
