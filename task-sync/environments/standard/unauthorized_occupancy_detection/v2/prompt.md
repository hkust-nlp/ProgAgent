You are a property management analyst responsible for identifying residential units that may be involved in unauthorized subletting or occupancy violations. The property management company has been receiving scattered reports of potential lease violations across its portfolio, and a systematic, data-driven investigation is now required.

Your analysis draws on two complementary classes of information. The first consists of files available in your working directory. Utility billing records stored as CSV files contain monthly electricity, water, and gas consumption figures for every unit over a six-month monitoring window. Access card reports in JSON format document the number of unique access cards associated with each unit along with the identities of the card holders and their entry counts. An investigation policy file in YAML format lays out the exact criteria, scoring weights, thresholds, and output formatting rules that govern whether a unit warrants formal investigation. An occupancy-standards reference file provides baseline utility consumption expectations organized by unit size, and a building-regulations document summarizes the relevant occupancy rules.

The second class of information is accessible only through the tools at your disposal. You can enumerate all units in the system, retrieve the lease agreement details for any given unit (including the maximum number of allowed occupants and whether subletting is permitted), look up the occupants formally registered on a lease, and pull every complaint that has been filed against a particular unit together with its category, date, and status.

The investigation follows a multi-signal scoring methodology described in the policy file. When a unit's average monthly electricity consumption exceeds the per-occupant threshold defined in the policy multiplied by the unit's maximum allowed occupant count, that constitutes one signal. The same logic applies independently to average monthly water consumption using its own threshold. If the number of unique access cards recorded for a unit is strictly greater than the maximum allowed occupant count multiplied by the access-card multiplier specified in the policy, that constitutes another signal. Complaints filed against a unit whose category appears in the policy's list of categories of concern each contribute points, with each qualifying complaint worth the score specified in the policy, up to a capped maximum also stated there. Separately, if a unit's lease explicitly prohibits subletting and at least one other signal has already been triggered for that unit, an additional penalty score is added as defined in the policy. Each signal carries a point value laid out in the policy, and a unit is flagged for formal investigation only when its accumulated score meets or exceeds the investigation threshold.

Once you have evaluated every unit in the system, produce a file called `investigation_report.csv`. This file must contain exactly four columns: `unit_id`, `total_score`, `signals`, and `subletting_allowed`. The `signals` column must list the names of all triggered signals for that unit in alphabetical order, separated by a semicolon followed by a space. The `subletting_allowed` column must read `yes` when the lease permits subletting and `no` when it does not. Only units whose total score meets or exceeds the investigation threshold should appear in the report. Sort the rows by `total_score` in descending order; where two units share the same score, break the tie by sorting `unit_id` in ascending alphabetical order.

Conclude your response by stating which units require formal investigation, listing their unit IDs separated by commas in the same order they appear in the report.
