You are a fraud analyst at a property insurance company tasked with reviewing all pending water damage claims to identify those that warrant further investigation. The company has recently seen a spike in suspicious water damage filings, and your job is to systematically evaluate every pending claim against a set of fraud indicators, score each one, and produce a prioritized list of claims that should be escalated to the special investigations unit.

Begin by locating the fraud detection configuration file, which is stored as a YAML document and defines all the indicators you must check, their scoring weights, the thresholds that trigger each indicator, and the rules for assigning priority levels. This configuration file also specifies the exact output format you must follow, including the required filename, column names, sort order, and how multi-value fields should be delimited. You must follow every detail in that configuration exactly.

For each pending claim, you need to retrieve its full details through the available claim lookup tools, then pull the associated policy information to learn the policyholder's name, coverage dates, and other relevant policy attributes. Next, query the historical weather records for the incident location on the date the damage allegedly occurred. You should also retrieve the policyholder's prior claims history to check for patterns of repeated filing.

Separately, you will find a directory of contractor repair estimate files, one per claim, stored as JSON documents. Each estimate file lists one or more contractor bids with amounts, contractor identifiers, and other details. You will also find a contractor registry file in CSV format that maps contractor identifiers to their license status, rating, and other attributes. Cross-reference the contractors appearing in each claim's estimates against this registry to determine whether any contractor involved lacks an active license.

The fraud indicators you must evaluate for every pending claim are as follows. A weather mismatch occurs when neither the precipitation nor the wind speed on the incident date at the incident zip code meet the thresholds defined in the configuration, and no storm event was recorded. An estimate discrepancy is triggered when the claimed amount exceeds the highest contractor estimate by more than the percentage threshold specified in the configuration. A repeat claimant flag applies when the policyholder's number of prior claims within the lookback window meets or exceeds the minimum count defined in the configuration. Late filing is flagged when the number of days between the incident date and the filed date exceeds the maximum allowed. An unlicensed contractor flag is raised if any contractor on the claim's estimate does not have an active license status in the registry. A coverage gap is identified when the policy was not active on the incident date, meaning the incident date falls outside the policy's effective and expiration date range.

Once you have evaluated all indicators for every pending claim, compute a fraud score for each claim by summing the weights of all triggered indicators. Only claims with a fraud score greater than zero should appear in the output. Assign each flagged claim a priority level according to the score thresholds in the configuration, checking from the highest level downward and assigning the first level whose minimum score is met. Sort the results by fraud score in descending order, breaking ties by claim identifier in ascending order, and assign a sequential priority rank starting from one.

Save the final output as `investigation_list.csv` with columns `claim_id`, `policy_id`, `holder_name`, `fraud_score`, `flags`, `priority_level`, and `priority_rank`, in that exact column order. The flags column must list all triggered indicator names for that claim, separated by a semicolon and a space, with the indicator names sorted alphabetically. Fraud scores should be written as integers.

In your final response, summarize the overall findings and explicitly name the top three highest-priority claims by their claim identifiers, along with their fraud scores, priority levels, and the specific flags that were triggered for each.
