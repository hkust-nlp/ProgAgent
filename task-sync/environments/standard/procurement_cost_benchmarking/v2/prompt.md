You are supporting a government agency preparing for a major public IT procurement cycle spanning several technology categories. The agency needs a comprehensive, risk-adjusted cost benchmark that accounts for historical bidding behavior, current material price trends, vendor financial stability, and internal budget constraints. Your job is to produce this benchmark by cross-referencing multiple data sources and applying a structured scoring methodology.

Begin by reviewing the eligibility criteria file, which specifies the minimum credit score, minimum current ratio, maximum debt-to-asset ratio, minimum years of active operation, and the reference fiscal year for financial evaluation, as well as the target procurement year. Using vendor listing and financial query capabilities, identify every vendor that satisfies all eligibility thresholds simultaneously: active status, credit score at or above the minimum, current ratio at or above the floor, debt-to-asset ratio at or below the ceiling, and years in business (procurement year minus incorporation year) meeting or exceeding the minimum. Vendors failing any single criterion must be excluded entirely.

For every eligible vendor, compute a composite risk score. The scoring configuration file defines three weighted components. The first, financial health, is itself a weighted blend of the normalized credit score (subtract the configured minimum from the raw score and divide by the configured range), the normalized current ratio (the ratio divided by the configured cap, clamped to a maximum of one), and a leverage score equal to one minus the debt-to-asset ratio; the sub-weights for these three elements are specified in the configuration. The second component, operational risk, is derived from an operational risk rating recorded in vendor assessment dossiers found among the provided files; each vendor has a rating on a one-to-five scale, and the score is computed as five minus the rating divided by four. The third component, delivery track record, comes from a past delivery score reported in performance data files; each vendor's score on a zero-to-one-hundred scale is divided by one hundred to normalize it. If a vendor's operational risk rating or delivery score cannot be found in the files, use a default rating of three-point-zero for operational risk and seventy for delivery score. Multiply each component by its configured weight and sum to obtain the composite risk score.

Next, retrieve all historical bids and group them by vendor and procurement category. For each eligible vendor in each category where they have submitted at least one past bid, calculate the average bid amount. Then determine the price adjustment factor for each category: look up the material price index at the reference year and quarter specified in the scoring configuration, divide it by the index at the configured base year and quarter, and multiply by the forecast adjustment factor for that category found in the market forecast file. The price-adjusted bid equals the historical average bid multiplied by this price adjustment factor.

Derive a risk premium for each vendor using the configured base premium and scale factor: the premium equals the base plus the scale factor multiplied by one minus the composite risk score. The risk-adjusted cost for each vendor-category pair is the price-adjusted bid multiplied by one plus the risk premium.

Once all vendor-category records have been computed, produce a file named `benchmark_report.csv` containing exactly these columns in this order: `category`, `vendor_id`, `vendor_name`, `historical_avg_bid`, `price_adjustment_factor`, `price_adjusted_bid`, `risk_score`, `risk_premium`, `risk_adjusted_cost`. Round all monetary values to two decimal places, and round `price_adjustment_factor`, `risk_score`, and `risk_premium` to four decimal places. Sort the rows first by category in ascending alphabetical order, then within each category by risk-adjusted cost in ascending order.

Separately, produce a file named `category_summary.json` structured as a JSON object whose keys are the category names. Each category object must contain: `benchmark_cost` set to the median of the risk-adjusted costs for that category (for an even count, average the two middle values and round to two decimals), `min_cost` and `max_cost` (the lowest and highest risk-adjusted costs rounded to two decimals), `num_eligible_vendors` (the count of eligible vendor entries in that category), `recommended_vendor_id` and `recommended_vendor_name` (the vendor with the lowest risk-adjusted cost in that category), `budget_allocation` (retrieved for that category and the procurement fiscal year, rounded to two decimals), and `within_budget` (a boolean indicating whether the benchmark cost does not exceed the budget allocation).

Finally, your concluding response must state the total estimated procurement cost, which is the sum of the benchmark costs across all categories, formatted as "Total estimated procurement cost: $X" where X uses commas as thousands separators and two decimal places. It must also state the overall risk rating determined by averaging the composite risk scores of all eligible vendors: if the average is at least zero-point-seven the rating is LOW, if at least zero-point-four it is MEDIUM, otherwise HIGH, formatted as "Overall risk rating: LEVEL". Include how many categories fall within their respective budgets.
