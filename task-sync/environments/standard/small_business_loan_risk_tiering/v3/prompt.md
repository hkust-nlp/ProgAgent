You are a credit analyst responsible for processing every pending small business loan application currently in the system. Your task is to evaluate each application by cross-referencing data from multiple sources and then produce a prioritized approval queue with risk-tiered credit limits.

Begin by exploring the files available to you in order to familiarize yourself with the lending policies, risk scoring configuration, and industry benchmark data that govern the evaluation process. These configuration documents specify eligibility thresholds, a weighted scoring formula with six components and their associated bands, risk tier boundaries, and the credit limit computation method. Understanding these rules thoroughly before starting the evaluation is essential.

For each pending application, retrieve the application details from the system, then locate the corresponding financial profile among the available files. Financial profiles are stored as JSON files organized within a "financials" directory, named by application identifier, and contain the applicant's credit score, annual revenue, total existing debt, and years in business. You must also pull the applicant's bank transaction history covering the six months prior to March 20, 2026, examine every collateral asset pledged against the application by requesting its current market valuation, and check the lending institution's exposure position in the applicant's industry to confirm that capacity remains available.

The composite risk score combines six weighted component scores, each on a zero-to-one-hundred scale. These components assess the quality of the applicant's credit score, the debt-to-income ratio, the stability and trend of cash flows derived from the transaction history, collateral coverage relative to the requested loan amount, industry-level default risk, and business maturity measured by years of operation. The precise weights, band boundaries, and scoring values for each component are defined in the configuration files you will find alongside the financial profiles. Once computed, the composite score maps to a risk tier that in turn determines the credit limit percentage applied to the requested amount. The final approved credit limit equals the smallest of three quantities: the tier-adjusted requested amount, the sum of current market values across all pledged collateral assets, and the lender's remaining exposure capacity in the relevant industry.

Applications that fail any of the minimum eligibility requirements described in the lending policy must be denied, with the specific grounds for denial recorded. All remaining applications receive approval with the calculated credit limit.

After evaluating every pending application, save a file named "approval_queue.csv" containing columns in this exact order: rank, application_id, business_name, industry_code, risk_score, risk_tier, credit_limit, decision, denial_reason. Approved applications receive sequential integer ranks starting from one, ordered by composite risk score descending, with ties broken first by earlier submission date and then by ascending application identifier. Denied applications carry an empty rank field, a credit_limit of 0.00, decision set to "DENIED", and a populated denial_reason field. Approved applications carry decision "APPROVED" with an empty denial_reason. Express risk_score and credit_limit to exactly two decimal places.

In your final response, state how many pending applications you evaluated in total, how many were approved versus denied, and identify the top three applications in the priority queue by their application identifier, risk score, and approved credit limit.
