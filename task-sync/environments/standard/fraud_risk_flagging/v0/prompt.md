You are a fraud analyst supporting the Special Investigations Unit of a workers' compensation insurance carrier. Your organization maintains a claims database that you can query through the available tools, and a collection of supplementary files that have been assembled by adjusters, auditors, and the internal investigations team. Your objective is to evaluate every claim for potential fraud risk, score each one according to a documented set of rules, and produce a prioritized investigation list so the SIU can allocate resources efficiently.

Begin by familiarizing yourself with the fraud scoring configuration. A YAML file named `fraud_scoring_rules.yaml` lays out each fraud indicator, the conditions that trigger it, and the point weight it carries. A CSV file called `risk_thresholds.csv` maps each injury type to the claim-amount ceiling above which the amount is considered excessive. A policy document named `investigation_priority_policy.md` describes how risk levels are assigned based on total score, the sorting and tie-breaking rules for the final list, and the exact fields required in the output. Study all three files carefully before proceeding — the thresholds and weights defined there govern every scoring decision.

Retrieve all claims through the claims-listing tool, paging through the full set. For each claim you will need to cross-reference multiple data sources. Query the medical-treatment records for the claim to check whether any treatment date falls before the reported injury date and to identify which providers were involved. Look up each provider's billing anomaly score in the provider audit report files stored under `provider_audit_reports`; a score exceeding the threshold defined in the scoring rules triggers the provider-anomaly indicator. Retrieve the witness statements for the claim and compute the average consistency rating from the database records, then consult the witness reliability assessments stored in `witness_assessments` to compute the average reliability rating for the same claim; if either average falls below its respective threshold, the witness-inconsistency indicator fires. Pull the employee record for the claimant to determine their hire date; an employee whose tenure before the injury date is shorter than the threshold counts as a new-employee indicator. Check the employee's full claims history to see whether they have filed more than one claim within the lookback window, which would trigger the repeat-claimant indicator. Finally, review the adjuster dossier files under `claim_dossiers` to obtain the credibility score assigned during the field assessment; a score below the credibility threshold triggers the low-credibility indicator. The late-filing and excessive-amount indicators are computed directly from the claim's filing delay and amount versus the injury-type threshold, respectively.

Sum the weights of all triggered indicators for each claim to obtain its total risk score. Classify the risk level according to the boundaries in the scoring rules — critical, high, or medium — for every claim whose score meets or exceeds the investigation threshold. Claims below the threshold are not included in the investigation output.

Save the investigation list as `investigation_list.csv`. Each row must contain the following columns in this order: `rank`, `claim_id`, `employee_name`, `department`, `risk_score`, `risk_level`, `fraud_indicators`, `recommended_action`. The rank starts at 1 for the highest-risk claim. The risk score must be an integer. The fraud indicators column must contain the triggered indicator names separated by pipe characters and sorted alphabetically within each cell, for example `late_filing|provider_anomaly`. The recommended action is the text associated with the claim's risk level in the scoring rules. The file must be sorted in descending order of risk score, with ties broken by claim ID in ascending lexicographic order.

Separately, produce a JSON file named `risk_summary.json` containing the total number of claims analyzed under the key `total_claims_analyzed`, the number of flagged claims under `flagged_claims`, a nested object `risk_distribution` mapping each risk level (critical, high, medium) to its count, and the average risk score across all analyzed claims (not just flagged ones) rounded to two decimal places under `average_risk_score`.

Once both files are written, respond with a brief summary stating how many claims were analyzed, how many were flagged, the breakdown by risk level, and the overall average risk score.
