You are a property management analyst responsible for identifying tenants who should receive proactive lease renewal offers. Your portfolio contains residential properties spread across multiple neighborhoods, and a number of current leases are approaching their expiration dates. The goal is to evaluate every tenant whose lease is due to expire soon, determine which ones merit a renewal incentive based on their track record, and produce a targeted outreach list with specific offer terms tailored to each qualifying tenant.

Begin by consulting the renewal policy configuration file stored in the working directory. It specifies the current reference date to use for all date-based calculations, the number of days that define the upcoming expiry window, the eligibility thresholds for payment timeliness, maintenance request frequency, and weighted damage costs, the scoring weights for each factor, and the rules governing market-based rent adjustments. A separate file provides the mapping from damage severity levels to their numerical weights, and another file lists the discount tiers with their score boundaries, corresponding discount percentages, and recommended lease term lengths in months.

Using the reference date and expiry window from the policy, search for all tenants whose leases end within that window. For each such tenant, retrieve their complete payment history, their maintenance request records, and their property damage incident log. A payment is considered on time if the paid date is on or before the due date. The payment score for a tenant is the fraction of their payments that were on time. The maintenance frequency is the total number of maintenance requests divided by the tenant's years of tenancy, where years of tenancy equals the number of days from lease start to the reference date divided by 365.25. The weighted damage cost considers only incidents where the tenant was responsible and is calculated by summing, for each such incident, the repair cost multiplied by the severity weight corresponding to that incident's severity level.

A tenant is eligible for a renewal incentive only if their payment score meets or exceeds the minimum threshold, their maintenance frequency does not exceed the maximum allowed, and their weighted damage cost does not exceed the maximum allowed. All three thresholds come from the policy file. For each eligible tenant, compute a maintenance score as the maximum of zero and one minus the maintenance frequency divided by the maximum allowed frequency, and a damage score as the maximum of zero and one minus the weighted damage cost divided by the maximum allowed weighted damage cost. The overall score is the weighted sum of the payment score, maintenance score, and damage score using the weights from the policy.

The overall score determines the tenant's discount tier. Match the score against the tier boundaries in the discount tiers file, where the lower bound is inclusive and the upper bound is exclusive, except that a perfect score of 1.0 falls into the highest tier. Each tier specifies a base discount percentage and a lease term in months.

Separately, determine the comparable market rent for each tenant's unit. For each tenant, look up the property to find its neighborhood and number of bedrooms. In the working directory there are market comparable report files, one per neighborhood, each named after the neighborhood and provided in varying formats. The comparable market rent is the average of the monthly rents across all listings in the matching neighborhood report that have the same number of bedrooms as the tenant's unit. Round this average to two decimal places.

Once both the tier discount and the market rent are known, check whether the tenant's current rent exceeds the market rent multiplied by the rent-above-market threshold from the policy. If it does, add the extra discount percentage specified in the policy to the base tier discount. The proposed rent is the tenant's current rent multiplied by one minus the total discount percentage divided by one hundred, rounded to two decimal places.

Compile the final outreach list and save it as `outreach_list.csv`. The file must contain exactly these columns in this order: `tenant_id`, `tenant_name`, `unit`, `current_rent`, `market_rent`, `payment_score`, `overall_score`, `tier`, `discount_pct`, `proposed_rent`, `lease_term_months`. Payment scores and overall scores should be rounded to four decimal places. The rows must be sorted by overall score in descending order, with ties broken by tenant id in ascending order.

After saving the file, provide a concise summary stating how many tenants were identified as eligible for lease renewal incentives out of the total number of tenants with expiring leases.
