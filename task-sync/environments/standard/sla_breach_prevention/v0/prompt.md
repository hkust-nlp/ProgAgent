Your IT department tracks support tickets across eight specialized groups, each overseen by a different manager. Every ticket carries a priority from P1 through P4 and is tied to a customer whose service tier determines the response time guaranteed under the SLA. A YAML configuration file in your working directory defines the number of hours each priority-and-tier combination allows before a breach, a warning-threshold percentage that marks when a ticket transitions from normal to at-risk status, and a reference timestamp that should be treated as the current moment for all deadline calculations.

Alongside this, JSON batch files hold per-ticket business impact assessments, each recording a numerical impact score on a one-to-ten scale, affected user counts, and hourly revenue exposure. CSV files capture technician availability for the current day, listing each technician's remaining available hours, active ticket count, and maximum concurrent capacity. A separate CSV maps each priority level to a numerical weight used in risk scoring. A JSON configuration provides the formula for computing a composite risk score, a reassignment penalty rate, an intervention threshold, an escalation threshold, minimum available-hours requirements, and a mapping of minimum skill levels required per priority. A markdown reference document maps each department to its manager by name.

Using the available tools, retrieve all currently active tickets, meaning those whose status is open or in progress. For each one, determine the customer's service tier, look up the applicable SLA window from the YAML configuration, and calculate how many hours remain before the deadline relative to the reference timestamp. A ticket qualifies as at risk when its remaining hours fall at or below the product of the total SLA window and the warning-threshold percentage divided by one hundred.

For every at-risk ticket, gather its full assignment history and escalation log through the tools, and look up its business impact score from the JSON batch files. Compute the risk score by multiplying the priority weight by a time-urgency factor, then by a reassignment-penalty factor, and finally by the business impact score divided by ten. The time-urgency factor is calculated as one minus the ratio of hours remaining to total SLA hours, clamped to a minimum of 0.1 and a maximum of 2.0. The reassignment-penalty factor equals one plus the penalty rate from the JSON configuration multiplied by the number of past reassignments, where the reassignment count is the total number of assignment records minus one. Round the resulting risk score to two decimal places.

Include in the intervention list only those at-risk tickets whose risk score meets or exceeds the intervention threshold from the configuration. For each such ticket, determine the recommendation as follows. If the risk score also meets or exceeds the escalation threshold, recommend escalation and set the target to the name of the department manager responsible for the department of the currently assigned technician. Otherwise, attempt reassignment by identifying, among technicians in the same department as the currently assigned technician, the one with the most available hours whose skill level value meets or exceeds the minimum required for the ticket's priority, excluding the current assignee and excluding anyone whose available hours fall below the minimum threshold. If a qualifying technician exists, recommend reassignment and set the target to that technician's name, breaking available-hours ties by choosing the technician with the lowest identifier. If no suitable candidate is found, recommend escalation instead, again targeting the department manager.

Save the resulting list as `intervention_list.csv` with columns in this exact order: `ticket_id`, `priority`, `customer_tier`, `sla_deadline`, `hours_remaining`, `reassignment_count`, `escalation_level`, `business_impact_score`, `risk_score`, `current_technician_id`, `recommendation`, `target`. Format `hours_remaining` to one decimal place and `risk_score` to two decimal places. Express `sla_deadline` as a timestamp in year-month-day hour-colon-minute-colon-second form. Sort rows by risk score in descending order, breaking ties by hours remaining in ascending order.

In your final response, report on separate lines the total number of active tickets examined as "Active tickets examined: N", the number of at-risk tickets found as "At-risk tickets: N", the total number of entries in the intervention list as "Interventions required: N", the breakdown as "Escalations: N, Reassignments: N", and the identifiers of the three tickets carrying the highest risk scores as "Top 3 risk ticket IDs: X, Y, Z" listed in descending risk-score order and separated by comma-space.
