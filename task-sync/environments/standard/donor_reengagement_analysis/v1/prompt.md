You are working as a data analyst for a large nonprofit organization that needs to win back donors who have gone silent. Your objective is to perform a thorough donor engagement analysis by pulling together information from several distinct sources — campaign email open rates, donation records, event attendance logs, and demographic segments — ultimately producing a prioritized re-engagement contact list with individually tailored suggested ask amounts.

Begin by locating the policy configuration file that governs the re-engagement analysis. It specifies a reference date that anchors all date calculations and a lapsed threshold expressed in months. A donor qualifies as lapsed when their most recent donation falls strictly before the date obtained by subtracting the threshold number of months from the reference date. Only donors who have made at least one past donation can be lapsed; individuals with no donation history at all should be ignored entirely.

Once you have assembled the full set of lapsed donors, compute an engagement score for each one. The engagement score is a weighted sum of three components whose weights are defined in the policy configuration. The first component is the donor's email open rate, calculated as the number of campaigns the donor opened divided by the number of campaigns they were sent. The second is the donor's event attendance rate, calculated as the number of events the donor actually attended divided by the total number of events for which the donor has any attendance record. The third is a giving history score equal to the donor's total lifetime donation amount divided by a benchmark figure specified in the policy, capped at a maximum of one. Multiply each component by its respective weight and sum the three products to obtain the final engagement score. A lapsed donor is deemed high-potential when their engagement score meets or exceeds the high-potential threshold stated in the policy file.

Before the list can be finalized you must consult the donor notes data files, which are JSON files containing per-donor records. Any donor whose record carries a do-not-contact flag set to true must be removed from the output regardless of how strong their engagement score is. Those same files also record each donor's preferred contact method, a field that must appear in the final deliverable.

For every high-potential lapsed donor who survives the exclusion step, calculate a suggested ask amount. Start from the donor's average past donation. Multiply that figure by the segment-specific multiplier found in the segment tier configuration file. Then apply a recency adjustment: the policy configuration lists recency tiers, each defined by a maximum-months-since-last-donation ceiling and a multiplication factor. Walk through the tiers in the order they appear and use the factor from the first tier whose ceiling is greater than or equal to the donor's months-since-last-donation value. Compute months since last donation as the integer obtained by taking the difference in years between the reference date and the last donation date, multiplying by twelve, then adding the difference in months. After multiplying by the recency factor, round the result to the nearest increment specified by the rounding parameter in the policy. Finally, clamp the value so it falls no lower than the minimum ask and no higher than the maximum ask defined for the donor's segment in the segment tier configuration.

Save the completed contact list as `reengagement_list.csv`. The file must contain exactly these columns in this order: `donor_id`, `first_name`, `last_name`, `email`, `phone`, `segment`, `engagement_score`, `months_since_last_donation`, `suggested_ask_amount`, `preferred_contact_method`. Format the engagement score to exactly four decimal places. The suggested ask amount must be a whole number with no decimal point. Sort all rows by engagement score in descending order; where two donors share the same engagement score, the one with the lower donor ID should appear first.

In your final response, state the total number of lapsed donors identified before any filtering, the number of high-potential donors that appear on the finished list after all exclusions, and the total suggested ask amount across every entry on the list formatted as a dollar figure with exactly two decimal places and comma separators for thousands.
