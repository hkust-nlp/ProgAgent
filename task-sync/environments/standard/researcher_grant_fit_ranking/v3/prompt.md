You are a research funding analyst working for a large university consortium. Your task is to match every researcher in the system with the open grant calls that best fit their profile, producing a comprehensive ranked output that the grants office can act upon immediately.

Begin by exploring the files in your working directory. You will find configuration files that specify the scoring weights, thresholds, and parameters for evaluation, a field taxonomy that defines compatibility scores between pairs of research fields, an institution registry listing each institution's tier classification, eligibility rules that govern which agencies impose tier or certification requirements, a scoring guide that describes the precise formulas for each scoring dimension, and a collection of researcher dossier files that provide supplementary information about each researcher's additional keywords, professional certifications, and past grant history. These dossier files contain information that is not available through any other channel and must be read to complete the task.

To gather structured data about researchers, funding opportunities, publication records, institutional expenditures, and collaboration networks, you have access to query tools. Use these tools to retrieve researcher listings, individual researcher profiles, funding call listings and details, publication records per researcher, expenditure reports for an institution and department, and collaborator network information for each researcher. Be aware that listing endpoints are paginated and you must iterate through all pages to ensure complete coverage.

For each researcher, determine which funding calls they are eligible for. A researcher qualifies for a specific grant only when their career stage appears among the grant's listed eligible career stages, their h-index meets or exceeds the grant's stated minimum, their home institution's tier is no greater than the tier threshold specified for the granting agency in the eligibility rules (where tier one is the most prestigious and a lower number is better), the researcher holds every certification that the agency requires according to the eligibility rules (checking the certifications listed in the researcher's dossier), and the external funding ratio for the researcher's home department at their institution meets or exceeds the minimum expenditure external ratio specified in the eligibility rules. The external funding ratio is calculated as external funding divided by total budget, using the most recent fiscal year's data.

Once you have identified all eligible researcher-grant pairs, compute a fit score for each. The score is a weighted sum of five dimensions, with weights drawn from the scoring configuration. The first dimension is field alignment: compute the compatibility score between each combination of the researcher's primary and secondary fields and the grant's primary and secondary focus areas, using the field taxonomy, then take the maximum of these four values. The second dimension is publication strength: consider only publications from the year specified as the recent-year cutoff onward, then among those keep only "relevant" ones whose field has a compatibility score at or above the field relevance threshold with the grant's primary focus, then calculate a count component as the number of relevant publications divided by the maximum relevant publications parameter (capped at one), and a citation component as the mean citation count of relevant publications divided by the citation normalization parameter (capped at one, or zero if there are no relevant publications), and combine them as six-tenths times the count component plus four-tenths times the citation component. The third dimension is expenditure fit: take the external funding ratio for the researcher's department at their institution for the most recent fiscal year and divide by the target external ratio from the configuration (capped at one). The fourth dimension is collaboration score: if the grant requires collaboration, count the researcher's collaborators whose shared field has a compatibility at or above the field relevance threshold with the grant's primary focus, then divide that count by one less than the grant's minimum team size (capped at one); if the minimum team size minus one is zero or negative, score one if any relevant collaborators exist and zero otherwise; if the grant does not require collaboration, divide the researcher's total number of collaborators by five (capped at one). The fifth dimension is keyword match: from the researcher's dossier, count how many of their research keywords have a compatibility at or above the field relevance threshold with the grant's primary focus, then divide by the total number of their keywords; if the researcher has no keywords, score zero.

The final fit score for each pair is the weighted sum of these five dimensions, rounded to exactly two decimal places. Exclude any pair whose fit score falls below the minimum fit score threshold from the configuration. For each researcher, retain at most the number of top grants specified by the top-n-per-researcher parameter. Rank grants per researcher in descending order of fit score, breaking ties by ascending grant identifier. The best-fit grant for each researcher receives rank one.

Save the results to a file named `ranked_matches.csv` containing exactly these columns in this order: `researcher_id`, `researcher_name`, `grant_id`, `grant_title`, `fit_score`, `rank`. Rows should be sorted by researcher identifier ascending and then by rank ascending within each researcher. Fit scores must appear with exactly two decimal places.

In your final response, provide a summary that lists every researcher who has at least one match, stating their name, their identifier, the title of their top-ranked grant, and the corresponding fit score.
