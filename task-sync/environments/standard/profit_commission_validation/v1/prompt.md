You are reviewing the quarterly profit commission statements submitted by a reinsurance company for all active treaties in the Q4-2025 period. Your task is to independently verify each statement's calculated commission amount by cross-referencing multiple data sources, and to produce a validation report that either approves each statement or flags it as a discrepancy with supporting evidence.

For each treaty, the reinsurer has filed a commission statement containing reported figures for ceded premiums, incurred losses, expense loading, the prior-year loss carry-forward applied, the resulting loss ratio, the applied commission rate, and the final commission amount. You need to reconstruct every one of these figures from the underlying source data and compare them against the reported values.

Begin by identifying all treaties under review. For each treaty, gather the individual ceded premium records and sum them to determine the true total ceded premium for the quarter. Next, collect all paid loss records for that treaty. Separately, each treaty has an associated loss bordereaux CSV file named following the pattern `bordereaux_<treaty_id>.csv`, where the treaty identifier takes the form used in the system. These files contain claim-level loss adjustment expense entries with columns for claim reference, expense type, expense amount, and adjuster name. The loss adjustment expense amounts listed in the bordereaux must be summed and added to the paid losses to arrive at total incurred losses for the treaty.

The expense loading for each treaty is calculated by multiplying the total ceded premium by a combined expense percentage. A JSON configuration file called `expense_parameters.json` provides management expense and brokerage fee percentages keyed by treaty identifier, along with a pre-computed total expense percentage field that should be used for the loading calculation.

Once the incurred losses are established, obtain each treaty's prior-year loss carry-forward. The relevant figure is the closing balance, which equals the opening balance plus any adjustments applied during the prior year's annual settlement. A reference document called `prior_year_summary.md` provides a narrative summary of these positions for cross-checking purposes.

To determine the applicable commission rate, first calculate the loss ratio as total incurred losses divided by total ceded premium. The carry-forward balance does not enter the loss ratio computation. Then consult the file `sliding_scale_commission.yaml`, which contains tiered commission rate schedules organized by line of business. Each tier specifies a minimum and maximum loss ratio range and the corresponding commission rate. The treaty's line of business determines which schedule applies. Find the bracket where the minimum is less than or equal to the loss ratio and the maximum is strictly greater than the loss ratio, and use the rate from that bracket.

With the loss ratio and commission rate in hand, compute the net profit as total ceded premium minus total incurred losses minus expense loading minus the loss carry-forward closing balance. If the net profit is positive, the profit commission equals the net profit multiplied by the commission rate from the sliding scale. If the net profit is zero or negative, the profit commission is zero. A complete specification of these formulas and the applicable tolerance thresholds is documented in a file called `validation_rules.txt`.

Compare your independently calculated commission amount against the reported commission amount from the filed statement. A variance of less than one dollar in absolute terms should be treated as an acceptable rounding difference and the statement should be approved. Any variance of one dollar or more constitutes a discrepancy requiring investigation.

Save your findings as `validation_report.csv` containing one row per treaty with the following columns in this exact order: `treaty_id`, `cedant_name`, `status`, `reported_commission`, `calculated_commission`, `variance`, `discrepancy_reason`. The `status` column must contain either `APPROVED` or `DISCREPANCY`. The `variance` column should equal the reported commission minus the calculated commission, rounded to two decimal places. For approved treaties the `discrepancy_reason` field should be empty. For discrepancies provide a brief explanation identifying the source of the variance, such as which input figure differs from the independently derived value.

In your final response, state the total count of approved treaties, the total count of discrepancies found, and the aggregate variance across all discrepant statements rounded to two decimal places.
