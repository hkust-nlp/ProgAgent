You are the revenue manager for a large hotel property and have been asked to produce daily rate recommendations for every room type over an upcoming forecast horizon. The property's configuration files describe the available room categories together with each category's base nightly rate, total room capacity, and permissible floor and ceiling rates. A separate pricing-rules file specifies the forecast reference date and the number of days to forecast, along with all numerical parameters that govern how demand signals translate into pricing adjustments.

Five distinct demand signals must be synthesized into a single adjustment percentage for each combination of forecast date and room type. The pricing-rules file lists the weight assigned to each signal, event-category impact factors, occupancy-scoring parameters (a baseline ratio and a scale factor), a flight-demand divisor, the number of recent days used when measuring the demand trend, minimum and maximum bounds on the final adjustment percentage, and a default conversion rate to apply when a flight's origin city is not recognized.

The first signal reflects current property occupancy. For each forecast date and room type, retrieve the occupancy figures showing how many rooms are occupied versus available, then incorporate any pre-confirmed group bookings found in the group-booking data files stored alongside the other data. Each group-booking record specifies a date, a room type, and a number of rooms; sum all matching group bookings and add them to the occupied count before dividing by available rooms. Cap this effective occupancy ratio at one. The occupancy score equals the difference between the effective ratio and the baseline from the pricing rules, multiplied by the scale factor.

The second signal captures competitive positioning. Several competitor rate-feed files are available, each containing daily rates organized by date and room type. Collect every competitor rate matching a given forecast date and room type, compute their arithmetic mean, and express the competitor score as the difference between that mean and your base rate divided by the base rate.

The third signal quantifies the influence of local events. For each forecast date, retrieve all scheduled events and compute the event score by summing, for every event on that date, the product of its expected attendance and the impact factor that corresponds to its category as listed in the pricing rules, then dividing by ten thousand.

The fourth signal estimates inbound travel demand. For each forecast date, retrieve all arriving flights and compute the total flight demand by summing, for each flight, the product of its passenger count and the booking-conversion rate for its origin city as found in the origin-profile data files. When an origin city does not appear in any profile, substitute the default conversion rate from the pricing rules. The flight score equals total flight demand divided by the flight-demand divisor.

The fifth signal measures the recent demand trend for each room type. Retrieve all available historical demand records for that room type. Compute the long-term average of net bookings, defined as bookings minus cancellations. Then compute the average net bookings over only the most recent window whose length in days is specified by the pricing rules. The demand-trend score is the fractional difference between the recent average and the long-term average, expressed relative to the long-term average.

Once all five scores are in hand for a given date and room type, multiply each by its respective weight and sum them to obtain the raw demand score. Look up the seasonal factor for the forecast date's calendar month from the seasonal-factors file, and multiply the raw demand score by it. Clamp the resulting adjustment percentage to the minimum and maximum bounds from the pricing rules. The recommended rate is the base rate multiplied by one plus this clamped adjustment percentage, itself then clamped between the floor and ceiling rates found in the rate-boundaries file. Round the recommended rate to two decimal places and the adjustment percentage to four decimal places.

Save the full set of recommendations as `rate_recommendations.csv` with columns `date`, `room_type`, `base_rate`, `recommended_rate`, and `adjustment_pct`, sorted first by date ascending and then alphabetically by room type. Separately, produce `forecast_summary.json` containing a top-level key `room_types` whose value is an object keyed by room type name; each entry should include `avg_recommended_rate` rounded to two decimal places, `avg_adjustment_pct` rounded to four decimal places, `max_rate_date` holding the date string on which that room type achieves its highest recommended rate, and `min_rate_date` holding the date string for the lowest. At the top level of the same JSON, include `total_revenue_projection` equal to the sum over every forecast date and room type of the recommended rate multiplied by that room type's capacity, rounded to two decimal places.

In your final response, state which room type has the highest average adjustment percentage across the entire forecast window and identify the single date on which the total recommended rate summed across all room types is greatest, including both the room type name and the specific date.
