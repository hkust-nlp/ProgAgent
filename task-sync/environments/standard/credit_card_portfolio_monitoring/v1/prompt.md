You are a credit risk analyst tasked with identifying high-risk credit card accounts that warrant proactive credit limit reduction. Your objective is to evaluate every active account in the portfolio by examining four key risk dimensions and producing a prioritized list of accounts for credit limit adjustment.

Begin by reviewing the available configuration and policy documents, which define the complete scoring methodology. These files specify the weights assigned to each risk factor, the threshold values that determine when a factor is triggered, the cutoff score separating high-risk accounts from the rest, tier-based risk multipliers, credit limit reduction rates, and the rules governing limit computation. Pay close attention to any regional adjustment factors, debt-to-income scoring provisions, and the factor priority ranking documented in the configuration.

For each active account in the portfolio, assess the following four risk dimensions over the configured evaluation period. First, examine the account's recent transaction history to calculate the ratio of cash advance amounts to total transaction amounts across all transaction types. If this ratio exceeds the configured threshold, the cash advance risk factor is considered triggered. Second, review the account's credit bureau history to measure how much the credit score has declined from the beginning to the end of the evaluation period. To locate the baseline score, find the credit report whose date falls closest to the start of the evaluation window, which is the configured number of months before the most recent report date. A decline exceeding the configured point threshold triggers the credit score decline factor. Third, analyze the account's monthly payment records to find the longest run of consecutive billing periods in which the cardholder paid only the minimum required amount, meaning the amount paid equals the minimum payment due. If this streak meets or exceeds the configured month threshold, the minimum payment factor is triggered. Fourth, look up the most recent macroeconomic delinquency rate for the account's region and multiply it by one plus the regional unemployment adjustment factor found in the configuration to obtain an adjusted rate. If the adjusted rate exceeds the configured regional delinquency threshold, this factor is triggered.

Each triggered factor contributes its configured weight to the account's raw risk score. On top of these factor scores, consult the account supplementary data to obtain each account's debt-to-income ratio and apply bonus points according to the tiered schedule defined in the scoring configuration. The resulting raw score is then multiplied by the risk multiplier for the account's tier to produce the adjusted risk score.

Any account whose adjusted risk score meets or exceeds the high-risk cutoff should be included in the final list, provided that at least one risk factor was actually triggered, with one critical exception: accounts indicated as enrolled in a hardship program in the supplementary data must be excluded regardless of score. Inactive accounts should not be evaluated at all. For each qualifying account, determine the risk level from the configured score boundaries, compute the recommended credit limit using the tier-specific reduction rate for that risk level, and apply the minimum floor and rounding rules described in the policy documents. Identify the primary risk factor for each account as the triggered factor carrying the highest scoring weight, with ties broken by the factor's position in the priority ranking defined in the scoring configuration where earlier position takes precedence.

Save the final results as `high_risk_accounts.csv` with columns `account_id`, `customer_name`, `current_limit`, `risk_score`, `risk_level`, `recommended_limit`, and `primary_risk_factor`, sorted by `risk_score` in descending order with ties broken by `account_id` in ascending order. Format each `risk_score` as a number rounded to two decimal places, and express `current_limit` and `recommended_limit` as integers.

Once the file is written, state in your response the total number of active accounts evaluated, the number of accounts flagged as high risk, and the count of flagged accounts at each risk level.
