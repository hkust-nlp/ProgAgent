Your organization manages a portfolio of multi-tenant commercial buildings. Each month, utility providers send a single master meter bill for each building covering electricity, gas, and water. Your finance team has been issuing preliminary tenant invoices using a naive equal-share method — splitting the full master meter cost evenly across all tenants regardless of their individual lease terms. You must now produce a corrected chargeback ledger that replaces these preliminary amounts with the charges computed from each tenant's actual lease agreement.

The master meter readings for each building and period are spread across files in the workspace directory with names beginning with `master_meter_`. Each file is a CSV with columns for building identifier, utility type, billing period (in YYYY-MM format), total usage, and unit of measure. The sub-meter readings — recorded only for tenants whose lease specifies individual metering — appear in files beginning with `sub_meter_`, each containing tenant identifier, building identifier, utility type, period, usage, and unit. Common-area allocation parameters for each building and utility are stored in files beginning with `ca_allocation_`, each a JSON array where every building entry lists the fraction of the master meter reading attributable to common areas and the basis by which that common-area cost is shared among tenants (either proportional to leased square footage or divided equally by unit count). Applicable tariff rates and historical billing records can be retrieved through the query interfaces.

For each combination of tenant, utility type, and billing period, determine the correct charge as follows. First retrieve the tenant's lease clause for that utility from the query interface, which specifies one of four billing methods. If the method is "fixed", the tenant pays a flat monthly amount regardless of usage; there is no metered charge and no common-area charge. If the method is "sub_metered", find the tenant's recorded sub-meter usage for that utility and period from the sub-meter files, multiply it by the tariff rate to obtain the metered charge, then compute the tenant's share of the common-area cost: take the common-area fraction from the building's allocation file, multiply it by the building's master meter total for that utility and period and by the tariff rate to get the total common-area cost, then apply the allocation basis — if the basis is "sqft", the tenant's share equals their leased square footage divided by the sum of all tenants' leased square footage in the building, multiplied by the total common-area cost; if the basis is "units", their share equals the total common-area cost divided equally among all tenants in the building. The sum of the metered charge and the common-area charge is the total charge. If the method is "pro_rata_sqft", the total charge equals the tenant's leased square footage divided by the total square footage of all tenants in the building, multiplied by the full master meter usage for that utility and period, multiplied by the tariff rate; there is no separate common-area charge. If the method is "pro_rata_units", the total charge equals one divided by the number of tenants in the building, multiplied by the full master meter usage and the tariff rate; again there is no separate common-area charge.

Once you have computed the total charge for each row, retrieve the original billed amount for that tenant, utility, and period from the billing history query interface. The adjustment is the total charge minus the original billed amount.

Save your completed ledger as `chargeback_ledger.csv` in the workspace. The file must contain exactly these columns in this order: `tenant_id`, `tenant_name`, `building_id`, `utility_type`, `period`, `billing_method`, `metered_charge`, `ca_charge`, `total_charge`, `original_billed`, `adjustment`. All monetary values must be rounded to exactly two decimal places. Rows must be sorted first by `building_id`, then by `tenant_id`, then by `utility_type`, then by `period`, all in ascending order.

In your final response, confirm that the ledger has been saved and identify every tenant whose adjustment exceeds five percent of their original billed amount in absolute terms, stating each such tenant's identifier and name.
