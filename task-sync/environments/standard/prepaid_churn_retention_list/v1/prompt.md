You have been brought in to support the retention operations team at a prepaid mobile carrier. The company has noticed rising churn among its prepaid subscriber base over the past quarter and wants to proactively reach out to those most likely to leave. Your job is to analyze the full customer base, compute a risk score for every eligible subscriber, and produce a prioritized retention call list that the outreach team can work through in order.

Several data retrieval functions are available for querying subscriber records, monthly call-detail summaries, top-up transaction histories, and customer service interaction logs. These cover the quantitative side of the analysis. In addition, you will find configuration files that define the scoring methodology you must follow exactly, including the weights assigned to each risk component, the threshold brackets that map raw metrics into component scores, and the boundaries that determine each subscriber's risk tier. A separate file provides the mapping from each combination of primary risk factor and plan type to the specific retention offer to recommend. Detailed analysis guidelines are also provided, covering edge-case handling, period definitions, and tiebreaker rules; read them carefully before starting any calculations.

Post-interaction survey responses have been collected from a sample of subscribers and are stored as structured files alongside the configuration. Each survey response includes the subscriber's identifier, a self-reported likelihood-to-switch rating on a one-to-ten scale, and optionally the name of a competitor the subscriber is considering. You will also find competitive intelligence reports describing active promotional campaigns from rival carriers; these document which regions and plan types each campaign targets. Both survey responses and competitive campaign data must factor into the churn-intent component of the risk score as described in the guidelines.

To score each subscriber, compute four risk components. The first is usage decline, computed from the percentage change in total voice call volume between the recent and historical periods. The second is top-up decline, an analogous comparison of total top-up amounts across the same periods. The third is sentiment, derived from the average sentiment score across service interactions with a penalty applied when any interaction was escalated. The fourth is churn intent, combining survey switching-likelihood ratings, competitor mentions in surveys, port-out request history from interactions, and whether any competitor campaign targets the subscriber's region and plan type. Each component is mapped to a zero-to-one score via the configured threshold brackets, then multiplied by the corresponding weight and summed to produce the overall risk score.

Only subscribers whose account status appears in the eligible list and whose account tenure meets the minimum-days requirement specified in the classification rules should be included in the analysis. After scoring, place each eligible subscriber into a risk tier using the tier boundaries from the classification rules. The final call list must include only subscribers falling in the two highest tiers. Sort the call list first by risk score in descending order and then by monthly average revenue per user in descending order as a tiebreaker.

Save the call list as `retention_call_list.csv` containing the following columns in this exact order: `subscriber_id`, `name`, `phone`, `region`, `plan_type`, `risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `usage_decline_pct`, `topup_decline_pct`, `avg_sentiment`, `churn_intent_score`, `primary_risk_factor`, `recommended_offer`. Round risk scores and churn intent scores to four decimal places. Round percentage values and average sentiment to two decimal places. The primary risk factor is the component whose raw score before weighting is highest; ties are broken by preferring in order usage_decline, then churn_intent, then negative_sentiment, then topup_decline. Look up the recommended offer in the offer matrix using the primary risk factor and the subscriber's plan type.

In your final response, state the total number of subscribers on the call list, provide how many fall into each of the two included risk tiers, give a count of at-risk subscribers broken down by region, and list the five subscribers with the highest risk scores showing each one's subscriber identifier, risk score, and risk tier.
