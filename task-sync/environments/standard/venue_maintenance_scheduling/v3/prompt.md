You are responsible for producing a comprehensive venue maintenance schedule and utilization report for a regional sports authority that manages multiple venues across several districts. The planning period covers the thirty calendar days from the first of May 2026 through the thirtieth of May 2026, inclusive.

Begin by gathering complete information about every venue in the system, including its name, district, surface type, and category designation. Venues whose surface type is listed under the excluded types in the maintenance policy configuration should be omitted from maintenance consideration entirely, though they must still appear in the utilization report. For each non-excluded venue, retrieve all historical pitch wear inspection records and identify the single most recent inspection per venue based on inspection date, breaking ties by the highest record identifier. The wear index from that most recent inspection is the starting point for assessing maintenance need.

Separately, consult the maintenance log files to determine whether each venue received any maintenance within the lookback window specified by the policy. If a venue had maintenance performed during that window, reduce its effective wear index by the reduction amount stated in the policy, but never below zero. This adjusted wear index determines which maintenance type applies according to the thresholds in the maintenance policy configuration: check from the most intensive type downward, and the first threshold the adjusted value meets or exceeds dictates the maintenance type. Venues whose adjusted wear index falls below all thresholds do not require maintenance and should be excluded from the schedule.

Once the set of venues requiring maintenance is established, compute a priority score for each one. The formula and rounding precision are described in the priority rules configuration. To compute the urgency factor, find the earliest fixture date at each venue within the planning window. The number of days from the start of the planning period to that earliest fixture determines which urgency band applies, as defined in the policy. Venues with no upcoming fixture in the window fall into the lowest urgency tier. Multiply the adjusted wear index by the venue category multiplier from the policy and by the urgency factor, then round to the precision specified in the priority rules.

Schedule all maintenance tasks in descending order of priority score, using ascending venue identifier to break ties. For each task, find the earliest calendar day within the planning window that satisfies every constraint simultaneously: the day must not coincide with any fixture at that venue, must not fall within the blocked window of any confirmed third-party event at that venue where the blocked window spans from the event date minus setup days through the event date plus teardown days inclusive, and the forecasted precipitation for the venue's district on that day must not exceed the maximum allowed by the policy. Tentative events should be disregarded when computing blocked dates. On the candidate day there must also be at least one eligible staff member available. A staff member is eligible if their skill level meets or exceeds the requirement for the maintenance type, they hold the required certification when one is specified, they have a shift with available status on that date, and they have not already been assigned to another maintenance task on that same day. Among all eligible candidates, assign the one with the numerically lowest staff identifier.

Save the resulting schedule as `maintenance_schedule.csv` with columns in this exact order: `date`, `venue_id`, `venue_name`, `maintenance_type`, `priority_score`, `assigned_staff_id`, `assigned_staff_name`, `estimated_hours`, `reason`. The reason field should contain the adjusted wear index and category for each entry, formatted as `adjusted_wear=XX.X, category=Y`. Rows must be sorted by date ascending, with ties broken by priority score descending.

On top of this, produce a utilization report covering every venue in the system regardless of surface type. For each venue, count the number of distinct fixture days, the number of distinct days blocked by confirmed third-party events including their setup and teardown windows, and the number of maintenance days you scheduled. Compute available days as thirty minus the sum of those three counts, flooring at zero. Calculate the utilization percentage as the sum of fixture, event, and maintenance days divided by thirty, multiplied by one hundred, rounded to one decimal place. Save this as `utilization_report.csv` with columns: `venue_id`, `venue_name`, `category`, `fixture_days`, `event_days`, `maintenance_days`, `available_days`, `utilization_pct`, sorted by venue identifier ascending.

Finally, in your response state the total number of maintenance tasks scheduled, the number of venues requiring deep restoration, the name and priority score of the highest-priority venue, and the total staff hours allocated across all tasks.
