You are the head of football operations for a top-tier club preparing for the upcoming transfer window. Your task is to produce a prioritized shortlist of transfer targets by cross-referencing multiple data sources and applying the club's transfer policy rules precisely.

Begin by examining the squad depth chart files, which detail each position's current staffing levels, quality ratings, tactical fit scores, and an urgency classification. Each position carries an urgency label — critical, high, medium, or low — reflecting how desperately the club needs reinforcement there. Separately, review the fixture calendar files to determine how many months in the upcoming season qualify as "congested." A month is congested when the number of scheduled fixtures in that month meets or exceeds the threshold defined in the transfer policy configuration. If the total count of congested months meets or exceeds the trigger value specified in the congestion rules section of the transfer policy, then every position currently rated as medium urgency must be promoted to high urgency. After applying this adjustment, the positions that qualify as priority are those whose final urgency is either critical or high — these are the only positions for which you will identify transfer targets.

For each priority position, search for available players at that position whose average overall scouting score meets the minimum threshold specified in the eligibility section of the transfer policy configuration. For every candidate returned, retrieve their full details — age, wage demands, market value, contract expiry year, and current club — and their individual scouting reports. A candidate is eligible only if their age does not exceed the position-specific maximum age cap defined in the policy, and their weekly wage demand does not exceed the wage ceiling. Candidates failing either check must be excluded entirely.

Once you have the eligible candidates' scouting data from the tools, you must also look up each candidate's supplementary profile in the scouting dossier files. These dossier files contain attributes not available through the scouting report tools: injury risk, attitude grade, adaptability, consistency, and leadership scores. Both the tool-provided scouting scores and the dossier attributes are required to compute a weighted composite score. The scoring weights configuration file specifies the weight for each component and explains how to convert raw values. Scouting scores obtained from tools are on a zero-to-one-hundred scale and must be divided by ten before applying the weights. Injury risk is inverted so that lower risk produces a higher component score. Attitude grades are mapped to numeric values according to the mapping table in the scoring weights file. All other dossier attributes are already on a one-to-ten scale. The weighted composite score is the sum of each component value multiplied by its weight. Any candidate whose weighted composite score falls below the minimum weighted composite score threshold from the eligibility rules — noting that the threshold in the policy file is expressed on the same zero-to-one-hundred scale as raw scouting scores and must be divided by ten for comparison — must also be excluded.

After filtering, rank the remaining candidates within each priority position by their weighted composite score in descending order, using alphabetical player name as the tiebreaker. Retain at most the top number of targets per position as specified by the shortlist configuration in the transfer policy. Then combine all retained candidates across all priority positions into a single list, sorted globally by weighted composite score descending with the same alphabetical tiebreaker, and assign sequential ranks starting from one.

For each shortlisted player, compute a recommended bid range. The bid calculation section of the transfer policy defines a base multiplier applied to the player's market value, a per-year contract premium based on how many years remain on the player's contract beyond 2026, a per-year age discount applied for every year the player's age exceeds the age threshold, and an urgency premium that varies by the position's final urgency level. The low end of the bid is computed as market value times the base multiplier times the quantity one plus the contract premium times years remaining minus the age discount times years over threshold plus the urgency premium, with a floor of 0.50. The high end is the low bid multiplied by the high range factor. Both bid values should be rounded to two decimal places.

To understand the club's spending power, retrieve the financial projections for the budget year specified in the policy. The available transfer budget equals the total revenue minus the sum of all expense categories listed in the budget section of the policy.

Save the final shortlist as `transfer_shortlist.csv` with the following columns in this exact order: `rank`, `player_name`, `position`, `age`, `current_club`, `weighted_score`, `bid_low_m`, `bid_high_m`, `position_urgency`. Each row should correspond to one shortlisted player in rank order.

In your final response, state which positions were identified as priorities and why, report the number of congested months and whether the medium-to-high urgency boost was applied, state the available transfer budget, and then present the complete ranked shortlist showing each player's rank, name, position, age, weighted composite score, and bid range. Conclude with a top recommendation for each priority position, naming the single highest-scoring candidate and their bid range.
