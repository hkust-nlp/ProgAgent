You have been tasked with performing a comprehensive chain-of-custody reconciliation audit for controlled substance shipments. A regulatory body requires a complete review of all recorded shipments to identify any discrepancies, custody gaps, or compliance violations that warrant investigation.

Your working directory contains several categories of supporting materials. Shipment manifest files list each shipment with its substance, quantity, DEA schedule classification, origin and destination facilities, assigned carrier, and departure timing. Route planning documents detail the expected checkpoint sequence for each shipment based on its route type, along with summary GPS telemetry counts and the zones actually observed during transit. Carrier assignment logs describe which carriers were assigned to which shipments, including DOT numbers. Facility inspection summaries provide background on each facility, including identifiers and DEA numbers. Configuration files define the recognized discrepancy categories and their severity classifications, recommended enforcement actions tied to each severity level, carrier compliance rules specifying which schedules require hazmat-endorsed carriers and whether DEA registration and controlled-substance certification are mandatory, geofence zone boundaries expressed as latitude and longitude ranges per zone, and DEA schedule-specific handling requirements including maximum transit hours and form requirements.

For every shipment in the manifests, you must retrieve the associated DEA Form 222 record to compare the authorized quantity and the schedule classification on the form against the manifest values. You must also verify the license status, expiry date, and schedule authorizations of both the origin and destination facilities as of the shipment's departure date. The GPS tracking log for each shipment must be obtained and examined: compare the set of zone names appearing in the GPS data against the required checkpoints listed in the route planning documents for that shipment; any required checkpoint zone that does not appear among the GPS log entries constitutes a custody gap. Separately, any GPS ping that carries a geofence status of "outside" indicates a geofence boundary violation. Carrier compliance must also be checked by retrieving the carrier's certification details and confirming that the carrier's certification has not expired before the departure date, that DEA registration and controlled-substance certification are both current, and that a hazmat endorsement is present when the regulatory configuration requires one for the shipment's schedule.

A quantity mismatch arises when the DEA Form 222 authorized quantity differs from the manifest's shipped quantity. A schedule mismatch occurs when the schedule recorded on the DEA form differs from the manifest's DEA schedule. A DEA form expiration is flagged when the form's expiry date falls before the shipment's departure date. An expired facility license flag is raised when either the origin or destination facility has a license status other than "active" or when the facility's license expiry date precedes the departure date. An unauthorized schedule flag applies when a facility's authorized schedule list does not include the shipment's DEA schedule classification. A custody gap means one or more required route checkpoints are absent from the GPS zone data for that shipment. A geofence violation means at least one GPS ping was recorded with a status of "outside." Carrier noncompliance covers any combination of expired carrier certification, missing hazmat endorsement for schedules that require it, absent DEA registration, or absent controlled-substance certification.

Each discrepancy should be recorded with the following columns: `shipment_id`, `manifest_number`, `substance_name`, `dea_schedule`, `discrepancy_type`, `severity`, `recommended_action`, and `detail`. The `discrepancy_type` value must exactly match one of these labels: `QUANTITY_MISMATCH`, `SCHEDULE_MISMATCH`, `DEA_FORM_EXPIRED`, `EXPIRED_FACILITY_LICENSE`, `UNAUTHORIZED_SCHEDULE`, `CUSTODY_GAP`, `GEOFENCE_VIOLATION`, `CARRIER_NONCOMPLIANT`. The severity and recommended action for each discrepancy type are defined in the regulatory configuration file. If a single shipment triggers the same discrepancy type more than once, for example when both the origin and destination facilities have expired licenses, record only one entry for that shipment-and-type combination, using whichever instance was encountered first.

Save the final audit roster as `audit_roster.csv`. The rows must be sorted first by severity in descending order of urgency, with critical entries appearing before high and high before medium. Within the same severity tier, sort by `shipment_id` in ascending lexicographic order. As a final tiebreaker within identical severity and shipment, sort by `discrepancy_type` in ascending alphabetical order.

Once the roster is written, provide a textual summary that states the total number of shipments reviewed, the number of unique shipments flagged with at least one discrepancy, and a per-type breakdown listing how many roster entries were recorded for each discrepancy type, referring to each type by its exact label.
