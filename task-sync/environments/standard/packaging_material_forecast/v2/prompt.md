You are the procurement planner for a subscription box company that ships curated packages to subscribers across multiple regions. The company offers several plan tiers, and each plan tier corresponds to different box configurations that require specific packaging materials. Your objective is to produce a complete purchase requisition for all packaging materials needed for the upcoming quarter, accounting for projected subscriber growth, existing assembly commitments, current stock levels, and supplier economics.

Begin by examining the configuration files available to you. One file specifies the date boundaries of the next quarter and serves as your planning horizon. A policy document describes the procurement rules you must follow, including the safety stock percentage that must be added on top of projected demand, the number of buffer days to subtract from the earliest need date when calculating order placement dates, the minimum reliability threshold for qualifying suppliers, and how order quantities should be rounded relative to each supplier's minimum order quantity. A separate document classifies material categories into priority and standard tiers, each associated with a different buffer-day value from the policy.

Once you understand the planning parameters, gather subscriber growth projections from the available forecast files. Each forecast record provides a growth rate percentage for a particular region and plan combination. Separately, retrieve the current active subscriber counts for every plan type through the available data retrieval interface, which will return counts broken down by region. For each plan type, compute a single weighted average growth rate by weighting each region's forecast rate by that region's active subscriber count, then dividing by the plan's total active subscribers.

Next, enumerate every box type in the system. Each box type is linked to a plan type. For each box type, retrieve its assembly schedule entries that fall within the next quarter and keep only those with a confirmed status. For every confirmed assembly, retrieve the bill of materials for that box type, which lists the packaging materials and the quantity of each material required per box. Multiply each confirmed assembly's planned quantity by the factor one plus the weighted growth rate (expressed as a decimal) for the corresponding plan type, rounding the result up to the nearest integer, to obtain the growth-adjusted assembly quantity. Then, for each material appearing in any bill of materials, sum the product of the adjusted quantity and the per-box quantity across all relevant assemblies to arrive at the total projected demand for that material.

Apply the safety stock percentage from the procurement policy: multiply each material's total demand by one plus the safety stock percentage divided by one hundred, and round the result up to the nearest integer. This is the gross requirement.

To determine the effective inventory for each material, retrieve its on-hand and reserved quantities from the inventory system, then consult the inventory adjustment files for any recorded damaged or expired quantities. Effective inventory equals on-hand minus reserved minus damaged minus expired, but never less than zero. Subtract effective inventory from the gross requirement to obtain the net requirement. Materials whose net requirement is zero or negative do not need to be ordered.

For every material that does need ordering, look up all suppliers capable of providing it. Discard any supplier that is inactive or whose reliability score falls below the policy's minimum threshold. For each remaining candidate, find that supplier's negotiated discount percentage from the contract terms files, then compute the effective unit cost as the list price multiplied by one minus the discount percentage divided by one hundred, rounded to two decimal places. Select the supplier with the lowest effective unit cost. If two suppliers tie on cost, prefer the one with the shorter lead time. If still tied, prefer the lower supplier identifier. If no supplier qualifies, exclude that material from the requisition.

Determine the order quantity by rounding the net requirement up to the nearest whole multiple of the chosen supplier's minimum order quantity. Calculate the order placement date by taking the earliest confirmed assembly date that uses the material, then subtracting the supplier's lead time in days and the applicable buffer days. Use the priority buffer if the material's category is classified as priority, otherwise use the standard buffer. The expected delivery date equals the order date plus the supplier's lead time.

Compile the results into a file named `purchase_requisition.csv` with the following columns in this exact order: `material_id`, `material_name`, `supplier_id`, `supplier_name`, `order_quantity`, `unit_cost`, `total_cost`, `order_date`, `expected_delivery_date`. The unit cost column should hold the effective cost after discount, rounded to two decimal places. The total cost should be the order quantity multiplied by the unit cost, also rounded to two decimal places. Dates must be in YYYY-MM-DD format. Sort the rows first by order date ascending, then by material identifier ascending.

In your final response, state the total number of distinct materials requiring purchase in the format "Total materials to order: N", report the sum of all total cost values as "Grand total cost: $X.XX" with exactly two decimal places, and include both "Earliest order date: YYYY-MM-DD" and "Latest order date: YYYY-MM-DD" drawn from the requisition you produced.
