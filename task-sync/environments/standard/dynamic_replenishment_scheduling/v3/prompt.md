You are supporting warehouse operations at a large distribution center that manages numerous SKUs across multiple temperature-controlled storage zones. Your objective is to produce a prioritized replenishment task list for the stocking team, identifying every pick face at risk of running low and estimating the time savings achieved by acting proactively rather than waiting for stockouts.

Begin by reviewing the configuration files available in your working directory. A YAML policy document defines stock-percentage thresholds below which a pick face must be flagged for replenishment; these thresholds differ by storage type (ambient, chilled, frozen). The same document specifies how to weight the four factors that determine each task's priority: urgency based on time until the pick face is depleted, demand intensity, a penalty factor tied to storage-temperature requirements, and whether an inbound dock shipment is already en route for that product. It also specifies a target fill percentage indicating how full each pick face should be restored to, a minimum battery percentage below which a forklift may not be assigned work, and a dock-proximity window defining the number of hours within which an arriving shipment is considered close enough to reduce that SKU's replenishment priority.

A JSON file specifies four time standards used for all duration estimates: the per-case handling rate under planned (proactive) conditions, the higher per-case rate that applies when replenishment happens reactively after a stockout, a base travel time per trip, and a per-incident idle penalty representing the average downtime incurred by order pickers when a pick face runs empty and they must wait for emergency restocking.

A CSV file maps each zone in the facility to its storage type, a travel-time multiplier, and a congestion multiplier reflecting current aisle-traffic conditions. A separate plain-text roster file lists every forklift along with its home zone and an efficiency rating; this rating should be used as a divisor on the per-case handling time so that a lower efficiency value results in longer per-case durations.

A series of additional CSV files contain demand-adjustment multipliers, one multiplier per SKU. Every SKU in the system appears in exactly one of these files. The multiplier must be applied to the base average picking rate obtained from the query tools to yield a demand-adjusted rate that reflects current ordering patterns. You must read every one of these files to assemble a complete mapping before proceeding with calculations.

Using the available query tools, first retrieve the list of active zone identifiers. Then, for each zone, obtain the pick-face stock records page by page. For every pick face whose current stock percentage — computed as current quantity divided by maximum capacity, expressed as a percentage — falls below the threshold that corresponds to the zone's storage type, you must gather two additional pieces of information through the tools: the SKU's case pack quantity (from the SKU detail lookup) and the historical average picking rate (from the picking-rate lookup). Compute the demand-adjusted picking rate by multiplying the average units per hour by the demand multiplier read from the CSV files. Hours until depletion equals the current quantity divided by this adjusted rate; if the adjusted rate is zero or negative treat depletion as 999 hours. Cases needed to restore the pick face equals the ceiling of the difference between the target quantity (maximum capacity times the target fill percentage divided by one hundred) and the current quantity, divided by the case pack quantity, with a floor of one case.

Query the dock schedule to identify any shipments whose status is "scheduled" or "in_transit" and whose ETA in hours is strictly less than the dock-proximity window and whose SKU matches a flagged pick face. For every such match the dock-proximity factor is zero; for all other flagged pick faces the factor is one.

Retrieve the most recent forklift telemetry for each zone. Within each zone the forklift assigned to that zone's replenishment tasks is the one whose latest telemetry shows a status of "idle" and a battery percentage greater than or equal to the minimum battery threshold; when multiple forklifts qualify, choose the one with the highest battery. If no forklift in a zone qualifies, record the assignment as NONE. A single forklift handles all tasks in its zone.

Compute each task's priority score as the sum of four weighted terms. The first is the urgency weight multiplied by the ratio of that task's reciprocal depletion hours to the maximum reciprocal depletion hours observed across all flagged tasks. The second is the demand weight multiplied by the ratio of that task's adjusted picking rate to the maximum adjusted rate. The third is the storage-penalty weight multiplied by the ratio of the SKU's storage-type factor (from the policy document) to the highest storage-type factor defined there. The fourth is the dock-proximity weight multiplied by the dock-proximity factor. Rank all tasks in descending order of this composite score, breaking ties by ascending SKU identifier.

For each task, the estimated replenishment time in minutes equals the base travel time multiplied by the zone's travel-time multiplier and its congestion multiplier, plus the number of cases needed multiplied by the proactive per-case rate divided by the assigned forklift's efficiency rating (use one as the efficiency when no forklift is assigned). The time saving in minutes equals the number of cases needed multiplied by the difference between the reactive and proactive per-case rates, divided by the same efficiency value, plus the idle penalty.

Save the completed plan as replenishment_plan.csv with these columns in exactly this order: priority_rank, sku_id, pick_face_id, zone_id, current_stock_pct, hours_until_depletion, cases_needed, assigned_forklift, estimated_replenish_time_min, time_saving_min. Values in priority_rank should be integers starting at one. Round current_stock_pct to one decimal place, hours_until_depletion to two decimal places, and both estimated_replenish_time_min and time_saving_min to two decimal places.

In your final response, state the total number of replenishment tasks generated, the total estimated time savings across all tasks rounded to two decimal places, and list the SKU identifiers of the three highest-priority tasks in rank order.
