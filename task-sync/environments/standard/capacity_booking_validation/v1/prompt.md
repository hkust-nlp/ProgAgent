A natural gas pipeline operator needs to validate and finalize capacity bookings for an upcoming nomination cycle. Shippers have submitted reservation requests across various pipeline segments, and each request must be evaluated against current operational conditions before capacity can be allocated. All of the relevant operational policies, reservation data, equipment condition records, and any regulatory constraints have been organized and made available for review.

The allocation process begins by identifying every pending reservation and gathering the operational data needed to assess each pipeline segment involved. The physical capacity of a segment is obtained through direct lookup, but some segments may also be subject to regulatory capacity limits that fall below their physical maximum. Whenever a regulatory limit exists for a segment, the lower of the physical capacity and the regulatory limit serves as the baseline capacity for that segment.

Maintenance activities affect the available capacity on a segment. For the nomination date specified in the operational policy, any scheduled maintenance events that overlap with that date reduce capacity. Each maintenance event carries a base reduction percentage, but the actual impact depends on the condition of the equipment involved, captured as an equipment age factor specific to each segment. The adjusted reduction for a single maintenance event equals the base reduction percentage multiplied by the equipment age factor. When multiple maintenance events affect the same segment, their adjusted reductions are summed. The operational policy specifies a cap on the combined maintenance reduction to prevent it from exceeding a physically meaningful limit.

Weather conditions introduce a further constraint. Each segment belongs to a region, and weather forecasts provide a severity level for the nomination date. The operational policy defines a mapping from each integer severity level to a percentage reduction in capacity. This weather adjustment is applied after the maintenance reduction.

Flow volatility also plays a role. Historical flow data yields a volatility index for each segment. The operational policy defines thresholds that classify a segment into a low, moderate, or high volatility tier, and each tier carries a corresponding safety margin percentage. This volatility safety margin is applied after the weather adjustment. The effective segment capacity is the result of applying all three reductions sequentially to the baseline capacity, each as a multiplicative factor of the form one minus the fractional reduction, rounded to two decimal places after the final multiplication.

Once effective capacities are established, reservations must be ordered for allocation. The operational policy assigns a numeric rank to each priority flag and to each contract tier. The primary sort criterion is the priority flag rank in ascending order, so that emergency nominations are served before all others regardless of tier. Among nominations sharing the same priority flag rank, they are ordered by contract tier rank ascending, meaning firm shippers precede interruptible shippers who precede spot shippers. Within the same priority flag rank and contract tier, critical nominations come before normal ones. If priority flag, tier, and criticality are all the same, shippers with higher reliability scores are served first. As a final tiebreaker, lower reservation identifiers take precedence.

Allocation proceeds greedily for each segment independently. Working through the reservations in the priority order described above, each reservation receives the lesser of its nominated volume and the remaining effective capacity for that segment. After each allocation the remaining capacity is decremented and rounded to two decimal places. A reservation that receives its full nominated volume is marked as confirmed, one that receives a partial amount is marked as curtailed, and one that receives nothing is marked as rejected.

The finalized allocation must be saved as `capacity_allocation_report.csv` with the following columns in exactly this order: `segment_id`, `segment_name`, `reservation_id`, `shipper_id`, `shipper_name`, `contract_tier`, `reliability_score`, `priority_flag`, `nominated_volume_mmbtu`, `allocated_capacity_mmbtu`, `allocation_status`, `effective_segment_capacity_mmbtu`. Rows must be sorted by segment identifier ascending, and within each segment by the allocation priority order described above. All numeric capacity and volume values must use two decimal places. Reliability scores must retain one decimal place.

After producing the report, provide a summary stating the total number of segments processed, the total number of reservations handled, how many were confirmed, how many were curtailed, how many were rejected, the total nominated volume across all reservations, the total allocated capacity, and the overall utilization rate expressed as the total allocated capacity divided by the sum of effective segment capacities across all segments that had reservations, formatted as a percentage rounded to two decimal places.
