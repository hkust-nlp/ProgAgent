Your organization manages a global network of distributor partners, each operating under territory-specific license agreements that govern the royalty rates applied to product shipments. Over time, discrepancies have emerged between the royalties distributors report and what the agreements and shipment volumes actually require. Your job is to perform a comprehensive licensing revenue leakage analysis across every active distributor, incorporating third-party quantity verification data and outstanding audit findings, and to produce a prioritized compliance review list.

Begin by reading the configuration files that define the parameters for this analysis. A YAML file specifies the royalty rate modifier for each territory, which must be applied as a multiplier when computing expected royalties. A separate YAML file lays out the risk scoring policy, including the weights assigned to five scoring dimensions (leakage amount, leakage percentage, audit severity, volume gap, and quantity discrepancy), the severity point values for each audit exception grade (critical, high, medium, low), the risk category thresholds, and the normalization caps for each dimension. A JSON configuration file sets the minimum leakage thresholds (both an absolute dollar amount and a percentage floor), the fiscal year date boundaries, and the review priority cutoff score.

Gather information on all active distributor partners through the available retrieval interface. For each active distributor, pull their license agreements, retrieve every shipment record they have on file, and collect their audit exception history. Shipment records include a reported royalty figure and reference a product SKU by its internal identifier; you will need to look up each distinct SKU to obtain its unit price and base royalty percentage. Agreement records specify the product category, territory, date range, minimum annual volume commitment, royalty discount percentage, and current status.

For each shipment that falls within the fiscal year window defined in the configuration, compute the expected royalty as follows: multiply the shipment quantity by the SKU unit price, then by the SKU base royalty percentage, then by the territory modifier for the shipment's territory. If the distributor holds an active agreement covering that territory and product category whose date range spans the shipment date, reduce the expected royalty by the agreement's royalty discount percentage. Separately, consult the CSV verification reports found alongside the configuration files; these third-party records list a verified quantity for shipments identified by invoice number. Wherever the verified quantity exceeds the quantity on file, use the verified quantity in place of the recorded quantity when computing expected royalty, since the higher figure reflects actual goods shipped.

Once expected and reported royalties are tallied for each distributor, determine total leakage as the non-negative difference between expected and reported totals. Compute leakage percentage as leakage divided by expected total. Exclude any distributor whose leakage falls below both the absolute and percentage thresholds defined in the compliance configuration.

For every remaining distributor, count the number of shipments where the verified quantity differs from the recorded quantity. Calculate the average volume gap across all of the distributor's active agreements that overlap the fiscal year: for each such agreement, if the distributor's total shipped quantity for the matching territory and product category is less than the agreement's minimum annual volume, the gap fraction is the shortfall divided by the committed volume, otherwise it is zero; average these fractions.

Compute the audit severity component from the distributor's unresolved audit exceptions. For each unresolved exception, map its severity to the point value specified in the scoring policy, then take the mean across all unresolved exceptions; if the distributor has none, the component is zero.

Calculate each of the five normalized component scores by dividing the raw value by its normalization cap and multiplying by one hundred, capping at one hundred. The final risk score is the weighted sum of the five component scores using the weights from the scoring policy. Assign a risk category by checking the score against the category thresholds in descending order: Critical, High, Medium, or Low. Identify the primary risk factor as the scoring dimension whose weighted contribution (weight times component score) is largest.

Save a file called `leakage_analysis.csv` containing one row per flagged distributor with the columns `partner_id`, `partner_name`, `territory`, `total_expected_royalty`, `total_reported_royalty`, `leakage_amount`, `leakage_pct`, `quantity_discrepancies`, `risk_score`, and `risk_category`. Monetary values must be rounded to two decimal places, leakage percentage to four decimal places, and risk score to two decimal places. Rows must be sorted by risk score in descending order, breaking ties by ascending partner ID.

Save a second file called `high_risk_summary.csv` containing only those distributors categorized as Critical or High, with columns `partner_id`, `partner_name`, `risk_score`, `risk_category`, and `primary_risk_factor`, in the same sort order.

In your final response, state the total number of high-risk partners identified as Critical or High and list each by name with its risk score.
