You are a recruitment analytics specialist tasked with producing a quarterly budget allocation plan for the upcoming quarter, Q1 2026. The organization uses several recruitment channels to fill positions across multiple departments, and historical data spanning four quarters of 2025 is available for analysis. Your goal is to correlate each channel's effectiveness with hiring outcomes and produce an optimized spend recommendation grounded in quality-of-hire metrics.

Begin by identifying every recruitment source and every department currently tracked in the system. For each combination of source and department, you must gather four categories of data through the available tools: hiring volume (total candidates and hires), average time from application to hire in days, first-year retention rate at the twelve-month mark, and average internal hiring cost covering recruiter time, interview panel time, and administrative overhead. Internal cost figures returned by the tools do not include external channel fees, which are defined separately in a configuration file that describes fee structures for job-board, referral, and agency-type sources along with average salaries per department. You will also need the number of hires from each source in the most recent quarter, 2025-Q4, since posting-fee allocation for job-board sources depends on that figure.

Separately, several batches of data files contain complementary information that cannot be obtained through tools. Interview score records list technical, behavioral, and cultural-fit ratings on a one-to-five scale for every interviewed candidate, labeled by source and department. Post-hire manager review records carry a manager satisfaction rating, also one-to-five, per hired candidate and source-department pair. Onboarding metric records report a completion percentage per hired candidate. You must read all of these files, aggregate the scores by source and department, and fold them into the quality-of-hire calculation.

A formula configuration file specifies the weights for five quality-of-hire sub-scores: retention rate, time-to-hire efficiency, interview quality, manager satisfaction, and onboarding success. It also describes how each sub-score is normalized. Retention rate is used directly as a zero-to-one value. Time-to-hire efficiency is computed as the greater of zero and one minus the average days-to-hire divided by sixty. Interview quality is the average of the three interview dimension means divided by five. Manager satisfaction is the average satisfaction rating divided by five. Onboarding success is the average completion percentage divided by one hundred. The composite quality score for each source-department pair is the weighted sum of these five normalized sub-scores.

Once you have the quality scores and total cost per hire (internal cost plus external channel fee), follow the budget allocation policy document to compute the final plan. That document describes an efficiency-weighted method: each pair's efficiency score equals its quality score divided by its total cost per hire, scaled by ten thousand; the raw allocation weight is the efficiency score multiplied by the historical hire count for that pair across all quarters; weights are normalized into percentages; per-source percentage totals are clamped to a minimum of eight percent and a maximum of thirty-five percent of the total budget, with excess or deficit redistributed proportionally among unconstrained sources; and each source's adjusted total is split back to its departments in proportion to the original department-level raw weights. The total quarterly budget is specified in a targets file. Final allocated budgets are rounded to the nearest whole dollar, and expected hires are the allocated budget divided by total cost per hire, rounded to one decimal place.

Save the budget plan as `budget_allocation.csv` with columns `source`, `department`, `allocated_budget`, `expected_hires`, and `cost_per_hire_estimate`. Save the quality-of-hire metrics as `quality_kpis.csv` with columns `source`, `department`, `avg_time_to_hire_days`, `retention_rate`, and `quality_score`. In your final response, summarize the allocation across all sources, call out which channel demonstrates the highest and lowest overall quality of hire, and provide a brief recommendation on where to increase or reduce investment.
