Your firm is undertaking a comprehensive review of consultant utilization and project profitability across the current engagement portfolio. The analysis period covers a fixed number of weeks defined in the utilization configuration file available to you. Several categories of supporting information are at your disposal: capacity tracking records that detail each consultant's weekly target hours, billing rate schedules organized by client tier and consultant role with potential client-specific negotiated overrides, project requirement documents listing the skills each engagement demands along with minimum proficiency thresholds, and policy documents governing utilization classification thresholds, over-servicing detection rules, skill demand prioritization weights, and reallocation constraints.

Begin by retrieving every consultant's profile, including their internal hourly cost rate, and the complete set of timesheet entries for the analysis period. Cross-reference each consultant's total logged hours against the sum of their weekly target hours drawn from the capacity tracking records. Compute a utilization rate as total hours worked divided by total available hours, and classify each consultant by comparing this rate against the underutilization and overutilization thresholds specified in the utilization policy configuration. Produce a file named `utilization_report.csv` containing the following columns in this exact order: `consultant_id`, `consultant_name`, `role`, `department`, `total_hours_worked`, `total_available_hours`, `utilization_rate`, `status`. Express total_hours_worked and total_available_hours rounded to one decimal place, utilization_rate as a decimal rounded to four decimal places, and status as exactly one of `underutilized`, `normal`, or `overutilized`. Sort rows by consultant_id in ascending order.

Separately, evaluate each project's financial performance. For every timesheet entry on a project, determine the labor cost as the hours multiplied by the consultant's internal hourly cost rate, and the revenue as the hours multiplied by the applicable billing rate whenever the entry is billable. The applicable billing rate depends on the consultant's role and the project's client: check the client rate schedule files first for a custom negotiated rate for that role, and if the client uses standard pricing, fall back to the tier-based rates found in the billing rate tier schedule using the client's assigned billing tier. Sum costs and revenues across all entries to obtain each project's totals, then compute the margin percentage as one hundred times the quantity total revenue minus total cost, divided by total revenue. If a project has zero total revenue, record its margin percentage as zero. Mark a project as over-serviced whenever its total labor cost exceeds its total budget by more than the percentage threshold specified in the over-servicing rules configuration. Write the results to `profitability_analysis.csv` with columns in this order: `project_id`, `project_name`, `client_name`, `total_cost`, `total_revenue`, `total_budget`, `margin_pct`, `overservicing_flag`. Round total_cost, total_revenue, and total_budget to two decimal places, margin_pct to two decimal places, and express overservicing_flag as `True` or `False`. Sort by project_id in ascending order.

Once you have identified which consultants are underutilized and which active projects exist, construct a reallocation proposal aimed at improving margins by redirecting underutilized consultants toward active engagements where they can contribute. For each consultant classified as underutilized, retrieve their skill profile and identify active projects whose skill requirements overlap with skills the consultant holds at or above the minimum proficiency level stated in the project requirement documents. Among eligible target projects, give priority to those flagged as over-serviced, since substituting a lower-cost underutilized resource may reduce overall project costs. Adhere to every constraint in the reallocation policy document: respect the maximum number of concurrent project assignments per consultant, ensure that the recommended weekly hours would not push the consultant's projected utilization above the overutilized threshold when accounting for all proposed reallocations for that consultant, and do not exceed the per-entry weekly hour cap. Projected utilization after reallocation is computed using the formula described in the reallocation policy file. For each proposed reallocation, calculate the projected weekly margin impact as the recommended weekly hours multiplied by the difference between the applicable billing rate and the consultant's internal hourly cost rate. Save this proposal as `reallocation_proposal.csv` with columns in this order: `consultant_id`, `consultant_name`, `target_project_id`, `target_project_name`, `recommended_weekly_hours`, `matching_skills`, `projected_weekly_margin_impact`. List matching skill names in the matching_skills field separated by semicolons, sorted alphabetically. Round recommended_weekly_hours to one decimal place and projected_weekly_margin_impact to two decimal places. Sort the file by consultant_id ascending, breaking ties by target_project_id ascending.

Once all deliverables are written, state in your final response the total forecasted weekly margin improvement, which is the sum of every projected_weekly_margin_impact entry in the reallocation proposal. Present it in the exact format `Total Forecasted Weekly Margin Improvement: $X.XX` with the dollar amount rounded to two decimal places.
