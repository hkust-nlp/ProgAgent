The airline's customer service team needs advance warning about travelers whose upcoming itineraries are fragile, those facing tight connections that shrink further when flights run late, those booked on carriers with spotty track records, or those whose flights are already showing delays or cancellations in the real-time status feed. Your job is to comb through every confirmed booking, score each itinerary for disruption risk, and deliver a ready-to-act rebooking list before things go sideways.

The files you have access to lay out everything you need. One specifies the minimum connection time required at each airport in minutes. Another defines the scoring methodology with component weights for connection tightness, carrier reliability, and current delay, along with key thresholds such as the buffer zone above the minimum connection time, the on-time performance cutoff below which a carrier is considered unreliable, and the delay level that maxes out the delay component. That same configuration carries historical on-time performance ratings per carrier and vulnerability multipliers for passengers flagged as needing special assistance or traveling as unaccompanied minors. A separate policy document spells out how to assign rebooking priority tiers based on score ranges and loyalty status, and how to break ties within a tier.

Effective layover at a connection equals the scheduled gap between the arriving and departing flights, reduced by any delay on the incoming leg and extended by any delay on the outgoing leg. When that effective layover drops below the airport's minimum, the connection tightness component takes its full weight; when it sits between the minimum and the minimum plus the buffer, the score scales linearly down to zero; beyond that, it contributes nothing. A cancelled flight automatically maxes out both the delay component and, if part of a connection, the tightness component. Carrier risk applies only to carriers below the reliability threshold and is computed as the carrier reliability weight multiplied by one minus the carrier's on-time performance rate; take the worst carrier across all segments. The delay component equals the current delay weight multiplied by the ratio of the worst single-segment delay to the severe-delay ceiling, capped at one. The raw total of these three components is then multiplied by the traveler's vulnerability factor, which you will find in the passenger information files available to you; look for entries indicating special assistance or unaccompanied-minor status and apply the corresponding multiplier from the configuration, defaulting to the base multiplier when neither applies.

Every itinerary whose adjusted score meets or exceeds the high-risk threshold belongs on the output list. For each, pinpoint the segment most at fault by checking, in order, whether any segment is cancelled, whether a connection is tight in which case the departing flight of the tightest connection is the culprit, whether a segment is severely delayed, or whether a segment flies an unreliable carrier. Then search for an alternative on the same route as that segment, choosing the earliest departure strictly after the original scheduled departure time that still has open seats. If nothing qualifies, mark the alternative as NONE.

Retrieve each flagged traveler's name and loyalty tier, then assign a rebooking priority following the rules in the policy document. Save the results as `rebooking_list.csv` with columns `pnr`, `traveler_name`, `loyalty_tier`, `risk_score`, `risk_factors`, `at_risk_segment`, `recommended_alternative`, and `rebooking_priority`. Round risk scores to one decimal place and express risk factors as a semicolon-separated list using the labels tight_connection, carrier_unreliable, flight_delayed, and flight_cancelled in that fixed order when multiple apply. Order rows by ascending priority, then descending score, then alphabetically by traveler name. In your final answer, report the total number of itineraries you analyzed, how many landed on the rebooking list, and which PNR carries the highest risk score together with that score value.
