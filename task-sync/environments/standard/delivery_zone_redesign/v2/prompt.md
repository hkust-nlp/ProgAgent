You are working with a food delivery operations team that needs to evaluate and restructure its delivery zone layout. The current system divides the city into delivery zones, each with an assigned rider quota and geographic boundary defined by a center point and a radius. Over time, some zones have become inefficient due to shifts in customer density, restaurant preparation bottlenecks, or suboptimal rider allocation. Your job is to analyze every zone, compute a composite efficiency score, classify each zone, and produce a formal re-zoning proposal.

Begin by retrieving the list of all delivery zones and then, for each zone, gather its operational metrics: the total number of orders and average delivery time, the count of active riders, the aggregate transit time statistics for rider movements between pickups, and the aggregate restaurant preparation duration statistics. All of these are available through the provided tools. You will also need to read the zone boundary definitions from `zone_boundaries.json`, which contains each zone's center latitude, center longitude, radius in kilometers, and current rider quota. Separately, you must consult the re-zoning policy file `rezoning_policy.yaml`, which specifies the efficiency scoring weights, penalty rates, baseline thresholds, and adjustment rules that govern the entire analysis.

Customer address density data is spread across multiple sector density files, each named with the pattern `sector_density_NNN.csv`. Each file contains rows of sub-sectors with columns `lat_min`, `lat_max`, `lng_min`, `lng_max`, `residential_addresses`, `commercial_addresses`, and `density_per_sq_km`. To determine a zone's density, identify every sub-sector whose geographic center falls within the zone's radius of the zone's center, using the haversine formula for distance. Then compute a weighted average density, where each overlapping sub-sector's `density_per_sq_km` is weighted by its total address count (residential plus commercial). The city-wide average density is the simple arithmetic mean of all zone densities.

The efficiency score for each zone is a weighted sum of four component scores, with weights taken from the policy file under `efficiency_weights`. The transit time component score equals one hundred minus the product of the excess average transit time above the `transit_baseline_min` and the `transit_penalty_per_min`, floored at zero. The prep time component score follows the same pattern using `prep_baseline_min` and `prep_penalty_per_min`. The order-to-rider ratio component score equals one hundred minus the product of the absolute difference between the zone's orders-per-active-rider and the `target_orders_per_rider` and the `ratio_penalty_per_unit`, floored at zero. The density utilization component score equals the zone's density divided by the city-wide average density, multiplied by one hundred, capped at one hundred. The final efficiency score is the dot product of these four component scores with their respective weights, rounded to two decimal places.

A zone whose efficiency score falls below the `efficiency_cutoff` specified in the policy file is classified as INEFFICIENT; all others are EFFICIENT. For each inefficient zone, compute a new rider quota using the formula: take the zone's total order count, multiply by the zone's density factor (zone density divided by city average density), divide by `target_orders_per_rider`, and round up to the nearest integer, with a minimum of one. Also compute a new radius by multiplying the zone's current radius by the `radius_shrink_factor` from the adjustment rules, clamping the result between `min_radius_km` and `max_radius_km`, and rounding to two decimal places. For efficient zones, the new rider quota and new radius remain equal to the current values.

Once all zones have been evaluated, save the results as `rezoning_proposal.csv` sorted by zone ID in ascending order. The CSV must contain exactly these columns in this order: `zone_id`, `zone_name`, `efficiency_score`, `status`, `current_rider_quota`, `new_rider_quota`, `current_radius_km`, `new_radius_km`. The `status` column must contain exactly the string INEFFICIENT or EFFICIENT in uppercase.

In your final response, state the total number of zones analyzed, the number of inefficient zones identified along with their names and zone IDs, and the new total rider quota summed across all zones after applying the proposed changes.
