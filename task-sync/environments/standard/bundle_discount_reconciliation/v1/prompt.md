You are a billing reconciliation analyst working for a telecommunications company that offers bundled service packages combining internet, television, voice, and third-party content subscriptions. The company has recently received an uptick in customer complaints about billing errors, and an internal audit has flagged systematic discrepancies between what customers should be paying according to their subscription terms and what they were actually charged. Your job is to identify every billing discrepancy, determine its root cause, and produce a structured adjustment report.

The company maintains several data sources that you will need to cross-reference. Customer subscription details, including each customer's plan tier and bundle type, are accessible through the system's lookup tools. Network usage records, which show how much data each customer consumed per billing period broken down by service type, are also available through the system. Discount eligibility logs, tracking which promotional offers each customer qualifies for along with the eligibility windows and current status, can be retrieved per customer. Third-party content provider invoices, showing what each provider actually charged for a given customer in a given period, are likewise queryable.

Separately, the actual billing statements that were sent to customers are stored as data files across multiple batches in various formats. These files contain the line-item charges each customer saw on their bill: the base charge, any bundle discount applied, any promotional discount applied, the usage overage charge, the content service charge, and the total billed amount. You will need to read all of these batch files to reconstruct what every customer was actually charged.

In addition to the billing data, there are several reference documents you must consult. A pricing tiers configuration specifies the monthly rate, included data allowance in gigabytes, and the per-gigabyte overage rate for each plan tier. A bundle discount rules document defines the percentage discount on the base charge for each bundle type. A promotional offer catalog describes each promo code's discount percentage, what it applies to (either the base charge specifically or the total bill), and its duration. A root cause definitions file lists and describes the five recognized discrepancy categories.

To identify a discrepancy, you must compute what each customer should have been billed for every billing period and compare that against what they were actually charged. The expected base charge comes from the customer's plan tier as defined in the pricing configuration. The expected bundle discount is the base charge multiplied by the bundle discount percentage corresponding to the customer's bundle type. For promotional discounts, you must check whether the customer has any active promotional offer whose eligibility window covers the billing period in question; if a promotion applies to the base charge, the discount is the base charge times the promotion's percentage, and if it applies to the total, it is the subtotal (after base charge minus bundle and promo discounts plus usage and content charges) times the percentage. When multiple promotions are active, take the one yielding the largest discount. The expected usage charge equals the overage (total usage minus the tier's included allowance, floored at zero) multiplied by the tier's overage rate. The expected content charge is the sum of all provider invoice amounts for that customer and period.

Once you have the expected total and the actual billed total for each customer-period pair, any pair where the two differ by at least one cent constitutes a discrepancy. For each discrepancy you must determine the root cause by examining which billing component diverged. If the base charge on the bill does not match the tier's monthly rate, the root cause is PLAN_RATE_ERROR. If the bundle discount differs from what the bundle rules prescribe, it is MISSING_BUNDLE_DISCOUNT. If the promotional discount on the bill does not match the expected promotional discount (including cases where a discount was applied but no active promo exists), it is EXPIRED_PROMO. If the usage charge was inflated beyond the correct overage calculation, it is USAGE_OVERCHARGE. If the content charge does not match the sum of provider invoices, it is PROVIDER_MISMATCH. Evaluate these conditions in the order listed and assign the first matching root cause.

After identifying all discrepancies, save a file called `adjustment_report.csv` containing one row per discrepancy, sorted by customer_id and then by billing_period in ascending order. The CSV must include exactly these columns in this order: `customer_id`, `billing_period`, `root_cause`, `expected_amount`, `billed_amount`, `adjustment_amount`. The expected_amount and billed_amount columns should contain the full period totals rounded to two decimal places. The adjustment_amount is the expected total minus the billed total, also rounded to two decimal places, so a negative value means the customer was overcharged and a positive value means they were undercharged.

In your final response, state the total number of discrepancies found, the net adjustment amount across all discrepancies, and a breakdown showing how many discrepancies fall under each root cause category.
