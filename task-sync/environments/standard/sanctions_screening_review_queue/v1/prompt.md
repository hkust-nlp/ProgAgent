You have been assigned to perform a full sanctions screening cycle on all customer registrations that are currently pending review. The registrations span both individuals and corporate entities, and each must be assessed against the OFAC, EU, and UN consolidated sanctions lists before any account can be activated. Your screening must be thorough, covering identity checks, cross-border payment intermediary analysis, and ultimate-beneficial-ownership evaluation, all guided by the compliance policy and configuration parameters available to you.

Begin by reviewing the compliance policy document and the screening configuration file. The policy document lays out the complete screening methodology in seven sections, covering name screening, intermediary review, UBO analysis, risk scoring, and priority classification. The configuration file supplies all numeric thresholds and scoring weights referenced by the policy. A country risk matrix is also available for determining risk tiers associated with each country. All decision parameters, including the name-matching similarity threshold, the minimum UBO ownership percentage for screening, the country risk tier that triggers a flag, the point weights for each flag type, and the priority breakpoints, are specified in these files and must be applied exactly as stated.

Once you have absorbed the rules, retrieve the full list of pending customers through paginated lookups and begin screening each one. For every customer, search the customer's registered name against all three sanctions lists and note any match whose similarity score meets or exceeds the configured threshold. Separately, customer registration batch files contain additional identity attributes for each customer, including any known aliases. Every alias must also be searched individually against the sanctions lists, and a hit on any alias constitutes a distinct flag type from a hit on the primary name.

For the intermediary leg of the review, pull each customer's payment intermediary records and perform two checks per intermediary. First, search the intermediary's name against the sanctions lists with the same similarity threshold. Second, determine the intermediary's operating country and compare its risk tier against the configured tier threshold, flagging the customer accordingly if the tier is met or exceeded.

On the beneficial-ownership side, retrieve each customer's UBO structure and screen every beneficial owner whose ownership stake meets or exceeds the ownership threshold from the configuration. Search each qualifying owner's name against the sanctions lists. In addition, UBO declaration files record whether each beneficial owner holds Politically Exposed Person status and, if so, the associated country. If a PEP's country carries a risk tier at or above the configured threshold, raise the corresponding flag for the customer.

After all checks are complete for a customer, compute the risk score by summing the weight of every distinct flag type triggered, applying each weight at most once per customer regardless of how many individual hits produced that flag. Classify the customer's review priority according to the configured score breakpoints into one of five levels from CRITICAL down to CLEAR. A customer with no flags at all receives a score of zero and a CLEAR priority.

Produce a file called `screening_results.csv` containing one row per customer. The file must include columns named exactly `customer_id`, `customer_name`, `risk_score`, `flagged`, `flag_reasons`, `matched_sanctions_ids`, and `review_priority`. The `flagged` column should read `True` or `False`. Flag reason codes must be listed in alphabetical order separated by semicolons with no spaces around the semicolons. Matched sanctions entry identifiers must likewise be listed in ascending order separated by semicolons with no spaces. Sort all rows by risk score descending, breaking ties by customer identifier ascending.

In your final response, state the total number of customers screened, the number flagged, the number clear, and a breakdown of how many fall into each priority level. List every customer assigned CRITICAL priority by identifier, name, and score.
