You are tasked with conducting a comprehensive review of benefit overpayments across all active recipients in a social benefits system. The goal is to identify individuals whose actual financial circumstances appear materially different from what they have self-reported, and to produce a prioritized investigation list documenting each suspected case along with an estimated discrepancy amount.

Three primary data sources inform this analysis. Employment status updates originating from external payroll data hubs are available through the query tools at your disposal; these records capture changes in a recipient's employment, including the employer name, their status such as employed or terminated, a reported monthly salary figure, and the date the status became effective. Periodic self-report declarations, wherein recipients state their monthly income and current employment situation, are organized into a series of numbered batch CSV files accessible in your directory. Bank transaction alert records, also reachable through the query tools, log deposits that have triggered automated monitoring, showing the deposit amount, the date, the deposit source type, and the account type.

Several configuration and reference files in your directory govern the investigation parameters. A YAML configuration file specifies the review period as a start and end month, a percentage threshold used to determine when deposits significantly exceed declared income, a monetary threshold for flagging deposit totals, a set of priority weights associated with each category of discrepancy flag, and severity level boundaries keyed to priority score ranges. A JSON file enumerates, for each benefit program type, the maximum allowable monthly income and whether the program mandates that the recipient be unemployed. A policy document describes in detail the methodology for calculating potential overpayment amounts under each flag type and how to select the final figure when multiple flags apply to a single case.

Your analysis should proceed over every recipient who has an active account status. For each such recipient, gather their employment update history, their bank transaction alerts, their benefit payment records falling within the review period, and their self-declared income reports from the batch files. Determine the individual's most recent employment status by selecting the update with the latest effective date that falls on or before the end of the review period. Compute total bank deposits during the review window and derive an average monthly deposit figure by dividing the total by the number of months in the review period. The detected monthly income is the greater of two values: the reported salary from the most recent employment update where the status indicates active employment, meaning employed or part time, and the average monthly deposits just calculated. If the most recent employment update does not indicate active employment, treat the salary component as zero for this computation.

Three types of discrepancy flags may apply. The first, denoted EMPLOYMENT_MISMATCH, applies when the benefit program requires the recipient to be unemployed, the most recent payroll employment update indicates active employment, and the recipient's most recent self-report within the review period declares their status as unemployed or not_working. The second, INCOME_OVER_LIMIT, applies when the detected monthly income exceeds the maximum monthly income specified for the recipient's benefit type in the JSON configuration. The third, UNDECLARED_DEPOSITS, applies when total deposits in the review period surpass the configured deposit monetary threshold and simultaneously exceed the sum of the recipient's declared monthly income values across all their self-reports in the review period, multiplied by one plus the configured percentage threshold divided by one hundred. Only recipients for whom at least one flag applies should appear in the output.

For each flagged recipient, compute a priority score by summing the weight values associated with each of their applicable flags as defined in the YAML configuration. Assign a severity label by identifying the highest severity level whose boundary the priority score meets or exceeds. Compute the discrepancy amount following the policy document's methodology, evaluating the appropriate formula for each applicable flag and selecting the largest resulting value as the final discrepancy amount, rounded to exactly two decimal places.

Save the complete investigation findings as `investigation_report.csv` containing the columns `recipient_id`, `recipient_name`, `benefit_type`, `monthly_benefit`, `total_benefits_in_period`, `discrepancy_amount`, `priority_score`, `severity`, and `flags`, in that exact column order. The flags column for each row should list all applicable flag codes separated by the pipe character with no surrounding spaces. Sort the rows by priority_score in descending order, breaking ties by discrepancy_amount in descending order. All monetary columns must display exactly two decimal places.

In your final response, state the total number of recipients flagged for investigation and identify the top three cases by priority score, providing for each the recipient ID, recipient name, and discrepancy amount formatted to two decimal places.
