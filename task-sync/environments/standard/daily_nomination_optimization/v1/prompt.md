You are a gas pipeline dispatch optimizer responsible for producing the daily nomination schedule for a natural gas transmission network. Your objective is to analyze every shipper's nominated volumes across all pipeline segments, cross-reference those volumes with real-time sensor data and compressor station logs, identify where the pipeline is constrained, apply curtailment rules in the correct priority order, and compute fuel costs for the day.

Begin by retrieving the full list of shippers registered on the system and, for each one, pull its nomination records to learn which segments it ships on and the daily volume in MMBtu it has nominated on each segment. Separately, retrieve the complete inventory of pipeline segments along with the hourly sensor readings for every segment; these readings include pressure in PSI, flow rate, and temperature for each of the twenty-four hours of the operating day. You will also need the full customer roster, noting which segment each customer is connected to, and every compressor station together with its twenty-four hourly log entries showing fuel consumed and throughput.

The configuration files available to you contain the parameters you need to carry out the analysis. Segment specification files provide each segment's maximum capacity in MMBtu per hour along with its minimum and maximum allowable pressure bounds. Shipper contract files give each shipper's priority tier (an integer from 1 to 5), its penalty rate, and crucially its twenty-four-element hourly profile, a set of fractional weights that sum to one and describe how the shipper's daily volume is distributed across the hours of the day. Customer profile files list each customer's minimum delivery obligation in MMBtu per hour, which represents the floor of gas that must continue to flow on the customer's connected segment even during curtailment. An operational rules file spells out the exact factors and procedures: when a sensor reading shows pressure above a segment's maximum, the segment's effective capacity for that hour drops to 85 percent of the stated maximum capacity; when pressure falls below the minimum, the effective capacity drops to 90 percent; otherwise, the full maximum capacity applies. A fuel price schedule provides the cost per MMBtu of compressor fuel for each hour of the day.

To compute each shipper's hourly volume on a given segment, multiply the shipper's daily nominated volume on that segment by the corresponding element of the shipper's hourly profile. Sum all shipper hourly volumes on a segment to get the total nominated flow for that segment-hour. Compare this total against the effective capacity you derived from the pressure check. Any segment-hour where total nominated flow exceeds effective capacity is constrained and requires curtailment.

When curtailment is necessary, first calculate the total minimum delivery obligation for all customers connected to that segment. The maximum amount you may curtail is the total nominated flow minus that minimum delivery sum; if the needed curtailment exceeds this maximum, cap it there. To allocate curtailment among shippers, sort them by priority tier in descending order so that tier-5 shippers are curtailed first and tier-1 shippers last; break ties by shipper identifier in ascending alphabetical order. Walk through the sorted list and reduce each shipper's hourly volume by up to its full amount until the remaining curtailment need reaches zero. The adjusted flow on the segment for that hour is the total nominated flow minus the actual curtailment applied.

For the fuel cost analysis, take each compressor station's hourly fuel consumption from the logs and multiply it by the fuel price for that hour from the price schedule. Sum across all stations and all hours to get the total daily fuel cost.

Save your first deliverable as `dispatch_instructions.csv` with columns segment_id, hour, total_nominated, effective_capacity, adjusted_flow, is_constrained, and curtailment_volume. This file should contain one row for every constrained segment-hour you identified. Save your second deliverable as `curtailment_allocations.csv` with columns segment_id, hour, shipper_id, priority_tier, original_nomination, curtailed_amount, and adjusted_nomination, containing one row per shipper for every constrained segment-hour showing how curtailment was distributed. Save your third deliverable as `fuel_cost_summary.csv` with columns station_id, segment_id, hour, fuel_consumed_mmbtu, fuel_price_usd, and fuel_cost_usd, containing one row per station per hour.

In your final response, include the following three summary statistics on separate lines exactly as formatted here: "Total constrained segment-hours: N" where N is the count of constrained segment-hour pairs, "Total curtailment volume: X.XX MMBtu" where X.XX is the sum of all curtailment volumes rounded to two decimal places, and "Total fuel cost: $X.XX" where X.XX is the total fuel cost rounded to two decimal places.
