You are responsible for preparing the coming week's feed ration schedule for a livestock feeding operation that manages cattle across several barns. Each barn contains multiple pens, and every pen holds a group of animals of a single breed at a particular stage of growth. Your objective is to determine, for every pen, the daily quantity of each feed commodity that should be delivered so that nutritional targets are met, operational constraints are respected, and feed costs are kept as low as practically possible.

Begin by identifying all pens in the operation and gathering each pen's details, including the breed, the number of head, the current average live weight, and the animals' age in weeks. For every pen, look up the breed-specific growth target that matches its current age to obtain the expected dry-matter intake as a percentage of body weight, the minimum crude protein requirement, and the minimum metabolizable energy requirement. The base daily intake for each animal in a pen equals the average weight multiplied by that percentage divided by one hundred, and the pen's total daily intake is that figure multiplied by the number of animals.

Recent health and condition assessments have been recorded for each pen in batch files stored alongside your other reference materials. Each record carries a health flag that determines whether the pen's intake should be scaled by a multiplier. The applicable multiplier values for each flag are documented in the formulation rules configuration file you will find among the reference materials in your directory, together with the rules governing feeding phases, category allocation percentages, commodity selection logic, and waste-based reduction parameters.

Consumption logs from the automated feeders are available through the system. Retrieve the recent history for each pen over the lookback window specified in the configuration and compute the average feed waste percentage across all log entries in that window. If the average waste exceeds the configured threshold, reduce the pen's total daily intake by the excess multiplied by the configured reduction factor divided by one hundred, applied as a multiplicative adjustment to the intake already modified by the health multiplier.

The ration for each pen must be broken down by feed-commodity category, with the fraction of total intake assigned to each category following the phase-specific allocation table in the rules. Two feeding phases exist, distinguished by an age-week boundary defined in the same configuration. Within any category that contains more than one commodity, choose the commodity with the lowest most-recent market price; in the event of a tie, choose the name that comes first alphabetically. For the supplement category, the split between individual supplement commodities is prescribed in the configuration rather than determined by price.

After the initial allocation, verify that the blended crude protein and metabolizable energy of the resulting ration meet the pen's minimum requirements. A reference document among your files describes the precise procedure for iteratively adjusting category shares to satisfy nutritional floors: protein adjustments are applied first, then energy adjustments.

Query the current total silo stock for each commodity and confirm that the total weekly demand across all pens does not exceed the available stock after reserving the safety margin defined in the cost policy file.

Save the final schedule as `weekly_ration_schedule.csv` with the columns `pen_id`, `commodity`, `daily_quantity_kg`, `weekly_total_kg`, and `daily_cost_usd`, in that exact order. Every numeric field must carry exactly two decimal places. Sort the rows first by pen identifier in ascending order and then by commodity name in ascending alphabetical order. The daily quantity represents the total amount for the entire pen across all its animals, the weekly total is the daily quantity multiplied by seven, and the daily cost is the daily quantity multiplied by the commodity's latest market price per ton divided by one thousand.

Separately, save a file called `adjustment_report.json` containing three keys. The first, `total_weekly_cost`, is the sum of every row's daily cost multiplied by seven, rounded to two decimal places. The second, `num_pens_adjusted`, counts how many pens had either a non-standard health flag or an average waste percentage exceeding the threshold. The third, `adjustments`, is a list of objects each carrying `pen_id`, `adjustment_type` (which must be exactly one of `health`, `waste`, or `both`), and a `description` string summarizing what was adjusted for that pen.

Once all deliverables are written, state in your final response the total weekly cost in the format `Total Weekly Cost: $X.XX` and the number of pens that received adjustments in the format `Number of Adjusted Pens: X`.
