You are tasked with generating personalized weekly training schedules for a roster of collegiate athletes for the upcoming week starting Monday, March 10, 2025 and ending Sunday, March 16, 2025. Each athlete carries a unique combination of physical readiness, academic obligations, and injury risk that must be carefully balanced when assigning training sessions.

Begin by retrieving every athlete from the system. For each one, pull their GPS session data spanning the four weeks immediately before the target week (February 10 through March 9, 2025 inclusive). From that data, compute the acute-to-chronic workload ratio, known as the ACWR, by dividing the total player load accumulated during the most recent seven days of that window by the average weekly player load across the full four-week span (that is, divide the total four-week load by four to obtain the weekly average, then divide the seven-day acute load by that average). Classify the ACWR risk for each athlete using the thresholds stored in the risk thresholds configuration file: an ACWR that falls within the low-risk band is considered safe, one that falls in either moderate band is flagged moderate, and anything outside those ranges or an incalculable ratio is marked high.

Separately, retrieve each athlete's most recent wellness survey and compute a composite score by summing the fatigue, soreness, and stress values. If the composite reaches or exceeds the composite risk threshold in the configuration, label the wellness risk high. Even if the composite stays below that threshold, flag the wellness risk as moderate whenever fatigue alone meets or exceeds its individual threshold, or soreness meets or exceeds its threshold, or sleep quality falls at or below the sleep quality threshold. Otherwise the wellness risk is low.

Retrieve each athlete's musculoskeletal screening records as well. Any screening where the body region appears in the critical-regions list and either the pain level meets or exceeds the high-risk pain threshold or the asymmetry percentage meets or exceeds the asymmetry threshold should drive the MSK risk to high. Short of that, if any screening regardless of region shows a pain level of four or above or an asymmetry of ten percent or above, mark the MSK risk moderate. If no screening triggers either condition, the MSK risk is low.

In addition to tool-retrieved data, check whether an athlete report file exists for each individual. Where one does, read the seven-day average RPE value and compare it to the RPE thresholds in the configuration: at or above the high threshold marks RPE risk high, at or above the moderate threshold marks it moderate, otherwise low. Athletes without a report file should be treated as having low RPE risk by default.

The overall risk level for each athlete is the worst case across ACWR, wellness, MSK, and RPE risks. Use the load management policy file to look up the constraints that correspond to each overall risk level, including the maximum number of sessions per week, the maximum allowed intensity tier, the minimum number of mandatory rest days, and any session types that must be excluded.

Once risk levels and constraints are established, retrieve each athlete's full academic timetable. The scheduling rules file defines four daily training windows. A window is considered blocked for a given athlete on a given day if any of their mandatory classes overlaps with it, meaning the class start time falls before the window end and the class end time falls after the window start. Weekends carry no academic commitments.

Build a seven-day training plan for each athlete, Monday through Sunday, by selecting training days that have at least one available window. Observe the following: never exceed the athlete's maximum allowed sessions per week, ensure at least the required number of rest days with zero sessions, do not schedule more than three consecutive training days, and never follow a high-intensity day with another high-intensity day on the very next calendar day. Choose session types from the session templates file, filtering out any types excluded for the athlete's risk tier and capping intensity at the tier's maximum. Assign the earliest available window that is not blocked, preferring windows listed in the athlete's report file as preferred whenever such a file exists. When no report file exists, fall back to the first unblocked window.

Save the completed schedule as `weekly_training_plan.csv` with the following columns in this exact order: `athlete_id`, `athlete_name`, `day`, `session_start`, `session_end`, `session_type`, `intensity`, `duration_min`, `focus_area`. Only include rows for actual training sessions; do not insert rows for rest days. Times must be in HH:MM format. Days must be spelled out in full (Monday, Tuesday, and so on).

Save a risk assessment summary as `athlete_risk_report.csv` with columns in this exact order: `athlete_id`, `athlete_name`, `acwr_value`, `acwr_risk`, `wellness_composite`, `wellness_risk`, `msk_risk`, `rpe_risk`, `overall_risk`, `max_sessions`, `max_intensity`, `mandatory_rest_days`. Round the ACWR value to two decimal places; if it was incalculable, enter N/A.

In your final response, state the total number of athletes processed. Then state the number of athletes in each risk category in the format: N high-risk, N moderate-risk, N low-risk. Finally, list by full name every athlete classified as high-risk, or explicitly state that zero high-risk athletes were identified if that is the case.
