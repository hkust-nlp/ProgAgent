The combined entity resulting from the recent acquisition requires a comprehensive customer lifetime value assessment to guide post-merger integration priorities. You have access to the full customer base from both the acquiring and target companies through the available data retrieval functions, which expose transactional records, product catalog details, and attrition risk data. Supplementary profitability analyses, loyalty indicators, and configuration parameters have been prepared by the deal team and are available as data files in your working directory.

Begin by retrieving the complete customer roster across both companies, along with each customer's historical purchase data and churn risk profile. From the transactional records, calculate each customer's average annual revenue by dividing their total historical purchase amount across all recorded years by three, reflecting the three-year lookback window. To determine the gross profit margin at the customer level, compute a weighted cost ratio: for each product a customer has purchased, weight that product's cost-to-price ratio by the customer's total spend on that product, then sum these weighted ratios and divide by the customer's total spend across all products. Gross profit equals the average annual revenue multiplied by one minus this weighted cost ratio.

Product-level synergy factors reflecting expected post-merger efficiency gains have been prepared in analysis files within your working directory. For each customer, compute a weighted average synergy factor across all products they have purchased, using purchase amounts as weights, and multiply the gross profit by this factor to obtain the synergy-adjusted profit.

Cross-selling represents a key value driver in the integration plan. Each customer has a cross-sell propensity score captured in the profile data files, and a conversion matrix specifies expected conversion rates organized by customer segment and product category. For each customer, determine the average conversion rate across all product categories for that customer's segment. The cross-sell value equals the customer's cross-sell propensity multiplied by this average conversion rate, further multiplied by the segment's value multiplier found in the tier rules configuration, and then by fifteen percent of the customer's average annual revenue.

Customers originating from the target company incur one-time integration costs that must be amortized. An integration parameters file specifies per-segment cost amounts and the amortization period. Only target company customers bear these costs; the annual integration cost per customer equals the relevant segment's integration cost divided by the amortization period. For acquiring company customers, this cost is zero.

Net annual value for each customer is the synergy-adjusted profit plus the cross-sell value minus the amortized integration cost. To project this forward over three years, apply the revenue growth rate specified in the configuration file, compounding annually. Each year's survival probability derives from the customer's effective churn rate: effective churn equals the annual churn probability multiplied by one minus the loyalty score divided by one hundred. Survival probability for year y equals one minus the effective churn rate raised to the power y. The present value contribution for year y is the grown net annual value multiplied by the survival probability, divided by one plus the discount rate raised to the power y. The total three-year customer lifetime value is the sum of these discounted contributions for years one, two, and three.
