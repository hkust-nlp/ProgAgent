You are evaluating potential locations for a new retail outlet across a large metropolitan area. Your objective is to identify every site that satisfies a set of eligibility requirements, score each qualifying site on multiple dimensions, compute a projected first-year footfall figure, and produce a ranked shortlist ordered by composite score.

Before doing anything else, locate and read the configuration documents that govern every calculation you will perform. One document specifies the eligibility thresholds that determine which zones and sites may proceed to scoring: minimum zone population and median household income, minimum site floor area, a ceiling on monthly rent per square foot, a maximum number of competitor stores allowed within a specified radius, and the radius itself in kilometres. A second document lays out the scoring methodology: five weighted components (demographic, traffic, competition, lease, and accessibility), together with all sub-weights, normalization scales, caps, and bonus rules for each component. A third document defines the footfall projection model, listing the base pedestrian capture rate, vehicle conversion rate, three competition-adjustment tiers with their thresholds, a seasonal factor, the weight applied to peak-hour boosts, a default peak boost, and the number of operating days per year. A fourth document maps each competitor retail category to a numeric threat level.

Begin by retrieving every zone in the study area and filtering for those whose population and median household income meet or exceed the stated thresholds. For each zone that passes, pull its full demographic profile so you have the population growth rate needed later for scoring. Then retrieve all candidate sites within each eligible zone and discard any whose floor area falls below the minimum. For each remaining site, obtain its lease listing and eliminate any whose monthly rent per square foot exceeds the ceiling. Next, for every site still in contention, query for competitors within the specified radius and exclude sites where the count exceeds the maximum. Finally, gather each surviving site's average weekday and weekend pedestrian and vehicle counts.

With the eligible set established, compute five sub-scores for every site. The demographic score blends three normalized terms: population divided by a population scale (capped at one) times one hundred, median household income divided by an income scale (also capped) times one hundred, and population growth rate (floored at zero, divided by a growth scale, capped) times one hundred, each multiplied by its respective sub-weight and summed. The traffic score is the minimum of one hundred and the quantity obtained by taking the weighted-average daily pedestrian count (weekday count times five plus weekend count times two, divided by seven) multiplied by the pedestrian weight, plus the analogous weighted-average daily vehicle count multiplied by the vehicle weight, all divided by the traffic divisor. The competition score starts at a base value and subtracts the product of each nearby competitor's category threat level and a per-threat-point penalty, floored at zero; then it is multiplied by one minus the zone's market saturation index divided by the saturation divisor. The market saturation index for each zone can be found in the market analysis reports filed for each region; where no report covers a zone, use a default index of fifty. The lease score equals the maximum of zero and one minus the rent divided by a reference maximum rent, multiplied by one hundred, plus a term bonus added whenever the lease duration meets or exceeds a threshold, with the total capped at the stated ceiling. The accessibility score is the site's accessibility rating, found in the field inspection reports, multiplied by the rating multiplier; where no inspection report covers a site, use the default rating specified in the scoring configuration.

The composite score is the weighted sum of these five sub-scores, rounded to exactly two decimal places. Rank all eligible sites in descending order of composite score; break ties by projected annual footfall descending, then by site identifier ascending.

To compute projected annual footfall, determine a competition adjustment tier: use the high-competition adjustment when the adjusted competition score is below the low threshold, the medium adjustment when it falls between the two thresholds, and the low-competition (most favorable) adjustment when it meets or exceeds the high threshold. Multiply the weighted-average daily pedestrian count by the base capture rate and by the competition adjustment, add the weighted-average daily vehicle count times the vehicle conversion rate, then multiply the sum by operating days, by the seasonal factor, and by one plus the site's peak-hour multiplier times the peak-boost weight. The peak-hour multiplier for each site appears in the traffic-sector reports; sites not listed receive the default peak boost. Round the result to the nearest integer.

Save the complete ranked shortlist as `site_rankings.csv` with these columns in this order: `rank`, `site_id`, `site_name`, `zone_id`, `zone_name`, `composite_score`, `projected_annual_footfall`. Format composite scores with exactly two decimal places and footfall as a whole number. In your final response, state how many sites qualified and list the top five by rank, including each site's identifier, name, zone identifier and name, composite score, and projected annual footfall.
