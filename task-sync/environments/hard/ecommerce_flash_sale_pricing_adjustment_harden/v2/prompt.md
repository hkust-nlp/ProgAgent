You are responsible for developing a pricing schedule for an upcoming flash sale campaign. The campaign targets a defined set of product categories, and your objective is to determine the optimal discount percentage for every eligible product using a multi-dimensional scoring approach that synthesizes clickstream engagement, competitive pricing intelligence, warehouse inventory pressure, and customer segment lifetime value.

The campaign configuration and policy documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification for this work. They define which product categories are eligible, how each of the four scoring dimensions is computed from the browsing logs and the pricing and inventory records, how raw values are normalized and combined into the composite score, how composite scores map to discount tiers, the absolute discount cap, and the margin floor that no sale price may violate, including the circumstances under which a product must be excluded from the schedule entirely. Follow those documents exactly.

Every product that appears in your final schedule must also have its final discount percentage recorded against the campaign in the pricing system, so that the database reflects the approved adjustments.

Save the completed pricing schedule as `pricing_schedule.csv`. The file must contain the following columns in exactly this order: `product_id`, `product_name`, `category`, `base_price`, `cost_price`, `composite_score`, `discount_tier`, `discount_pct`, `sale_price`. Round composite_score to two decimal places. Express base_price, cost_price, and sale_price to two decimal places. Express discount_pct as a whole number with no decimal point. Sort the rows by composite_score in descending order, breaking ties by product_id in ascending lexicographic order.

In your final response, state the total number of products included in the pricing schedule, the count of products assigned to each discount tier, and the overall average discount percentage across all included products rounded to two decimal places, and confirm that the adjustments have been recorded in the pricing system.
