You have been brought in as a healthcare fraud analyst to investigate potential upcoding across a network of medical providers. Upcoding occurs when a provider systematically bills for more expensive or complex procedures than what was actually performed, and your job is to analyze all available evidence and produce a prioritized audit list ranking providers by their overall risk.

The audit methodology and configuration documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification: they define the risk components and their exact formulas, the scoring weights, the flagging thresholds and priority levels, each specialty's authorized procedure categories, the regional billing benchmarks, peer group assignments, historical adjustment records, and the typical patient age ranges. Records of the same kind may be spread across multiple files, and all of them must be consolidated. Provider, claim, procedure, and patient records are available through the network's data services. Nothing beyond the documents and that data should be assumed, and every calculation must follow them.

Save the results as `audit_list.csv` containing exactly these columns in order: provider_id, provider_name, specialty, composite_risk_score, frequency_deviation, out_of_scope_rate, denial_rate_deviation, adjustment_rate, demographic_anomaly_rate, priority. Include only flagged providers and sort rows in descending order by composite risk score, breaking ties by provider_id in ascending order.

In your final response, state how many providers were flagged for audit and list the top five by composite risk score, giving for each the provider identifier, name, specialty, composite risk score, and priority level.
