You are a property management analyst responsible for identifying residential units that may be involved in unauthorized subletting or occupancy violations. The property management company has been receiving scattered reports of potential lease violations across its portfolio, and a systematic, data-driven investigation is now required. Every unit in the portfolio must be evaluated; the evidence you need is spread between the records available in your working directory and the property management system itself.

The investigation policy and methodology documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification: they define each investigation signal, the exact condition under which it triggers, the thresholds, multipliers, caps, and point values involved, the score a unit must accumulate to warrant formal investigation, and the output formatting rules. Every determination in your report must follow those definitions exactly — nothing beyond them should be assumed.

Produce a file called `investigation_report.csv`. This file must contain exactly four columns: `unit_id`, `total_score`, `signals`, and `subletting_allowed`. The `signals` column must list the names of all triggered signals for that unit in alphabetical order, separated by a semicolon followed by a space. The `subletting_allowed` column must read `yes` when the lease permits subletting and `no` when it does not. Only units whose total score meets or exceeds the investigation threshold should appear in the report. Sort the rows by `total_score` in descending order; where two units share the same score, break the tie by sorting `unit_id` in ascending alphabetical order.

Conclude your response by stating which units require formal investigation, listing their unit IDs separated by commas in the same order they appear in the report.
