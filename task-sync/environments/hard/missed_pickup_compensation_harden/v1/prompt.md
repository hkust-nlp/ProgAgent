You are working as a data analyst for a municipal waste-collection authority that needs to resolve a backlog of resident complaints about missed trash pickups. The authority operates a fleet of trucks across several service zones, each zone assigned a dedicated truck and specific collection days of the week, and residents subscribe to one of several service tiers with tier-specific contractual terms. Your goal is to determine which pending missed-pickup complaints represent genuinely missed pickups that warrant a financial credit, classify each validated miss, compute the correct refund amount, record every approved credit, and produce a final deliverable file.

The policy and configuration documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification for this work: they define which complaints are eligible, how a claimed miss must be verified against the truck GPS activity and bin-lift sensor evidence, how each validated miss is classified, and exactly how the credit amount is calculated, including the treatment of repeat misses. Follow those documents precisely; complaints they deem ineligible or invalid receive no credit. Alongside the documents you have access to the authority's operational records — service requests, resident accounts, truck GPS stops, bin-lift sensor readings, and claim history — to gather whatever evidence the policy requires.

Every complaint you validate must also be posted to the authority's compensation ledger: record a credit memo against the resident's account, referencing the originating service request and carrying the miss classification and the final credit amount, so the database reflects each approved refund.

After processing every pending complaint, save the validated results to a file called `refund_list.csv`. This file must contain a header row followed by one row per validated refund, with columns in exactly this order: `request_id`, `resident_id`, `resident_name`, `zone_id`, `service_tier`, `scheduled_pickup_date`, `miss_type`, `base_credit`, `final_credit`. Use the exact string `full_miss` or `partial_miss` for the `miss_type` column. Sort all rows by `request_id` in ascending order. Format `base_credit` and `final_credit` as numbers with exactly two decimal places.

In your final response, state the total number of validated refund entries and the total credit amount as a sum of all `final_credit` values, formatted to two decimal places.
