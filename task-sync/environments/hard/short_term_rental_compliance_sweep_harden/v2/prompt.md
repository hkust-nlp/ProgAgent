You are a municipal compliance analyst tasked with identifying every property that is operating as an unpermitted short-term rental. The city maintains records of property tax assessments, rental platform listings, monthly utility consumption, and occupancy permits, all of which you can query. In addition, a collection of files has been provided to you containing complaint reports filed by residents, intelligence gathered from rental platforms, and several policy and configuration documents.

The enforcement methodology document and the policy and configuration documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification for this sweep: they define which properties are in scope, what constitutes each type of violation, how the risk score and each of its modifier bonuses are computed, how the recommended enforcement action is determined, and how estimated annual revenue is aggregated per property. Every figure in your report must follow those definitions exactly — nothing beyond them should be assumed.

Compile your findings into a CSV file named `compliance_cases.csv`. The file must contain the columns `property_id`, `address`, `owner_name`, `violation_type`, `risk_score`, `recommended_action`, and `evidence_summary`, in that exact order. Each row represents one violation property. The `evidence_summary` column should be a concise semicolon-separated string that includes the violation type, the number of complaints, whether a utility anomaly was detected, the number of active listings, and the estimated annual revenue. Sort the rows by risk score in descending order, breaking ties by property identifier in ascending order.

In your final response, state the total number of compliance violation cases you identified. Provide a breakdown showing how many cases fall under each violation type. Then list the top three highest-risk properties by their property identifiers, their risk scores, and their violation types.
