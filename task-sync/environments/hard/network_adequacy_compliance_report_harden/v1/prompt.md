You are conducting a network adequacy compliance review for a regional health plan that operates across several service areas in California. The central question is whether enrolled members in every service area have adequate geographic access to qualified providers for each covered medical specialty, measured against regulatory distance thresholds that vary by specialty and by the urban, suburban, or rural classification of the area.

The eligibility guidelines and configuration documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification: they define the conditions under which a provider may be counted as serving a member, how distance is measured and which limit applies to each specialty under each area classification, the certification reference date, how coverage is determined and expressed, and the minimum coverage percentage each specialty must meet. Member enrollment records and provider credential records are likewise found among those reference materials. Every determination in your report must follow those definitions exactly — nothing beyond them should be assumed.

Once all distances and eligibility determinations are complete, produce a file named `deficiency_report.csv` containing one row for each combination of service area and specialty. The columns, in this exact order, must be `service_area_id`, `service_area_name`, `area_type`, `specialty_id`, `specialty_name`, `distance_threshold_miles`, `total_members`, `members_with_access`, `members_without_access`, `coverage_pct`, `required_coverage_pct`, and `compliant`. Express `coverage_pct` and `required_coverage_pct` as numeric values rounded to exactly two decimal places. The `compliant` column takes the value `YES` when coverage meets or exceeds the requirement and `NO` otherwise. Sort the rows by `service_area_id` ascending, breaking ties by `specialty_id` ascending.

In your final response, enumerate every non-compliant service area and specialty pair, stating for each the service area name, the specialty name, the achieved coverage percentage, and the required coverage percentage, so that regulatory follow-up can be directed precisely where the network falls short.
