You are responsible for preparing the coming week's feed ration schedule for a livestock feeding operation that manages cattle across several barns. Each barn contains multiple pens, and every pen holds a group of animals of a single breed at a particular stage of growth. Your objective is to determine, for every pen, the daily quantity of each feed commodity that should be delivered so that nutritional targets are met, operational constraints are respected, and feed costs are kept as low as practically possible.

The policy, rules, and reference documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification and govern every aspect of the computation: how each pen's base intake is derived from its growth target, how health flags and feed-waste history adjust that intake, how the adjusted intake is allocated across feed-commodity categories by feeding phase, how commodities are selected and priced, how nutritional floors are enforced, and how inventory feasibility is checked against the safety margin. Recent health and condition assessments for each pen are recorded in batch files stored with those documents, and consumption history from the automated feeders is available through the system. Follow the documented methodology exactly.

Save the final schedule as `weekly_ration_schedule.csv` with the columns `pen_id`, `commodity`, `daily_quantity_kg`, `weekly_total_kg`, and `daily_cost_usd`, in that exact order. Every numeric field must carry exactly two decimal places. Sort the rows first by pen identifier in ascending order and then by commodity name in ascending alphabetical order. The daily quantity represents the total amount for the entire pen across all its animals, the weekly total is the daily quantity multiplied by seven, and the daily cost is the daily quantity multiplied by the commodity's latest market price per ton divided by one thousand.

Separately, save a file called `adjustment_report.json` containing three keys. The first, `total_weekly_cost`, is the sum of every row's daily cost multiplied by seven, rounded to two decimal places. The second, `num_pens_adjusted`, counts how many pens had either a non-standard health flag or an average waste percentage exceeding the threshold. The third, `adjustments`, is a list of objects each carrying `pen_id`, `adjustment_type` (which must be exactly one of `health`, `waste`, or `both`), and a `description` string summarizing what was adjusted for that pen. For every pen that received an adjustment, also record that adjustment in the operation's adjustment ledger so the herd-management system reflects it, identifying the pen and its adjustment type.

Once all deliverables are written, state in your final response the total weekly cost in the format `Total Weekly Cost: $X.XX` and the number of pens that received adjustments in the format `Number of Adjusted Pens: X`.
