Your organization maintains a standard cost system for every finished good it manufactures, with each product's standard cost built from a material component defined by its bill of materials and a labor component defined by its production routing. Over recent periods actual costs have drifted away from these standards: raw material spot prices have shifted, the price actually paid depends on which approved vendor supplies each material and on material-specific handling surcharges, labor performance on the shop floor has varied from the routing standards, and production yield further affects the effective per-unit cost. You are to perform a full cost variance analysis for every finished good, comparing each product's standard material and labor costs against the corresponding actual costs and determining which products must be flagged and which warrant a formal cost revision.

The costing methodology document and the policy and configuration documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification: they define how standard and actual material and labor costs are computed, how vendor selection, quoted prices, and handling surcharges determine actual material prices, how production yield enters the calculation, how each variance and variance percentage is derived, and the thresholds and conditions that govern flagging and cost-revision recommendations. Every figure in your deliverable must follow those definitions exactly — nothing beyond them should be assumed.

Save your completed analysis as `variance_report.csv` with one row per product, sorted in descending order by the absolute value of total variance percentage. The file must contain exactly these columns in this order: `product_id`, `product_name`, `standard_material_cost`, `actual_material_cost`, `material_variance`, `material_variance_pct`, `standard_labor_cost`, `actual_labor_cost`, `labor_variance`, `labor_variance_pct`, `yield_rate`, `yield_adjusted_total`, `total_standard_cost`, `total_variance`, `total_variance_pct`, `flag`, `revision_recommended`. All monetary values must be rounded to exactly two decimal places. Percentage values must be rounded to two decimal places and represent the variance as a fraction of the corresponding standard cost multiplied by one hundred. The yield rate must be rounded to four decimal places. The `flag` and `revision_recommended` columns must contain either `YES` or `NO`.

In your final response, provide a concise summary listing every product that has been recommended for cost revision, stating for each one its product identifier, product name, total variance percentage, and the specific reason or reasons the revision was triggered.
