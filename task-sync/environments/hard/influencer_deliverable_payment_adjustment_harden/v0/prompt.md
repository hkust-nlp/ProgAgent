You are a compliance analyst at an influencer marketing agency responsible for reviewing every contract that is currently pending review. For each such contract you must evaluate whether the influencer's posts meet the agreed-upon deliverable requirements — post timing, engagement performance, audience geographic composition, and bot activity — as well as the contracted post count, flag every individual violation, and apply the resulting payment penalties.

The policy and configuration documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification for this review. They define every threshold, penalty percentage, formula, and boundary convention, the region-to-country mapping, how bot-detection scan records are selected, where the supporting audience data lives, and how the total penalty and adjusted payment are computed. Follow them exactly.

Produce a file named `flagged_posts.csv` containing columns `post_id`, `contract_id`, `violation_type`, and `detail`. Each row represents one violation on one post. The `violation_type` value must be exactly one of `date_violation`, `hour_violation`, `low_engagement`, `high_bot_score`, or `audience_location`. The `detail` column should carry a brief human-readable explanation. Sort rows by `contract_id` ascending, then `post_id` ascending, then `violation_type` alphabetically.

In addition, for every contract that is pending review, regardless of whether it received a penalty, record its payment adjustment through the payments system so that the adjustment ledger reflects the review outcome: the contract's total penalty percentage and its adjusted payment rounded to exactly two decimal places.

Once the file is written and every adjustment is recorded, provide a final summary with three lines in exactly this format: "Total violations: N" stating the total number of individual post-violation rows, "Contracts penalized: N" stating how many contracts received a penalty greater than zero, and "Total deductions: $X.XX" stating the sum of all base-payment-minus-adjusted-payment values across every reviewed contract, formatted as a dollar amount with two decimal places.
