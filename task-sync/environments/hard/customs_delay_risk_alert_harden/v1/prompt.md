You have been brought in to conduct a comprehensive customs delay risk assessment for all shipments that have not yet completed customs clearance. This includes any shipment currently in transit, sitting at port, or awaiting clearance processing. The goal is to flag those shipments most likely to encounter inspection holds or processing delays, so that customs brokers can be dispatched to intervene before problems escalate.

The risk methodology document and the policy and configuration files stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification for this assessment. They define the risk dimensions and how each raw score is computed, the weights, caps, and rounding conventions for the composite score, the alert threshold and the risk-level and broker-priority classifications, how the primary risk factor and its tie-breaks are determined, the recommended broker actions, and the dispatch policy. Every score and classification you produce must follow those definitions exactly; assume nothing beyond them.

For every shipment that qualifies as high risk, file a broker dispatch through the dispatch system, recording that shipment together with its broker priority.

Every risk score must be rounded to exactly two decimal places. Save the results as `high_risk_alerts.csv` with columns in this order: `shipment_id`, `carrier_name`, `supplier_name`, `destination_port`, `hs_code`, `risk_score`, `risk_level`, `primary_risk_factor`, `recommended_action`, `broker_priority`. Rows must be sorted by `risk_score` descending, with ties broken by `shipment_id` in ascending lexicographic order. In your final response, state the total number of high-risk shipments you identified and name the shipment with the highest composite risk score together with that score.
