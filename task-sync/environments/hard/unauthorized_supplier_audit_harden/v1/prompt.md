Your organization operates a nationwide franchise network and recent internal reviews have raised concerns that some franchisees may be procuring goods from suppliers who are either absent from the approved vendor lists or whose vendor approvals have lapsed. You have been tasked with conducting a comprehensive procurement compliance audit across the entire franchise system to identify and document every instance of non-compliant purchasing activity.

The audit methodology, policy, and configuration documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification for this audit. They define which franchisees fall within the audit scope, the four violation types and exactly how each one is determined, the complaint threshold, the base severity weights, the severity multipliers and the severity score formula, and the convention for matching purchase orders against central distribution shipment records. Apply those definitions exactly; assume nothing beyond them.

Once all violations have been identified, produce a file called `compliance_report.csv` containing one row for every individual violation instance. The CSV must contain exactly these columns in this order: `franchisee_id`, `franchisee_name`, `supplier_id`, `supplier_name`, `violation_type`, `invoice_number`, `order_date`, `total_amount`, `severity_score`, `evidence`. The severity score for any violation type other than QUALITY_FLAGGED equals the base severity weight for that type as specified in the compliance policy. All severity scores must be formatted to exactly two decimal places. The evidence column should contain a concise explanation of why the violation was flagged: for UNAPPROVED_SUPPLIER, mention the supplier name, its ID, and the region where it was not found; for EXPIRED_APPROVAL, include the supplier name, its ID, the approval expiry date, and the order date; for QUALITY_FLAGGED, state the supplier name, its ID, the complaint count, the threshold, and the highest severity level; for DC_BYPASS, note the invoice number for which no shipment record was found. Sort the entire report first by severity_score in descending order, then by franchisee_id in ascending order as the first tie-breaker, then by order_date in ascending order as the second tie-breaker.

In your final response, state the total number of violations found, break them down by each violation type with their respective counts, report how many distinct franchisees and how many distinct suppliers are involved in violations, and list the top three franchisees by total violation count along with their names and counts.
