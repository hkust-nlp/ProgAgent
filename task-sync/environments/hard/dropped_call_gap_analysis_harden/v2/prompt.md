You are a network optimization analyst at a telecommunications company that has been receiving a growing volume of dropped call complaints. Your objective is to systematically analyze these complaints alongside network performance data, maintenance records, third-party signal surveys, and device-level signal readings to identify which cell towers suffer from persistent coverage gaps, then produce a ranked list of the towers most urgently in need of upgrading.

The scoring methodology and configuration documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification for this analysis. They define which towers qualify for assessment in the first place, the scoring dimensions with their exact formulas and weights, how the final priority score is assembled and adjusted, the boundaries between urgency tiers, how each tower's primary issue is determined, and which upgrade should be recommended for each technology generation. Every figure in your output must follow those definitions exactly; the inputs you need live partly behind the query interface and partly in files in your working directory.

Save your results as `tower_upgrade_priority.csv` with columns `rank`, `tower_id`, `tower_name`, `region`, `priority_score`, `priority_tier`, `primary_issue`, and `recommended_upgrade`. Round the final priority score to exactly two decimal places. Sort the rows by `priority_score` in descending order, breaking ties by `tower_id` in ascending order. The `rank` column should be an integer starting at one reflecting this final ordering. The recommended upgrade for each tower comes from the technology upgrade paths configuration, based on the tower's current technology generation.

In your final response, state the total number of towers that appear on the upgrade list, report how many fall into each of the four priority tiers (critical, high, medium, and low), and identify the single tower with the highest priority score by giving both its tower_id and tower_name.
