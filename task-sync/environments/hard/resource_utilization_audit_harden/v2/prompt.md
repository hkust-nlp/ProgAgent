You are conducting an emergency services resource utilization audit for a metropolitan fire and rescue department. The department operates multiple unit types including engine companies, ladder companies, rescue squads, EMS units, and hazmat teams spread across numerous stations. Your objective is to identify every unit that requires retraining, re-equipping, or both, and to produce a prioritized list ranked by the severity of their deficiencies.

The audit methodology document and the policy and configuration documents stored alongside the data are the authoritative specification: they define the audit reference date, every threshold and penalty value, which deficiency types count toward retraining versus re-equipping, the certification level required for each role within each unit type, exactly how each deficiency is assessed and aggregated into scores, which units belong in the report, and how the required action label is assigned. The dispatch transcripts stored in the same location document radio communications during incidents and are the source for protocol violations, attributed according to the rule in the audit policy. Every number in your report must follow those definitions exactly — nothing beyond them should be assumed.

Save the final output as `priority_report.csv` containing the columns unit_id, unit_name, unit_type, retraining_score, reequip_score, total_priority_score, and action_required, in exactly that order. All score values must be integers. Sort the rows by total_priority_score in descending order; where two units share the same total, break the tie by unit_id in ascending lexicographic order.

In your final response, state the total number of units flagged for action and list the top five highest-priority units by their identifiers and names along with their total priority scores, retraining scores, re-equipping scores, and required actions.
